## Steps

Step 1: Resolve the metric on the two sides of $s=0$

At $s=0$, each added chord has exactly the same length as an existing geodesic: $d(a_1,a_3)=2$, $d(b_1,b_3)=2$, and $d(a_2,b_2)=4$ through $\ast$. Hence the metric is the wedge of two unit $4$-cycles at the common vertex $\ast$.

If $s<0$, all three chords are longer than those geodesics, so for $|s|<\frac14$ they are inactive and $d_s=d_0$ exactly.

If $s>0$, the only distances that change are
$$
d_s(a_1,a_3)=2-s,
\qquad
d_s(b_1,b_3)=2-2s,
\qquad
d_s(a_2,b_2)=4-s.
$$
Every other path using a new chord has, at $s=0$, excess at least $1$ over the old shortest path, while the total shortening is less than $1$ for $s<\frac14$.

Step 2: Find the two-dimensional equality space at $p=1$

Use reduced coordinates relative to $\ast$. For one $4$-cycle, order the non-base vertices as $(1,2,3)$ and set
$$
r=(1,2,1)^T,
\qquad
C=\begin{pmatrix}
0&1&2\\
1&0&1\\
2&1&0
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
The reduced $p=1$ matrix is
$$
R_4=C-\mathbf1r^T-r\mathbf1^T
=\begin{pmatrix}
-2&-2&0\\
-2&-4&-2\\
0&-2&-2
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Its eigenvalues are $0,-2,-6$, and its kernel is generated by
$$
k=(-1,1,-1)^T.
$$
For the wedge of the two cycles, cross distances satisfy $d_0(a_i,b_j)=r_i+r_j$, so the reduced cross block at $p=1$ is zero. Thus
$$
R_0=R_4\oplus R_4
$$
is negative semidefinite with two-dimensional kernel generated by
$$
e_A=(k,0),
\qquad
e_B=(0,k).
$$
Therefore $(Y,d_0)$ has $1$-negative type.

Step 3: Show that $\wp(0)=1$ and compute the $p$-derivative on the kernel

The full zero-sum vector corresponding to $e_A$ has coefficients $(1,-1,1,-1)$ on the first $4$-cycle and zero on the second. Its quadratic form is
$$
4(2^p-2),
$$
which is positive for every $p>1$. Hence
$$
\wp(0)=1.
$$

Let $P$ be the derivative with respect to $p$ at $(p,s)=(1,0)$, restricted to $\operatorname{span}\{e_A,e_B\}$. For one cycle,
$$
e_A^TPe_A=e_B^TPe_B=8\log2.
$$
For the cross block, with $r=(1,2,1)$, differentiating
$$
(r_i+r_j)^p-r_i^p-r_j^p
$$
at $p=1$ and contracting with $k$ on both sides gives
$$
e_A^TPe_B=20\log2-12\log3.
$$
Hence in the basis $(e_A,e_B)$,
$$
P=
\begin{pmatrix}
8\log2&20\log2-12\log3\\
20\log2-12\log3&8\log2
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
In the symmetric and antisymmetric basis
$$
q_+=\frac{e_A+e_B}{\sqrt2},
\qquad
q_-=\frac{e_A-e_B}{\sqrt2},
$$
this becomes
$$
P_K=\begin{pmatrix}U&0\\0&V\end{pmatrix},
$$
where
$$
U=4\log\frac{128}{27}>0,
\qquad
V=12\log\frac32>0.
$$

Step 4: Compute the first-order metric perturbation on the critical space

For $s>0$, let $H$ be the derivative of the reduced $p=1$ matrix with respect to $s$. The distance $d(a_1,a_3)$ decreases at rate $1$, so
$$
e_A^THe_A=2(-1)(-1)(-1)=-2.
$$
The distance $d(b_1,b_3)$ decreases at rate $2$, so
$$
e_B^THe_B=2(-1)(-1)(-2)=-4.
$$
Finally $d(a_2,b_2)$ decreases at rate $1$, hence
$$
e_A^THe_B=(1)(1)(-1)=-1.
$$
Thus in the basis $(e_A,e_B)$,
$$
H_K=\begin{pmatrix}-2&-1\\-1&-4\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Passing to $(q_+,q_-)$ gives
$$
H_K'=\begin{pmatrix}-4&1\\1&-2\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Step 5: Reduce the boundary to a generalized $2\times2$ splitting

Write $t=p-1$. Decompose a reduced coefficient vector as $x=z+y$, with $z\in\ker R_0$ and $y\perp\ker R_0$. On the complement, $R_0$ has eigenvalues at most $-2$, so maximizing the quadratic form over $y$ gives $y=O(|s|+|t|)$ and changes the value only by $O((|s|+|t|)^2)$. Therefore the sign to first order is governed by
$$
sH_K'+tP_K.
$$

For $s<0$, the metric is exactly $d_0$, so
$$
\wp'(0^-)=0.
$$
For $s>0$, write
$$
\wp(s)=1+\sigma s+o(s).
$$
At the first loss of negative semidefiniteness, the smaller generalized eigenvalue reaches zero, so
$$
\det\left(H_K'+\sigma P_K\right)=0.
$$
Thus
$$
(\sigma U-4)(\sigma V-2)-1=0,
$$
or
$$
UV\sigma^2-(2U+4V)\sigma+7=0.
$$
Since $-H_K'$ and $P_K$ are positive definite, both roots are positive; the first boundary is the smaller root.

Step 6: Extract the right-hand derivative

The smaller root is
$$
\sigma=\frac{2U+4V-\sqrt{(2U+4V)^2-28UV}}{2UV}.
$$
Hence
$$
\wp'(0^+)=\frac{2U+4V-\sqrt{(2U+4V)^2-28UV}}{2UV}.
$$
Combining both sides gives the requested triple.

Final Answer: $\boxed{(1,0,\frac{2U+4V-\sqrt{(2U+4V)^2-28UV}}{2UV})}$
