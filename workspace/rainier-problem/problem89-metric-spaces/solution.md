## Steps

Step 1: Resolve the one-sided metric perturbation

At $s=0$, each added chord has the same length as an old geodesic, so $d_0$ is the wedge of two unit $4$-cycles at $\ast$. Put
$$
L=\log2,\qquad M=\log\frac32.
$$
Since $8L-12M=4\log\frac{32}{27}>0$, all three chords are longer than the old geodesics for $s<0$. For $0<s<\frac18$, the only changed distances are
$$
d_s(a_1,a_3)=2-4Ls+s^2,
$$
$$
d_s(b_1,b_3)=2-4Ls,
\qquad
d_s(a_2,b_2)=4-(8L-12M)s.
$$
The added $s^2$ term does not change which paths are active because $4Ls-s^2>0$ on this interval.

Step 2: Find the two-dimensional critical kernel at $p=1$

Use reduced coordinates relative to $\ast$. For one $4$-cycle, with non-base vertices ordered $(1,2,3)$,
$$
R_4=
\begin{pmatrix}
-2&-2&0\\
-2&-4&-2\\
0&-2&-2
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Its eigenvalues are $0,-2,-6$, and its kernel is generated by
$$
k=(-1,1,-1)^T.
$$
For the wedge, cross distances are sums of distances to $\ast$, so the reduced cross block vanishes at $p=1$. Hence
$$
R_0=R_4\oplus R_4
$$
is negative semidefinite with kernel generated by
$$
e_A=(k,0),\qquad e_B=(0,k).
$$
The full zero-sum vector supported on the first cycle with coefficients $(1,-1,1,-1)$ has quadratic form $4(2^p-2)$, which is positive for every $p>1$. Therefore
$$
\wp(0)=1.
$$

Step 3: Compute the first-order kernel pencil

Let $P=\partial_pR(1,0)$ restricted to $\operatorname{span}\{e_A,e_B\}$. Direct contraction gives
$$
P=
\begin{pmatrix}
8L&8L-12M\\
8L-12M&8L
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
With
$$
q_+=\frac{e_A+e_B}{\sqrt2},\qquad q_-=\frac{e_A-e_B}{\sqrt2},
$$
this becomes
$$
P_K=\begin{pmatrix}U&0\\0&V\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
U=16L-12M,
\qquad
V=12M.
$$
The first-order metric derivative is unchanged by the new $s^2$ term. The two internal chords and the cross chord therefore give
$$
H=-P
$$
on the whole critical space. If $p=1+\sigma s+o(s)$, the first-order critical form is $(\sigma-1)sP_K$. Hence
$$
\wp'(0^+)=1.
$$

Step 4: Compute the second-order effective form before the new correction

Write
$$
p=1+s+\kappa s^2.
$$
For a distance $d(s)=d-\rho s+\gamma s^2$, Taylor expansion gives
$$
d(s)^p=d+s(d\log d-\rho)
+s^2\left(d\kappa\log d+\frac d2(\log d)^2-\rho(1+\log d)+\gamma\right)+O(s^3).
$$
For the original linear chord rates, the first-order matrix couples the kernel only to the $-6$ eigendirections
$$
f_A=(1,2,1,0,0,0)^T,
\qquad
f_B=(0,0,0,1,2,1)^T.
$$
They satisfy
$$
f_A^TR_0f_A=f_B^TR_0f_B=-36,
$$
$$
f_B^TA_1e_A=f_A^TA_1e_B=18(M-L),
$$
with all other kernel-to-complement contractions zero. Eliminating the complement therefore contributes
$$
9(L-M)^2I_2
$$
to the second-order critical matrix.

Combining the direct Taylor terms with this correction gives, in the basis $(q_+,q_-)$ and before the new $+s^2$ term,
$$
\begin{pmatrix}
U\kappa+C_+&0\\
0&V\kappa+C_-
\end{pmatrix},
$$
where
$$
C_+=-3L^2-6LM+3M^2-16L+12M,
$$
$$
C_-=13L^2-30LM+15M^2-12M.
$$

Step 5: Insert the asymmetric quadratic correction

Only the chord $\{a_1,a_3\}$ has the additional coefficient $\gamma=1$. On the kernel basis $(e_A,e_B)$, this adds
$$
\begin{pmatrix}2&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix}
$$
to the second-order bilinear form, because the two ordered occurrences of the pair $(a_1,a_3)$ contribute $2(-1)(-1)=2$.

Passing to $(q_+,q_-)$ transforms this increment into
$$
\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Thus the full second-order effective critical matrix is
$$
K(\kappa)=
\begin{pmatrix}
U\kappa+A&1\\
1&V\kappa+B
\end{pmatrix},
$$
where
$$
A=C_++1,
\qquad
B=C_-+1.
$$
Equivalently,
$$
A=-3L^2-6LM+3M^2-16L+12M+1,
$$
$$
B=13L^2-30LM+15M^2-12M+1.
$$

Step 6: Find the first second-order loss of negative semidefiniteness

At $\kappa=0$, the matrix $K(0)$ is negative definite; numerically $A\approx-7.859$ and $B\approx-3.585$, with $AB-1>0$. Since $U,V>0$, the first boundary occurs at the smaller root of
$$
\det K(\kappa)=0.
$$
That equation is
$$
UV\kappa^2+(UB+VA)\kappa+AB-1=0.
$$
Its discriminant simplifies to
$$
(UB-VA)^2+4UV.
$$
Hence
$$
\kappa_*=\frac{-(UB+VA)-\sqrt{(UB-VA)^2+4UV}}{2UV}.
$$
Therefore
$$
\wp(s)=1+s+\kappa_*s^2+o(s^2),
$$
so
$$
\wp''(0^+)=2\kappa_*
=-\frac{UB+VA+\sqrt{(UB-VA)^2+4UV}}{UV}.
$$

Final Answer: $\boxed{(1,1,-\frac{UB+VA+\sqrt{(UB-VA)^2+4UV}}{UV})}$
