## Steps

Step 1: Resolve the active shortest-path regime

At $s=0$, each added chord has the same length as an old geodesic, so $d_0$ is the wedge of two unit $4$-cycles at $\ast$. The constants satisfy
$$
4L>0,\qquad 8L-12M=4\log\frac{32}{27}>0.
$$
Also $g\approx3.361$, $h\approx2.137$, $u\approx-3.420$, $v\approx-4.420$, and $w\approx-4.242$. Hence, after shrinking to $|s|<\frac18$, all three chords are inactive for $s<0$ and active for $s>0$. Every other route using a chord has excess at least $1$ at $s=0$, while the total shortening is less than $\frac12$, so no other shortest path changes. Thus for $s>0$ the only varying distances are exactly the three displayed chord distances.

Step 2: Find the two-dimensional critical kernel at $p=1$

Use reduced coordinates relative to $\ast$. For one $4$-cycle, with non-base vertices ordered $(1,2,3)$,
$$
R_4=
\begin{pmatrix}
-2&-2&0\\
-2&-4&-2\\
0&-2&-2
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Its eigenvalues are $0,-2,-6$, with kernel vector
$$
k=(-1,1,-1)^T.
$$
For the wedge, cross distances are sums of distances to $\ast$, so at $p=1$ the reduced cross block is zero. Hence
$$
R_0=R_4\oplus R_4
$$
is negative semidefinite with kernel generated by
$$
e_A=(k,0),\qquad e_B=(0,k).
$$
The full zero-sum vector supported on the first cycle with coefficients $(1,-1,1,-1)$ has quadratic form $4(2^p-2)$, which is positive for every $p>1$. Therefore
$$
\wp(0)=1.
$$

Step 3: Cancel the complete first-order kernel pencil

Let $P=\partial_pR(1,0)$ restricted to $\operatorname{span}\{e_A,e_B\}$. Direct contraction of the fixed base distances gives
$$
P=
\begin{pmatrix}
8L&8L-12M\\
8L-12M&8L
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
In the basis
$$
q_+=\frac{e_A+e_B}{\sqrt2},\qquad q_-=\frac{e_A-e_B}{\sqrt2},
$$
this is
$$
P_K=\begin{pmatrix}U&0\\0&V\end{pmatrix},
$$
with $U,V>0$. The linear chord rates contribute $-P$ on the entire critical space. Therefore, if
$$
p=1+\sigma s+O(s^2),
$$
the first-order critical form is $(\sigma-1)sP_K$, so
$$
\wp'(0^+)=1.
$$

Step 4: Cancel the complete second-order effective form

Write
$$
p=1+s+\kappa s^2+O(s^3).
$$
For a varying distance $d(s)=d-\rho s+\gamma s^2+O(s^3)$ with $\ell=\log d$,
$$
d(s)^p=d+s(d\ell-\rho)+s^2\left(d\kappa\ell+\frac d2\ell^2-\rho(1+\ell)+\gamma\right)+O(s^3).
$$
Let
$$
f_A=(1,2,1,0,0,0)^T,\qquad f_B=(0,0,0,1,2,1)^T.
$$
The first-order matrix couples the kernel only through
$$
f_A^TR_0f_A=f_B^TR_0f_B=-36,
$$
$$
f_B^TA_1e_A=f_A^TA_1e_B=18(M-L),
$$
with the other kernel-to-complement contractions zero. Eliminating the complement contributes $9(L-M)^2I_2$. Combining this with the direct Taylor terms gives, before the quadratic chord corrections,
$$
\begin{pmatrix}
U\kappa+C_+&0\\
0&V\kappa+C_-
\end{pmatrix}
$$
in the $(q_+,q_-)$ basis. The two equal internal corrections $g$ and the cross correction $h$ add
$$
\begin{pmatrix}2g+h&0\\0&2g-h\end{pmatrix}.
$$
By the definitions of $g$ and $h$, $2g+h=-C_+$ and $2g-h=-C_-$. Thus the entire second-order effective form is
$$
\begin{pmatrix}U\kappa&0\\0&V\kappa\end{pmatrix},
$$
so the boundary has $\kappa=0$ and therefore
$$
\wp''(0^+)=0.
$$

Step 5: Compute the third-order effective critical matrix

Now write
$$
p=1+s+\mu s^3+O(s^4).
$$
For $d(s)=d-\rho s+\gamma s^2+\delta s^3$ and $\ell=\log d$, the cubic coefficient in $d(s)^p$ is
$$
d\ell\mu+\frac d6\ell^3+\delta+\gamma(1+\ell)-\rho\ell-\frac\rho2\ell^2+\frac{\rho^2}{2d}.
$$
If
$$
R=R_0+sA_1+s^2A_2+s^3A_3+O(s^4),
$$
the cubic Schur-complement term on the kernel is
$$
A_{3,KK}-A_{1,KC}R_{0,C}^{-1}A_{2,CK}-A_{2,KC}R_{0,C}^{-1}A_{1,CK}
+A_{1,KC}R_{0,C}^{-1}A_{1,CC}R_{0,C}^{-1}A_{1,CK}.
$$
Substituting the fixed distances, the linear rates, and the already chosen $g,h$ gives, before the cubic chord corrections,
$$
\begin{pmatrix}D_+&0\\0&D_-\end{pmatrix}+\mu\begin{pmatrix}U&0\\0&V\end{pmatrix}.
$$
The cubic chord coefficients $u,v,w$ add
$$
\begin{pmatrix}u+v+w&u-v\\u-v&u+v-w\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Their definitions give
$$
u-v=1,
$$
$$
D_++u+v+w=1,
\qquad
D_-+u+v-w=2.
$$
Hence the complete cubic effective form is
$$
T(\mu)=
\begin{pmatrix}
1+U\mu&1\\
1&2+V\mu
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Step 6: Find the first third-order loss of negative semidefiniteness

For sufficiently negative $\mu$, the matrix $T(\mu)$ is negative definite because $U,V>0$. The first boundary is therefore the smaller root of
$$
\det T(\mu)=0.
$$
This equation is
$$
UV\mu^2+(2U+V)\mu+1=0.
$$
Its smaller root is
$$
\mu_*=\frac{-(2U+V)-\sqrt{4U^2+V^2}}{2UV}.
$$
Thus
$$
\wp(s)=1+s+\mu_*s^3+o(s^3),
$$
so
$$
\wp'''(0^+)=6\mu_*=-\frac{3(2U+V+\sqrt{4U^2+V^2})}{UV}.
$$

Final Answer: $\boxed{(1,1,0,-\frac{3(2U+V+\sqrt{4U^2+V^2})}{UV})}$
