## Steps

Step 1: Resolve the one-sided metric perturbation

At $s=0$, each added chord has the same length as an old geodesic, so $d_0$ is the wedge of two unit $4$-cycles at $\ast$. Moreover,
$$
8L-12M=4\log\frac{32}{27}>0.
$$
Thus for $s<0$ all three chords are longer than the old geodesics. For $0<s<\frac18$, the only distances that change are
$$
d_s(a_1,a_3)=2-4Ls,
\qquad
d_s(b_1,b_3)=2-4Ls,
$$
$$
d_s(a_2,b_2)=4-(8L-12M)s.
$$
Every other route using a chord has excess at least $1$ at $s=0$, while the largest shortening is $4Ls<\frac12$, so no other shortest path changes.

Step 2: Find the two-dimensional critical kernel at $p=1$

Use reduced coordinates relative to $\ast$. For one $4$-cycle, with non-base vertices ordered $(1,2,3)$,
$$
R_4=
\begin{pmatrix}
-2&-2&0\\
-2&-4&-2\\
0&-2&-2
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Its eigenvalues are $0,-2,-6$, and its kernel is generated by
$$
k=(-1,1,-1)^T.
$$
For the wedge, cross distances are sums of distances to $\ast$, so the reduced cross block vanishes at $p=1$. Hence
$$
R_0=R_4\oplus R_4
$$
is negative semidefinite with kernel generated by
$$
e_A=(k,0),\qquad e_B=(0,k).
$$
The full zero-sum vector supported on the first cycle with coefficients $(1,-1,1,-1)$ has quadratic form $4(2^p-2)$. It vanishes at $p=1$ and is positive for every $p>1$. Therefore
$$
\wp(0)=1.
$$

Step 3: Compute the first-order kernel pencil

Let $P=\partial_pR(1,0)$ restricted to $\operatorname{span}\{e_A,e_B\}$. Direct contraction of the fixed base distances gives
$$
P=
\begin{pmatrix}
8L&8L-12M\\
8L-12M&8L
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Indeed, the diagonal entry is $8\log2$, while the cross entry is
$$
20\log2-12\log3=8L-12M.
$$
Let $H=\partial_sR(1,0^+)$. The two internal chords contribute $-2(4L)=-8L$ on the diagonal kernel entries, and the cross chord contributes $-(8L-12M)$ off diagonal. Thus
$$
H=-P.
$$
The eigenvectors
$$
q_+=\frac{e_A+e_B}{\sqrt2},\qquad q_-=\frac{e_A-e_B}{\sqrt2}
$$
diagonalize $P$, with positive eigenvalues
$$
U=16L-12M,
\qquad V=12M.
$$
If $p=1+\sigma s+o(s)$, the first-order kernel form is $(\sigma-1)sP$. Hence the boundary has
$$
\wp'(0^+)=1.
$$

Step 4: Reduce the second-order boundary by a Schur complement

Write
$$
p=1+s+\kappa s^2.
$$
For a distance $d(s)=d-\rho s$, Taylor expansion gives
$$
d(s)^p=d+s(d\log d-\rho)
+s^2\left(d\kappa\log d+\frac d2(\log d)^2-\rho(1+\log d)\right)+O(s^3).
$$
Thus the first-order matrix $A_1$ in
$$
R(p,s)=R_0+sA_1+s^2A_2+O(s^3)
$$
has zero quadratic form on the kernel, but it couples the two kernel directions to the $-6$ eigendirections
$$
f_A=(1,2,1,0,0,0)^T,
\qquad
f_B=(0,0,0,1,2,1)^T.
$$
The contractions are
$$
f_A^TR_0f_A=f_B^TR_0f_B=-36,
$$
$$
f_B^TA_1e_A=f_A^TA_1e_B=18(M-L),
$$
with all other kernel-to-complement contractions zero. Eliminating the complement therefore adds
$$
9(L-M)^2I_2
$$
to the second-order kernel matrix.

Step 5: Compute the two second-order kernel modes

Substituting the three chord rates into the Taylor formula from Step 4 and then adding the Schur-complement correction gives the two diagonal entries in the $(q_+,q_-)$ basis:
$$
E_+(\kappa)=U\kappa-3L^2-6LM+3M^2-16L+12M,
$$
$$
E_-(\kappa)=V\kappa+13L^2-30LM+15M^2-12M.
$$
The corresponding roots are
$$
\kappa_+=\frac{3L^2+6LM+16L-3M^2-12M}{16L-12M},
$$
$$
\kappa_-=1-\frac{13L^2-30LM+15M^2}{12M}.
$$
To determine which mode reaches zero first, put $x=L/M>1$. A common-denominator subtraction gives
$$
\kappa_+-\kappa_-
=\frac{M^3(26x^3-75x^2+84x-27)}{6M(4L-3M)}.
$$
The denominator is positive. For
$$
f(x)=26x^3-75x^2+84x-27,
$$
we have $f(1)=8$ and
$$
f'(x)=78x^2-150x+84>0
$$
because its discriminant is negative. Hence $\kappa_-<\kappa_+$.

Step 6: Extract the second derivative

For $\kappa<\kappa_-$ both second-order kernel modes are negative, while at $\kappa=\kappa_-$ the antisymmetric mode reaches zero first. Therefore
$$
\wp(s)=1+s+\kappa_-s^2+o(s^2)
$$
for $s\downarrow0$. Consequently
$$
\wp''(0^+)=2\kappa_-
=2-\frac{13L^2-30LM+15M^2}{6M}.
$$
Combining this with $\wp(0)=1$ and $\wp'(0^+)=1$ gives the requested triple.

Final Answer: $\boxed{(1,1,2-\frac{13L^2-30LM+15M^2}{6M})}$
