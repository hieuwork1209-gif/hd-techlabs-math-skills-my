## Steps

Step 1: Model inhabitants as labeled forests

A normal inhabitant has components
$$
(h_0N_0)\otimes\cdots\otimes(h_{n-1}N_{n-1}).
$$
Each $N_i:p$ is a plane full binary tree with internal nodes labeled by $b_j$ and leaves labeled by $x_j,y_j$. Linearity forces every label to occur exactly once.

The number of ordered forest shapes with $n$ roots and $n$ internal nodes is
$$
\frac13\binom{3n}{n},
$$
so
$$
|\mathcal N_n|=\frac13\binom{3n}{n}n!(2n)!=\frac{(3n)!}{3}.\tag{1}
$$

Step 2: Remove rotational stabilizers

Let $h=c^{n/2}$. Since $n=2^r$, every nontrivial subgroup of $\langle c\rangle$ contains $h$. Hence trivial rotational stabilizer is equivalent to not being fixed by $h$.

With $m=n/2$, an $h$-fixed forest is determined by its first half. The first half is a forest counted by $\mathcal N_m$, with one representative chosen from every pair
$$
\{b_i,b_{i+m}\},\{x_i,x_{i+m}\},\{y_i,y_{i+m}\}.
$$
Therefore
$$
A:=|\operatorname{Fix}(h)|=2^{3m}|\mathcal N_m|=\frac{2^{3n/2}(3n/2)!}{3}.\tag{2}
$$

Step 3: Count reflection fixed points

Let $s_k=c^ks$. For odd $k$, the reflection has no fixed indices. Its fixed forests are again half-sized forests with transversal choices, giving
$$
|\operatorname{Fix}(s_k)|=A.\tag{3}
$$

For even $k$, the reflection has two fixed indices. This forces fixed labels to occupy fixed tree positions. The two fixed root positions cannot accommodate all six fixed resource labels from the three families $b,x,y$, so
$$
|\operatorname{Fix}(s_k)|=0\qquad(k\text{ even}).\tag{4}
$$

Step 4: Reflection intersections

For odd $k$, the group generated by $h$ and $s_k$ has index orbits of size $4$. Writing $d=n/4$,
$$
B:=|\operatorname{Fix}(h)\cap\operatorname{Fix}(s_k)|
=4^{3d}|\mathcal N_d|
=\frac{2^{3n/2}(3n/4)!}{3}.\tag{5}
$$
Hence each odd reflection fixes $A-B$ elements among the rotationally free set.

Step 5: Count free dihedral orbits

A rotationally free element cannot be fixed by two different reflections, because their product would be a nontrivial rotation. Therefore the number of elements with trivial dihedral stabilizer is
$$
\frac{(3n)!}{3}-A-\frac n2(A-B).
$$
Every such orbit has size $2n$, so the number of full dihedral orbits is
$$
\frac{(3n)!-(1+n/2)2^{3n/2}(3n/2)!+(n/2)2^{3n/2}(3n/4)!}{6n}.
$$

Final Answer: $\boxed{\frac{(3n)!-(1+n/2)2^{3n/2}(3n/2)!+(n/2)2^{3n/2}(3n/4)!}{6n}}$

---

## Answer

$\frac{(3n)!-(1+n/2)2^{3n/2}(3n/2)!+(n/2)2^{3n/2}(3n/4)!}{6n}$
