## Steps

Step 1: Convert nilpotent invariance into module isotropy
Let
$$
R=\mathbb{F}_q[t]/(t^m),
\qquad
V=R^4,
$$
and let
$$
\Omega(x,y)
=
x_1y_3+x_2y_4-x_3y_1-x_4y_2
\in R.
$$
The form in the problem is
$$
\omega(x,y)=[t^{m-1}]\Omega(x,y).
$$

If $L$ is invariant under multiplication by $t$, then $L$ is an $R$-submodule of $V$. Suppose $\omega$ vanishes on $L$. For $x,y\in L$ and $0\leq k\leq m-1$, the vector $t^kx$ also lies in $L$, so
$$
0
=
\omega(t^kx,y)
=
[t^{m-1}]t^k\Omega(x,y)
=
[t^{m-1-k}]\Omega(x,y).
$$
Every coefficient of $\Omega(x,y)$ is therefore zero. Hence
$$
\Omega(x,y)=0
$$
for all $x,y\in L$. Conversely, $\Omega|_L=0$ immediately implies $\omega|_L=0$.

The condition that $N|_L$ has exactly two Jordan blocks of size $m$ says precisely that, as an $R$-module,
$$
L\cong R^2.
$$
Indeed, one Jordan block of size $m$ is the cyclic $R$-module generated by a vector whose annihilator is $(t^m)=0$.

We also need that such an embedded copy of $R^2$ reduces to a two-dimensional subspace modulo $t$. Let $A:R^2\to R^4$ be an injective map with image $L$. If the reduction of $A$ modulo $t$ had rank less than $2$, there would be a vector $u\in R^2$ with at least one unit coordinate such that
$$
Au\in tR^4.
$$
Then
$$
t^{m-1}Au=0,
$$
while
$$
t^{m-1}u\neq0,
$$
contradicting injectivity. Thus
$$
\overline L=L/tL
$$
is a two-dimensional subspace of
$$
V/tV\cong\mathbb F_q^4.
$$
Since $\Omega|_L=0$, the reduction $\overline L$ is isotropic for the standard symplectic form on $\mathbb F_q^4$.

Step 2: Count the possible reductions modulo t
Let $\overline\Omega$ be the standard symplectic form on $\mathbb F_q^4$. Every one-dimensional subspace is isotropic because $\overline\Omega(v,v)=0$.

Count ordered bases $(v,w)$ of isotropic two-dimensional subspaces. There are
$$
q^4-1
$$
choices for $v\neq0$. Its orthogonal space $v^\perp$ has dimension $3$, so there are
$$
q^3-q
$$
choices for
$$
w\in v^\perp\setminus\operatorname{span}(v).
$$
Each two-dimensional subspace has
$$
(q^2-1)(q^2-q)
$$
ordered bases. Therefore the number of Lagrangian planes in $\mathbb F_q^4$ is
$$
\frac{(q^4-1)(q^3-q)}
{(q^2-1)(q^2-q)}
=
(q+1)(q^2+1).
$$

Step 3: Count all lifts of one reduced Lagrangian plane
Fix one Lagrangian plane
$$
\overline P\subset\mathbb F_q^4.
$$
Choose a basis $e_1,e_2$ of $\overline P$. Nondegeneracy of the standard symplectic form lets us choose $f_1,f_2$ with
$$
\overline\Omega(e_i,f_j)=\delta_{ij}.
$$
If
$$
c=\overline\Omega(f_1,f_2),
$$
replace $f_2$ by $f_2+ce_1$. The pairings with $e_1,e_2$ stay unchanged, while
$$
\overline\Omega(f_1,f_2+ce_1)=c-c=0.
$$
Thus
$$
\overline Q=\operatorname{span}(f_1,f_2+ce_1)
$$
is a complementary Lagrangian plane. Lift these four basis vectors to constant vectors in $R^4$. They span free $R$-submodules
$$
P,Q\subset R^4
$$
with
$$
R^4=P\oplus Q,
$$
and $\Omega$ pairs $P$ and $Q$ perfectly while vanishing on each of them.

Let $L\subset R^4$ be a free rank-$2$ $R$-submodule whose reduction is $\overline P$. The projection
$$
\pi_P:L\to P
$$
is an isomorphism: modulo $t$ it is the identity on $\overline P$, so its determinant is a unit in the local ring $R$. Hence $L$ is the graph of a unique $R$-linear map
$$
X:P\to Q
$$
whose reduction modulo $t$ is zero. Thus
$$
X\in t\operatorname{Hom}_R(P,Q).
$$

Use the perfect pairing to identify $Q$ with $P^*$. Define
$$
B_X(p,p')=\Omega(p,Xp').
$$
For $p,p'\in P$,
$$
\Omega(p+Xp,p'+Xp')
=
B_X(p,p')-B_X(p',p).
$$
Therefore the graph of $X$ is $\Omega$-isotropic exactly when $B_X$ is symmetric.

After choosing the lifted bases, $X$ is represented by a $2\times2$ matrix with entries in $tR$, and the isotropy condition says that matrix is symmetric. The ideal $tR$ has
$$
q^{m-1}
$$
elements. A symmetric $2\times2$ matrix has three independent entries, so every fixed reduced Lagrangian plane has exactly
$$
q^{3(m-1)}
$$
free rank-$2$ isotropic lifts.

Step 4: Combine the reduction and lifting counts
Every subspace requested in the problem reduces to one Lagrangian plane in $\mathbb F_q^4$ by Step 1. Step 3 shows that each such plane has exactly
$$
q^{3(m-1)}
$$
lifts satisfying the nilpotent Jordan-type and isotropy conditions. Since distinct lifts have distinct $R$-submodules and every lift is counted through its unique reduction, the total number is
$$
q^{3(m-1)}(q+1)(q^2+1).
$$
Final Answer: $\boxed{q^{3(m-1)}(q+1)(q^2+1)}$
