Step 1: Convert nilpotent invariance into module isotropy
Let
$$
R=mathbb{F}_q[t]/(t^m),
qquad
V=R^4,
$$
and let
$$
Omega(x,y)
=
x_1y_3+x_2y_4-x_3y_1-x_4y_2
in R.
$$
The form in the problem is
$$
omega(x,y)=[t^{m-1}]Omega(x,y).
$$

If $L$ is invariant under multiplication by $t$, then $L$ is an $R$-submodule of $V$. Suppose $omega$ vanishes on $L$. For $x,yin L$ and $0leq kleq m-1$, the vector $t^kx$ also lies in $L$, so
$$
0
=
omega(t^kx,y)
=
[t^{m-1}]t^kOmega(x,y)
=
[t^{m-1-k}]Omega(x,y).
$$
Every coefficient of $Omega(x,y)$ is therefore zero. Hence
$$
Omega(x,y)=0
$$
for all $x,yin L$. Conversely, $Omega|_L=0$ immediately implies $omega|_L=0$.

The condition that $N|_L$ has exactly two Jordan blocks of size $m$ says precisely that, as an $R$-module,
$$
Lcong R^2.

Indeed, one Jordan block of size $m$ is the cyclic $R$-module generated by a vector whose annihilator is $(t^m)=0$.

We also need that such an embedded copy of $R^2$ reduces to a two-dimensional subspace modulo $t$. Let $A:R^2	o R^4$ be an injective map with image $L$. If the reduction of $A$ modulo $t$ had rank less than $2$, there would be a vector $uin R^2$ with at least one unit coordinate such that
