## Steps

Step 1: Reveal the hidden Jacobi algebra
Let
$$
E=\frac{X+Y}{2},
\qquad
F=\frac{X-Y}{2},
\qquad
H=Z.
$$
The defining relations give
$$
[H,E]=2E,
\qquad
[H,F]=-2F,
\qquad
[E,F]=H,
$$
while
$$
[E,p]=0,
\qquad [E,q]=p,
$$
$$
[F,p]=q,
\qquad [F,q]=0,
$$
and
$$
[H,p]=p,
\qquad [H,q]=-q.
$$
Also
$$
[p,q]=c,
$$
with $c$ central. Thus $R$ is the enveloping algebra of the semidirect product of $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ with the three-dimensional Heisenberg Lie algebra. In particular PBW gives unique normal forms, so $R$ is a domain and localization at powers of $c$ is injective.

Step 2: Split the localization into a Weyl part and an $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ part
Work in
$$
R_c=R[c^{-1}].
$$
Define
$$
\widehat E=E-\frac{p^2}{2c},
\qquad
\widehat F=F+\frac{q^2}{2c},
$$
and
$$
\widehat H=H+\frac{pq+qp}{2c}.
$$
Using $[p,q]=c$, direct commutator calculations give
$$
[\widehat E,p]=[\widehat E,q]=0,
$$
$$
[\widehat F,p]=[\widehat F,q]=0,
$$
$$
[\widehat H,p]=[\widehat H,q]=0.
$$
They also satisfy
$$
[\widehat H,\widehat E]=2\widehat E,
\qquad
[\widehat H,\widehat F]=-2\widehat F,
\qquad
[\widehat E,\widehat F]=\widehat H.
$$
Hence, over
$$
K=k[c,c^{-1}],
$$
the localization is the tensor product of the Weyl algebra generated by $p,q$ with $[p,q]=c$ and the enveloping algebra generated by $\widehat E,\widehat F,\widehat H$. The change is invertible, so PBW gives
$$
R_c\cong W_K(p,q)\otimes_K U_K(\mathfrak{sl}_2).
$$

Step 3: Compute the localized center
The center of the Weyl factor is $K$. Indeed, if a PBW polynomial in $p,q$ commutes with $p$, its positive $q$-degree vanishes because
$$
[p,q^n]=ncq^{n-1},
$$
and then commuting with $q$ eliminates positive $p$-degree.

For the $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ factor, set
$$
\Omega=\widehat H^2+2\widehat H+4\widehat F\widehat E.
$$
A direct use of the three $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ brackets shows that $\Omega$ is central. To see that it generates the full center, use the PBW filtration. In the associated graded polynomial ring $K[e,h,f]$, a Poisson-central polynomial $P$ satisfies
$$
\{h,P\}=2e\partial_eP-2f\partial_fP=0,
$$
so $P=P(h,s)$ with $s=ef$. Then
$$
\{e,P\}=e\left(-2\partial_hP+h\partial_sP\right)=0.
$$
Thus $P$ is a polynomial in
$$
h^2+4s.
$$
Subtracting a matching polynomial in $\Omega$ lowers PBW degree, so induction gives
$$
Z\bigl(U_K(\mathfrak{sl}_2)\bigr)=K[\Omega].
$$
Therefore
$$
Z(R_c)=K[\Omega].
$$

Now define
$$
\Theta=4c\Omega.
$$
Expanding back to the original generators gives
$$
\Theta=
4c\bigl(Z^2+Z+(X-Y)(X+Y)\bigr)-3c
+8pqZ-4p^2(X-Y)+4q^2(X+Y).
$$
Hence
$$
Z(R_c)=k[c,c^{-1},\Theta].
$$

Step 4: Intersect back with $R$
Let $z\in Z(R)$. By Step 3, for some $N\geq0$,
$$
z=c^{-N}F(c,\Theta)
$$
with $F\in k[c,T]$, and choose $N$ minimal. Suppose $N>0$. Since $z\in R$, reducing the numerator modulo $c$ gives
$$
F(0,\overline\Theta)=0
$$
in $R/(c)$, where
$$
\overline\Theta
=8pqZ-4p^2(X-Y)+4q^2(X+Y).
$$
Filter $R/(c)$ by total degree in $X,Y,Z$. The element $\overline\Theta$ has degree one and nonzero leading symbol in the polynomial domain
$$
k[p,q,x,y,z].
$$
Therefore the powers of $\overline\Theta$ have distinct nonzero leading degrees, so the substitution map
$$
k[T]\to R/(c),
\qquad T\mapsto\overline\Theta
$$
is injective. Thus
$$
F(0,T)=0,
$$
so $c$ divides $F(c,T)$ in $k[c,T]$, contradicting minimality of $N$. Hence $N=0$ and
$$
Z(R)=k[c,\Theta].
$$
Final Answer: $\boxed{k[c,4c(Z^2+Z+(X-Y)(X+Y))-3c+8pqZ-4p^2(X-Y)+4q^2(X+Y)]}$
