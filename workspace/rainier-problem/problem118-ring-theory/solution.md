## Steps

Step 1: Build a PBW normal form and isolate the resonant term
Put
$$
F(h,s)=(\zeta^{-1}-1)h+(\zeta^{-2}-1)s+(\zeta^{-3}-1)hs+hs^2.
$$
Start from the commutative polynomial ring $k[h,s]$. Adjoin $x$ using the automorphism
$$
\sigma_0(h)=\zeta h,
\qquad
\sigma_0(s)=\zeta^2s,
$$
so that $xh=\zeta hx$ and $xs=\zeta^2sx$. Next adjoin $y$ using
$$
\tau(h)=\zeta^{-1}h,
\qquad
\tau(s)=\zeta^{-2}s,
\qquad
\tau(x)=x,
$$
and the $\tau$-derivation determined by
$$
\delta(h)=\delta(s)=0,
\qquad
\delta(x)=F(h,s).
$$
For example,
$$
\delta(xh-\zeta hx)=F(h,s)h-\zeta\tau(h)F(h,s)=0,
$$
and similarly $\delta(xs-\zeta^2sx)=0$. Hence every element of $R$ has a unique PBW normal form
$$
\sum_{a,b,c,d\geq0}\lambda_{a,b,c,d}h^as^bx^cy^d.
$$

Set
$$
g(h,s)=h+s+hs,
\qquad
P=hs^2.
$$
Then
$$
F(h,s)=g(\zeta^{-1}h,\zeta^{-2}s)-g(h,s)+P.
$$
The monomial $P$ is resonant because
$$
P(\zeta h,\zeta^2s)=\zeta^5hs^2=P.
$$

Step 2: Convert the algebra into a generalized Weyl form
Define
$$
C=xy-g(h,s).
$$
Since $x$ and $y$ have opposite weights on both $h$ and $s$, the product $xy$ commutes with $h$ and $s$, so $C$ does as well. Also
$$
yx=C+g(\zeta^{-1}h,\zeta^{-2}s)+P.
$$
Using $xf(h,s)=f(\zeta h,\zeta^2s)x$, we obtain
$$
Cx
=x\bigl(C+g(\zeta^{-1}h,\zeta^{-2}s)+P\bigr)-g(h,s)x
=xC+Px,
$$
so
$$
xC=(C-P)x.
$$
Similarly,
$$
yC=(C+P)y.
$$

The subalgebra generated by $h,s,C$ is a polynomial ring
$$
B=k[h,s,C].
$$
Indeed, in the PBW basis from Step 1, $C^r$ has leading term $x^ry^r$, so no nonzero polynomial in $h,s,C$ can vanish. Define an automorphism $\sigma$ of $B$ by
$$
\sigma(h)=\zeta h,
\qquad
\sigma(s)=\zeta^2s,
\qquad
\sigma(C)=C-P.
$$
Then
$$
xb=\sigma(b)x,
\qquad
yb=\sigma^{-1}(b)y
\qquad(b\in B).
$$
Because $\sigma(P)=P$, iteration gives
$$
\sigma^n(C)=C-nP.
$$
In particular,
$$
\sigma^5(C)=C-5P\neq C.
$$
Thus the fifth-root symmetry on $h,s$ does not make the full automorphism periodic; the resonant term produces a nontrivial translation in $C$.

Step 3: Eliminate every nonzero graded piece from the center
Give $R$ the $\mathbb Z$-grading
$$
\deg x=1,
\qquad
\deg y=-1,
\qquad
\deg h=\deg s=\deg C=0.
$$
Using $xy=C+g(h,s)$ and the PBW basis, each positive homogeneous component has the form
$$
bx^n\qquad(b\in B,\ n>0),
$$
and each negative homogeneous component has the form
$$
by^n\qquad(b\in B,\ n>0).
$$
A central element has central homogeneous components, so consider $bx^n$. Commutation with $C$ gives
$$
(bx^n)C=b\sigma^n(C)x^n=b(C-nP)x^n,
$$
whereas
$$
C(bx^n)=bCx^n.
$$
Since $B$ is a domain, $P=hs^2\neq0$, and the characteristic is zero, these can agree for $n>0$ only when $b=0$. The same argument with $y^n$ uses $\sigma^{-n}(C)=C+nP$ and again forces $b=0$.

Therefore every central element lies in the degree-zero polynomial subalgebra $B$.

Step 4: Compute the fixed ring inside the degree-zero subalgebra
An element $b\in B$ is central exactly when
$$
\sigma(b)=b.
$$
Such an element is also fixed by $\sigma^5$. Since $\sigma^5$ fixes $h,s$ and sends $C$ to $C-5P$, write
$$
b=\sum_{j=0}^{d}b_j(h,s)C^j,
\qquad b_d\neq0.
$$
If $d>0$, then the coefficient of $C^{d-1}$ in
$$
b(h,s,C-5P)-b(h,s,C)
$$
is
$$
-5dP\,b_d(h,s),
$$
which is nonzero in the domain $k[h,s]$. Hence $d=0$, so every central element actually lies in $k[h,s]$.

Now
$$
\sigma(h^as^b)=\zeta^{a+2b}h^as^b.
$$
Therefore a monomial is fixed exactly when
$$
a+2b\equiv0\pmod5.
$$
The semigroup of such pairs is generated by
$$
(5,0),
\qquad(3,1),
\qquad(1,2),
\qquad(0,5).
$$
Indeed, after removing multiples of $(0,5)$, the five possible residues of $b$ are generated respectively by $(5,0)$, $(3,1)$, $(1,2)$, $(3,1)+(1,2)$, and $2(1,2)$. Hence
$$
Z(R)=k[h^5,h^3s,hs^2,s^5].
$$
Final Answer: $\boxed{k[h^5,h^3s,hs^2,s^5]}$
