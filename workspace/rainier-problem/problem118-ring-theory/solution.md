## Steps

Step 1: Encode the relations by one hidden automorphism
Let
$$
A=k[h,s,t].
$$
Define an automorphism $\sigma$ of $A$ by
$$
\sigma(h)=\zeta h,
\qquad
\sigma(s)=\zeta^2(s+h^2),
\qquad
\sigma(t)=\zeta^3(t+hs+2h^3).
$$
Its inverse is
$$
\sigma^{-1}(h)=\zeta^{-1}h,
\qquad
\sigma^{-1}(s)=\zeta^{-2}(s-h^2),
\qquad
\sigma^{-1}(t)=\zeta^{-3}(t-hs-h^3),
$$
as is checked by direct substitution. The defining relations are exactly
$$
xa=\sigma(a)x,
\qquad
ya=\sigma^{-1}(a)y
\qquad(a\in A),
$$
together with $yx=xy$. Hence $R$ is an iterated skew polynomial ring and has a unique PBW normal form
$$
\sum_{a,b,c,i,j\geq0}\lambda_{a,b,c,i,j}h^as^bt^cx^iy^j.
$$

Set
$$
C=xy.
$$
Because $x$ and $y$ commute and act on $A$ by inverse automorphisms, $C$ commutes with $A,x,y$. Thus
$$
C\in Z(R).
$$

Step 2: Expose the infinite-order shear and its polynomial invariant
Let $\rho$ be the diagonal automorphism
$$
\rho(h)=\zeta h,
\qquad
\rho(s)=\zeta^2s,
\qquad
\rho(t)=\zeta^3t,
$$
and let $D$ be the derivation of $A$ defined by
$$
D(h)=0,
\qquad
D(s)=h^2,
\qquad
D(t)=hs+\frac32h^3.
$$
Since $D^2(s)=0$, $D^2(t)=h^3$, and $D^3(t)=0$, the exponential $\exp(D)$ is a polynomial automorphism. It satisfies
$$
\exp(D)(s)=s+h^2,
\qquad
\exp(D)(t)=t+hs+2h^3.
$$
The derivation is homogeneous for the weights $1,2,3$, so $D$ commutes with $\rho$. Therefore
$$
\sigma=\rho\exp(D)
$$
and, because $\rho^5=\operatorname{id}$,
$$
\sigma^5=\exp(5D).
$$
In particular, $\sigma$ has infinite order.

Now define
$$
Q=2ht-s^2-3h^2s.
$$
A direct calculation gives
$$
D(Q)
=2h\left(hs+\frac32h^3\right)-2sh^2-3h^4
=0.
$$
Also every term of $Q$ has $\rho$-weight $4$, so
$$
\sigma(Q)=\zeta^4Q.
$$

Step 3: Compute the fixed ring of $\sigma$ on $A$
We first show
$$
\ker D=k[h,Q].
$$
Localize at $h$ and put
$$
u=\frac{s}{h^2}.
$$
Then $D(u)=1$. Moreover
$$
t=\frac{Q+s^2+3h^2s}{2h},
$$
so
$$
A_h=k[h,h^{-1},u,Q].
$$
On this localization, $D$ is differentiation with respect to $u$, hence
$$
(\ker D)_h=k[h,h^{-1},Q].
$$
If an element of $A$ lies in $k[h,h^{-1},Q]$, write it as
$$
\sum_{j=0}^{d}c_j(h)Q^j
$$
with Laurent-polynomial coefficients and choose the smallest $h$-valuation among the $c_j$. If that valuation were negative, multiplying by the corresponding power of $h$ and reducing modulo $h$ would give a nonzero polynomial in $Q\bmod h=-s^2$, a contradiction to polynomiality in $A$. Thus all $c_j(h)$ lie in $k[h]$, proving the claim.

If $f\in A$ is fixed by $\sigma$, then it is fixed by $\sigma^5=\exp(5D)$. In the localized coordinates above, $\exp(5D)$ sends $u$ to $u+5$ and fixes $h,Q$. A polynomial invariant under the nonzero translation $u\mapsto u+5$ is independent of $u$, so $f\in\ker D=k[h,Q]$.

On $k[h,Q]$, the automorphism acts diagonally:
$$
\sigma(h)=\zeta h,
\qquad
\sigma(Q)=\zeta^4Q.
$$
Hence $h^aQ^b$ is fixed exactly when
$$
a+4b\equiv0\pmod5,
$$
equivalently $a\equiv b\pmod5$. The corresponding semigroup is generated by
$$
(5,0),
\qquad(1,1),
\qquad(0,5).
$$
Therefore
$$
A^\sigma=k[h^5,hQ,Q^5].
$$

Step 4: Eliminate nonzero degrees and assemble the center
Give $R$ the $\mathbb Z$-grading
$$
\deg x=1,
\qquad
\deg y=-1,
\qquad
\deg A=0.
$$
Since $C=xy$ is central, the degree-zero part is the polynomial ring
$$
B=A[C],
$$
and every positive homogeneous element has the form $bx^n$ with $b\in B$, while every negative homogeneous element has the form $by^n$.

Suppose $bx^n$ is central with $n>0$. Commuting with $A$ forces $\sigma^n$ to act trivially wherever $b$ is nonzero. If $5\nmid n$, then
$$
\sigma^n(h)=\zeta^n h\neq h,
$$
so centrality with $h$ gives $b=0$. If $n=5m$, then
$$
\sigma^n=\exp(nD)
$$
and therefore
$$
\sigma^n(s)=s+nh^2\neq s.
$$
Centrality with $s$ again gives $b=0$. The same argument using $\sigma^{-n}$ eliminates every negative homogeneous component.

Thus every central element lies in $B$. Since $\sigma(C)=C$, an element of $B$ commutes with $x$ and $y$ exactly when its $A$-coefficients lie in $A^\sigma$. Hence
$$
Z(R)=A^\sigma[C]
=k[C,h^5,hQ,Q^5].
$$
Substituting $C=xy$ and $Q=2ht-s^2-3h^2s$ gives
$$
Z(R)=k\left[xy,h^5,h(2ht-s^2-3h^2s),(2ht-s^2-3h^2s)^5\right].
$$
Final Answer: $\boxed{k[xy,h^5,h(2ht-s^2-3h^2s),(2ht-s^2-3h^2s)^5]}$
