## Steps

Step 1: Build a PBW normal form and isolate the hidden difference equation
Put
$$
F(h)=(\zeta^{-1}-1)h+(\zeta^{-2}-1)h^2.
$$
Start from $k[h]$ and adjoin $x$ with
$$
xh=\zeta hx.
$$
Next adjoin $y$ with
$$
yh=\zeta^{-1}hy,
\qquad
yx=xy+F(h).
$$
This is an Ore extension: on the algebra generated by $h,x$, use the automorphism
$$
\tau(h)=\zeta^{-1}h,
\qquad
\tau(x)=x
$$
and the $\tau$-derivation determined by
$$
\delta(h)=0,
\qquad
\delta(x)=F(h).
$$
The only compatibility to check is the relation $xh-\zeta hx=0$, and
$$
\delta(xh-\zeta hx)=F(h)h-hF(h)=0.
$$
Hence every element has a unique PBW normal form
$$
\sum_{a,b,c\geq0}\lambda_{a,b,c}h^a x^b y^c.
$$

Now set
$$
g(h)=h+h^2.
$$
The inhomogeneous commutator is exactly the multiplicative difference
$$
F(h)=g(\zeta^{-1}h)-g(h).
$$

Step 2: Construct the hidden central Casimir
Define
$$
C=xy-g(h)=xy-h-h^2.
$$
Since $x$ and $y$ have opposite $h$-weights, $xy$ commutes with $h$, so $[C,h]=0$. Also
$$
yx=C+g(\zeta^{-1}h),
\qquad
xy=C+g(h).
$$
Using $xf(h)=f(\zeta h)x$ for every polynomial $f$, we get
$$
Cx
=xyx-g(h)x
=x\bigl(C+g(\zeta^{-1}h)\bigr)-g(h)x
=xC.
$$
Likewise, using $yf(h)=f(\zeta^{-1}h)y$,
$$
yC
=yxy-yg(h)
=\bigl(C+g(\zeta^{-1}h)\bigr)y-g(\zeta^{-1}h)y
=Cy.
$$
Therefore
$$
C\in Z(R).
$$

Step 3: Produce the root-of-unity central powers
Because $\zeta^5=1$, the element $h^5$ commutes with both $x$ and $y$.

For $x^5$, from $xy=C+g(h)$ we have, for $n\geq1$,
$$
x^n y
=\bigl(C+g(\zeta^{n-1}h)\bigr)x^{n-1},
$$
whereas
$$
yx^n
=\bigl(C+g(\zeta^{-1}h)\bigr)x^{n-1}.
$$
At $n=5$, $\zeta^4=\zeta^{-1}$, so $x^5y=yx^5$. Since $x^5$ also commutes with $h$, it is central.

Similarly,
$$
y^n x
=\bigl(C+g(\zeta^{-n}h)\bigr)y^{n-1},
$$
while
$$
xy^n
=\bigl(C+g(h)\bigr)y^{n-1}.
$$
At $n=5$, $\zeta^{-5}=1$, so $y^5x=xy^5$. Hence
$$
h^5,
\qquad x^5,
\qquad y^5,
\qquad C
$$
are all central.

Step 4: Prove that no other central elements occur
The identity
$$
xy=C+g(h)
$$
reduces one positive $x$-power and one positive $y$-power at a time. Repeating shows that $R$ is spanned by
$$
C^r h^a x^b
\quad(r,a,b\geq0),
$$
and
$$
C^r h^a y^c
\quad(r,a\geq0,\ c\geq1).
$$
This spanning family is linearly independent. Indeed, after expanding powers of $C=xy-g(h)$ in the PBW basis from Step 1, the first family has leading $(x,y)$-exponents $(r+b,r)$, while the second has leading exponents $(r,r+c)$; these pairs are all distinct. Thus it is a basis.

Let a central element be expanded in this basis. Commuting with $h$ multiplies a term containing $x^b$ by $\zeta^b$ and a term containing $y^c$ by $\zeta^{-c}$. Linear independence therefore forces
$$
b\equiv0\pmod5,
\qquad
c\equiv0\pmod5.
$$
Since $x^5$, $y^5$, and $C$ are already central, every central element is therefore a finite sum of terms
$$
P(C,x^5,y^5)f(h).
$$
Finally, commuting with $x$ gives
$$
f(\zeta h)=f(h).
$$
Because $\zeta$ is a primitive fifth root, a polynomial satisfies this identity exactly when it lies in $k[h^5]$. Hence
$$
Z(R)=k[h^5,x^5,y^5,C].
$$
Substituting $C=xy-h-h^2$ gives
$$
Z(R)=k[h^5,x^5,y^5,xy-h-h^2].
$$
Final Answer: $\boxed{k[h^5,x^5,y^5,xy-h-h^2]}$
