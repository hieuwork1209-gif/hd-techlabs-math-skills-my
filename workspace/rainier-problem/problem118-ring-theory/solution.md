## Steps

Step 1: Localize at the Heisenberg center
Let \(\mathfrak g\) be the Lie algebra with basis \(E,F,H,p,q,z\) and brackets from the problem. Then \(R=U(\mathfrak g)\), and PBW shows that \(R\) is a domain. In particular the central element \(z\) is regular, so we may form
\[
S=R[z^{-1}].
\]
Inside \(S\), define
\[
e=E-\frac{p^2}{2z},\qquad
f=F+\frac{q^2}{2z},\qquad
h=H+\frac{pq}{z}-\frac12.
\]
Using \([p,q]=z\), \([E,q]=p\), \([F,p]=q\), \([H,p]=p\), and \([H,q]=-q\), one checks
\[
[e,p]=[e,q]=[f,p]=[f,q]=[h,p]=[h,q]=0,
\]
and
\[
[h,e]=2e,\qquad [h,f]=-2f,\qquad [e,f]=h.
\]
Thus \(e,f,h\) form a copy of \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\) commuting with the localized Weyl pair \(p,q\).

Step 2: Compute the center after localization
Put \(K=k[z^{\pm1}]\). Every element of \(S\) has a unique expansion
\[
\sum_{i,j\ge0}p^iq^j u_{ij},\qquad u_{ij}\in U_K(\mathfrak{sl}_2),
\]
where \(U_K(\mathfrak{sl}_2)\) is generated by \(e,f,h\). If such an element commutes with \(p\), then
\[
[p,q^j]=jzq^{j-1}
\]
forces all terms with \(j>0\) to vanish. Commuting with \(q\) then uses
\[
[q,p^i]=-izp^{i-1}
\]
and forces all terms with \(i>0\) to vanish. Hence a central element of \(S\) lies in \(U_K(\mathfrak{sl}_2)\).

For \(U_K(\mathfrak{sl}_2)\), set
\[
\Omega=h^2+2h+4fe.
\]
A direct commutator calculation shows \(\Omega\) is central. To see it generates the full center, use PBW. In the associated graded algebra, an invariant polynomial first has weight zero under \(h\), so it is a polynomial in \(s=ef\) and \(h\). Invariance under \(e\) gives
\[
h\,\partial_sP-2\partial_hP=0,
\]
whose polynomial solutions are exactly \(K[h^2+4s]\). Therefore the leading symbol of any central element is a polynomial in the leading symbol of \(\Omega\), and PBW-degree induction yields
\[
Z\!\left(U_K(\mathfrak{sl}_2)\right)=K[\Omega].
\]
Consequently
\[
Z(S)=k[z^{\pm1},\Omega].
\]

Step 3: Produce the polynomial central generator in \(R\)
Since \(z\) is invertible in \(S\), we may replace \(\Omega\) by \(D=4z\Omega\). Expanding in the original generators gives
\[
D=4zH^2+8Hpq+4zH+16zFE-8p^2F+8q^2E-3z.
\]
Indeed, if
\[
X=2zE-p^2,\qquad Y=2zF+q^2,\qquad T=2zH+2pq-z,
\]
then \(X=2ze\), \(Y=2zf\), \(T=2zh\), and
\[
T^2+4zT+4YX=4z^2\Omega=zD.
\]
The displayed expansion shows \(D\in R\). Since \(D\) is central in \(S\) and \(R\hookrightarrow S\), it is central already in \(R\). Thus
\[
k[z,D]\subseteq Z(R).
\]

Step 4: Intersect the localized center back with \(R\)
Let \(c\in Z(R)\). From Step 2,
\[
c\in k[z^{\pm1},D].
\]
Write
\[
c=z^{-m}P(z,D),
\]
with \(m\ge0\), \(P\in k[z,D]\), and \(P\) not divisible by \(z\) if \(m>0\). If \(m>0\), then
\[
P(z,D)=z^m c\in zR.
\]
Reduce modulo \(z\). Since
\[
D\equiv 8(Hpq-p^2F+q^2E)\pmod z,
\]
its image in \(R/(z)\) is nonzero of positive PBW degree and therefore transcendental over \(k\). Hence \(P(0,D\bmod z)\neq0\), contradicting \(P(z,D)\in zR\). Thus \(m=0\), so \(c\in k[z,D]\).

Therefore
\[
Z(R)=k[z,D].
\]
Final Answer: $\boxed{k[z,4zH^2+8Hpq+4zH+16zFE-8p^2F+8q^2E-3z]}$
