## Steps

Step 1: Recognize the shifted Laplacian

Let
$$
s_1=(12),\qquad s_2=(23),\qquad s_3=(34),\qquad s_4=(45).
$$
Let $B$ be the adjacency matrix of the Cayley graph $\Gamma=\operatorname{Cay}(S_5,\{s_1,s_2,s_3,s_4\})$, so
$$
B_{\sigma,\tau}=1
$$
exactly when $\sigma^{-1}\tau\in\{s_1,s_2,s_3,s_4\}$. Since $\Gamma$ is $4$-regular, its graph Laplacian is $L=4I-B$. Hence the matrix in the problem is
$$
A=5I-B=I+L.
$$
In particular, $A$ is positive definite, so its determinant is positive.

Step 2: Decompose the regular representation

Right convolution by
$$
b=s_1+s_2+s_3+s_4\in\mathbb C[S_5]
$$
has matrix $B$ in the standard basis of the regular representation. The regular representation decomposes as
$$
\mathbb C[S_5]\cong\bigoplus_{\lambda\vdash5}(S^\lambda)^{\oplus f^\lambda},
$$
where $f^\lambda=\dim S^\lambda$. Therefore, if
$$
B_\lambda=\rho_\lambda(s_1)+\rho_\lambda(s_2)+\rho_\lambda(s_3)+\rho_\lambda(s_4),
$$
then
$$
\det A=\prod_{\lambda\vdash5}\det(5I_{f^\lambda}-B_\lambda)^{f^\lambda}.
$$
Unlike a class sum, $b$ is not central, so the blocks $B_\lambda$ are genuine matrices rather than scalars.

Step 3: Compute the irreducible block polynomials

Use Young's seminormal basis $\{v_T\}$ of $S^\lambda$, indexed by standard Young tableaux. If $c_T(k)$ is the content of the box containing $k$ and
$$
r_i(T)=c_T(i+1)-c_T(i),
$$
then for $s_i=(i,i+1)$,
$$
\rho_\lambda(s_i)v_T
=\frac1{r_i(T)}v_T
+\sqrt{1-\frac1{r_i(T)^2}}\,v_{s_iT}
$$
whenever $s_iT$ is standard. If $i,i+1$ lie in the same row or column, then $r_i(T)=1$ or $-1$ and the second term is absent. Thus each $B_\lambda$ is obtained explicitly from the standard tableaux of shape $\lambda$.

Writing
$$
p_\lambda(t)=\det(tI-B_\lambda),
$$
direct evaluation gives

| $\lambda$ | $f^\lambda$ | $p_\lambda(t)$ |
|---|---:|---|
| $(5)$ | $1$ | $t-4$ |
| $(4,1)$ | $4$ | $(t^2-5t+5)(t^2-3t+1)$ |
| $(3,2)$ | $5$ | $(t^2-2t-1)(t^3-2t^2-5t+4)$ |
| $(3,1,1)$ | $6$ | $t^2(t-1)(t+1)(t^2-5)$ |
| $(2,2,1)$ | $5$ | $(t^2+2t-1)(t^3+2t^2-5t-4)$ |
| $(2,1,1,1)$ | $4$ | $(t^2+3t+1)(t^2+5t+5)$ |
| $(1,1,1,1,1)$ | $1$ | $t+4$ |

As a consistency check, conjugating a partition tensors the representation by the sign character, so every $s_i$ changes sign; accordingly the polynomials for conjugate shapes are related by $B_{\lambda'}=-B_\lambda$.

Step 4: Evaluate the seven block determinants

Substituting $t=5$ gives

| $\lambda$ | $f^\lambda$ | $p_\lambda(5)$ |
|---|---:|---:|
| $(5)$ | $1$ | $1$ |
| $(4,1)$ | $4$ | $55$ |
| $(3,2)$ | $5$ | $756$ |
| $(3,1,1)$ | $6$ | $12000$ |
| $(2,2,1)$ | $5$ | $4964$ |
| $(2,1,1,1)$ | $4$ | $2255$ |
| $(1,1,1,1,1)$ | $1$ | $9$ |

Hence
$$
\det A
=55^4\,756^5\,12000^6\,4964^5\,2255^4\,9.
$$

Step 5: Collect prime powers

The needed factorizations are
$$
55=5\cdot11,\qquad
756=2^2 3^3\cdot7,\qquad
12000=2^5 3\cdot5^3,
$$
$$
4964=2^2\cdot17\cdot73,\qquad
2255=5\cdot11\cdot41,\qquad
9=3^2.
$$
Therefore
$$
\det A
=2^{50}3^{23}5^{26}7^5 11^8 17^5 41^4 73^5.
$$

Final Answer: $\boxed{2^{50}3^{23}5^{26}7^5 11^8 17^5 41^4 73^5}$

The matrix is $I+L$ for the canonical Cayley graph of $S_5$ generated by the adjacent transpositions, equivalently the one-skeleton of the type-$A_4$ permutahedron. The diagonal value $5$ is forced by the natural shift $I+L$ because the graph is $4$-regular. The hard part is genuinely structural: the adjacency element is noncentral, so one must analyze non-scalar irreducible blocks. No tuned cancellation, arbitrary parameter, or extra notation layer is introduced.
