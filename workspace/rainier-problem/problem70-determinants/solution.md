## Steps

Step 1: Determine the splitting field and its automorphisms

Let
$$
\alpha=2^{1/16},\qquad \zeta=\zeta_{16},\qquad F=\mathbb Q(\zeta).
$$
Then $L=F(\alpha)$ and
$$
\alpha^8=\sqrt2=\zeta^2+\zeta^{-2}\in F.
$$
We first show that $[L:F]=8$. If $\sqrt2$ were a square in $F$, then $F$ would contain $2^{1/4}$. But $F/\mathbb Q$ is abelian Galois, so every intermediate field is Galois over $\mathbb Q$, whereas $\mathbb Q(2^{1/4})/\mathbb Q$ is not Galois. Thus $\sqrt2$ is not a square in $F$.

Because $F$ contains all eighth roots of unity, $F(\alpha)/F$ is Galois: every root of $x^8-\sqrt2$ is $\xi\alpha$ with $\xi^8=1$. If its degree were a proper divisor $m<8$, its Galois group would be the unique order-$m$ subgroup of the cyclic group of eighth roots of unity, so $\alpha^m\in F$. Then
$$
\sqrt2=(\alpha^m)^{8/m}
$$
would be a square in $F$, a contradiction. Hence $[L:F]=8$, and therefore
$$
[L:\mathbb Q]=8\cdot\varphi(16)=64.
$$

An automorphism of $L$ is determined by
$$
\sigma_{a,u}(\alpha)=\zeta^a\alpha,
\qquad
\sigma_{a,u}(\zeta)=\zeta^u,
$$
where $u\in U=(\mathbb Z/16\mathbb Z)^\times$. Since
$$
\sigma_{a,u}(\alpha^8)=(-1)^a\sqrt2,
$$
while $u$ sends $\sqrt2$ to $\sqrt2$ for $u\equiv1,7\pmod8$ and to $-\sqrt2$ for $u\equiv3,5\pmod8$, the allowed pairs satisfy
$$
a\equiv
\begin{cases}
0\pmod2,&u\equiv1,7\pmod8,\\
1\pmod2,&u\equiv3,5\pmod8.
\end{cases}
$$
There are $64$ such pairs, so they are all automorphisms. Their multiplication is
$$
(a,u)(b,v)=(a+ub,uv).
$$
Let
$$
A=\{(2j,1):j\in\mathbb Z/8\mathbb Z\}.
$$
Then $A\cong C_8$ is the kernel of the restriction map $G=\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb Q)\to U$.

Step 2: Translate the field count into a subgroup count

By the Galois correspondence, $[E:\mathbb Q]=8$ is equivalent to
$$
|H|=8,
\qquad H=\operatorname{Gal}(L/E).
$$
Thus we count order-$8$ subgroups $H\le G$.

Put $d=|H\cap A|$. Since $A$ is cyclic of order $8$,
$$
d\in\{1,2,4,8\},
\qquad
|\pi(H)|=\frac8d,
$$
where $\pi:G\to U$ is restriction to $F$.

Step 3: Count the cases $d=8$ and $d=4$

If $d=8$, then $H=A$, giving one subgroup.

If $d=4$, then
$$
H\cap A=K_4=\{(0,1),(4,1),(8,1),(12,1)\}
$$
and $\pi(H)$ has order $2$. The three order-$2$ subgroups of $U$ are generated by $7,9,15$.

For any of these three values of $u$, an allowed lift is $(a,u)$ with $a$ even. Modulo $K_4$ there are exactly two possible lift cosets, represented by $a\equiv0$ or $2\pmod4$. Also
$$
(a,u)^2=(a(1+u),1)\in K_4,
$$
so both cosets produce an order-$8$ subgroup. Hence this case contributes
$$
3\cdot2=6.
$$

Step 4: Count the case $d=2$

Now
$$
H\cap A=K_2=\{(0,1),(8,1)\},
$$
and $\pi(H)$ has order $4$. The three order-$4$ subgroups of $U$ are
$$
B_3=\langle3\rangle,\qquad
B_5=\langle5\rangle,\qquad
B_V=\{1,7,9,15\}.
$$

For $B_3$, every lift $x=(a,3)$ has $a$ odd and
$$
x^4=(8,1).
$$
Modulo $K_2$ there are four such lift cosets, and each generates an order-$8$ subgroup. Thus $B_3$ contributes $4$.

For $B_5$, every lift $x=(a,5)$ has $a$ odd and
$$
x^4=(12a,1),
$$
which has order $4$. Hence $x$ has order $16$, so no order-$8$ subgroup can map onto $B_5$. This contributes $0$.

For $B_V$, choose lift cosets
$$
x=(a,7)K_2,\qquad y=(b,15)K_2,
$$
with $a,b\in\{0,2,4,6\}\pmod8$. Their images generate $B_V$, and $x^2=y^2=1$. They give a subgroup of order $8$ exactly when they commute modulo $K_2$. Since
$$
xy=(a+7b,9),\qquad yx=(b+15a,9),
$$
this is equivalent to
$$
a\equiv b\pmod4.
$$
Among the $4\cdot4=16$ pairs $(a,b)$, exactly $8$ satisfy this condition. Therefore the $d=2$ case contributes
$$
4+0+8=12.
$$

Step 5: Exclude $d=1$ and finish

If $d=1$, then $\pi(H)=U$, so $H$ contains a lift $(a,5)$ with $a$ odd. As above every such lift has order $16$, impossible inside an order-$8$ group. Hence this case contributes $0$.

The total number of order-$8$ subgroups, and therefore of degree-$8$ intermediate fields, is
$$
1+6+12=19.
$$

Final Answer: $\boxed{19}$
