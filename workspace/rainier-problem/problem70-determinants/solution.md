## Steps

Step 1: Determine the splitting field, its automorphisms, and conjugation

Let
$$
\alpha=2^{1/16},\qquad \zeta=\zeta_{16},\qquad F=\mathbb Q(\zeta).
$$
Then $L=F(\alpha)$ and
$$
\alpha^8=\sqrt2=\zeta^2+\zeta^{-2}\in F.
$$
As in the degree computation for this splitting field, $\sqrt2$ is not a square in $F$: otherwise $2^{1/4}\in F$, impossible because every subfield of the abelian Galois extension $F/\mathbb Q$ is Galois over $\mathbb Q$, while $\mathbb Q(2^{1/4})/\mathbb Q$ is not. Because $F$ contains all eighth roots of unity, $L/F$ is Galois: every root of $x^8-\sqrt2$ is $\xi\alpha$ with $\xi^8=1$. If $[L:F]=m<8$, then $\operatorname{Gal}(L/F)$ is the unique order-$m$ subgroup of the cyclic group of eighth roots of unity, so $\alpha^m\in F$. Since $m\in\{1,2,4\}$,
$$
\sqrt2=(\alpha^m)^{8/m}
$$
would be a square in $F$, a contradiction. Therefore $[L:F]=8$, and hence
$$
[L:\mathbb Q]=8\varphi(16)=64.
$$

Every automorphism is
$$
\sigma_{a,u}(\alpha)=\zeta^a\alpha,
\qquad
\sigma_{a,u}(\zeta)=\zeta^u,
$$
where $u\in U=(\mathbb Z/16\mathbb Z)^\times$ and
$$
a\equiv
\begin{cases}
0\pmod2,&u\equiv1,7\pmod8,\\
1\pmod2,&u\equiv3,5\pmod8.
\end{cases}
$$
Write such an automorphism as $(a,u)$. Then
$$
(a,u)(b,v)=(a+ub,uv).
$$
Let
$$
A=\{(2j,1):j\in\mathbb Z/8\mathbb Z\}\cong C_8.
$$
This is the kernel of the projection $\pi:G=\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb Q)\to U$. A direct calculation from the multiplication law gives
$$
(c,w)(a,u)(c,w)^{-1}=(wa+(1-u)c,u).
$$
In particular, conjugation preserves the $U$-coordinate.

Step 2: Translate $\mathbb Q$-isomorphism into subgroup conjugacy

For an intermediate field $E=L^H$, the condition $[E:\mathbb Q]=8$ is equivalent to $|H|=8$.

Two such fields $E_1=L^{H_1}$ and $E_2=L^{H_2}$ are $\mathbb Q$-isomorphic if and only if $H_1$ and $H_2$ are conjugate in $G$. Indeed, a $\mathbb Q$-isomorphism $E_1\to E_2$ extends to an automorphism of an algebraic closure; because $L/\mathbb Q$ is normal, that automorphism preserves $L$ and hence lies in $G$. Conversely, a $G$-conjugacy sends one fixed field to the other.

Thus we must count conjugacy classes of order-$8$ subgroups of $G$.

Step 3: Classify the order-$8$ subgroups

Put $d=|H\cap A|$. Since $A\cong C_8$,
$$
d\in\{1,2,4,8\},\qquad |\pi(H)|=\frac8d.
$$

If $d=8$, then $H=A$, giving one subgroup.

If $d=4$, then
$$
K_4=H\cap A=\{(0,1),(4,1),(8,1),(12,1)\}.
$$
The image $\pi(H)$ is one of the three order-$2$ subgroups generated by $u=7,9,15$. For each $u$, modulo $K_4$ there are two lift cosets, represented by $a\equiv0$ or $2\pmod4$. Hence there are $6$ subgroups in this case.

If $d=2$, then
$$
K_2=H\cap A=\{(0,1),(8,1)\}.
$$
The three possible order-$4$ images in $U$ are
$$
B_3=\langle3\rangle,\qquad B_5=\langle5\rangle,\qquad B_V=\{1,7,9,15\}.
$$
For $B_3$, the four odd lift classes $(a,3)$ modulo $K_2$ give four subgroups. For $B_5$, every lift $(a,5)$ has order $16$, so there are none. For $B_V$, write lift cosets as $(a,7)K_2$ and $(b,15)K_2$ with $a,b\in\{0,2,4,6\}\pmod8$. They commute modulo $K_2$ exactly when $a\equiv b\pmod4$, giving $8$ subgroups. Thus $d=2$ contributes $12$ subgroups.

If $d=1$, then $\pi(H)=U$, but $H$ would contain a lift of $5$, every such lift having order $16$. Hence this case is impossible.

So there are $1+6+12=19$ order-$8$ subgroups, but we still have to identify their conjugacy orbits.

Step 4: Compute the normalizers and orbit sizes

For a subgroup in the $d=4$ case with image generated by $u$ and lift parameter $a\pmod4$, the conjugation formula shows that $(c,w)$ normalizes it exactly when
$$
(w-1)a+(1-u)c\equiv0\pmod4.
$$
For $u=9$ this congruence is automatic, so both subgroups over $\langle9\rangle$ are normal and have normalizer size $64$. For $u=7$ or $15$, the condition reduces to $c$ even; exactly half of $G$ satisfies this, so each of the remaining four $d=4$ subgroups has normalizer size $32$.

For a $B_3$ subgroup in the $d=2$ case, represented by an odd $a\pmod8$, normalization is equivalent to
$$
(w-1)a-2c\equiv0\pmod8.
$$
Since $a$ is odd, compatibility with the parity rule for $c$ occurs exactly for $w\equiv1$ or $3\pmod8$. There are four such elements $w\in U$, and for each one the congruence has four solutions $c\pmod{16}$. Thus each of the four $B_3$ subgroups has normalizer size $16$.

For a $B_V$ subgroup represented by even $a,b\pmod8$ with $a\equiv b\pmod4$, normalization requires
$$
(w-1)a+2c\equiv0\pmod8,
\qquad
(w-1)b+2c\equiv0\pmod8.
$$
The two congruences are compatible because $a\equiv b\pmod4$. Their right-hand sides are multiples of $4$, so $c$ must be even; by the parity rule this means $w\equiv1$ or $7\pmod8$. There are four such $w\in U$, and for each one there are four solutions $c\pmod{16}$. Thus each of the eight $B_V$ subgroups also has normalizer size $16$.

Therefore the conjugacy-orbit sizes are:
- the subgroup $A$: orbit size $1$;
- the two subgroups over $\langle9\rangle$: orbit size $1$ each;
- the other four $d=4$ subgroups: orbit size $64/32=2$;
- all twelve $d=2$ subgroups: orbit size $64/16=4$.

Hence the number of conjugacy classes is
$$
1+2+\frac42+\frac{12}{4}=8.
$$

Step 5: Return to fields

By Step 2, these subgroup conjugacy classes are exactly the $\mathbb Q$-isomorphism classes of degree-$8$ intermediate fields of $L/\mathbb Q$.

Final Answer: $\boxed{8}$
