## Steps

Step 1: Classify the primitive norm representations
Let $r=\sqrt{-5}$ and $\mathcal O=\mathbb Z[r]$, the ring of integers of $\mathbb Q(r)$. Its norm is
$$
N(a+br)=a^2+5b^2.
$$
Set
$$
\pi=4+3r,\qquad \rho=3+4r.
$$
Then
$$
N(\pi)=61,\qquad N(\rho)=89.
$$
Because these norms are rational primes, $(\pi)$, $(\overline\pi)$, $(\rho)$, and $(\overline\rho)$ are prime ideals. Unique factorization of ideals in $\mathcal O$ shows that an element $\alpha=x+yr$ of norm $(61\cdot89)^m$ has
$$
(\alpha)=(\pi)^i(\overline\pi)^{m-i}(\rho)^j(\overline\rho)^{m-j}
$$
for unique integers $0\le i,j\le m$.

If $0<i<m$, then $(61)=(\pi)(\overline\pi)$ divides $(\alpha)$, so $61$ divides both $x$ and $y$. Thus primitivity forces $i\in\{0,m\}$, and the same argument with $89$ forces $j\in\{0,m\}$. Conversely, each of these four choices is primitive. Since the only units are $\pm1$, the primitive solutions are generated by the signs and conjugates of the two powers
$$
A^m,\qquad B^m,
$$
where, using
$$
\pi\rho=-48+25r,\qquad \pi\overline\rho=72-7r,
$$
we may take
$$
A=48+25r,\qquad B=72+7r.
$$
For every $m\ge1$, the $A$-family contributes four solutions and the $B$-family contributes four solutions.

Step 2: Prove the coefficient-valuation lemma
Let $z=a+br$ with $a$ even and $b$ odd, and write
$$
z^m=X_m+Y_mr.
$$
Expanding by the number $\ell$ of factors equal to $a$ gives
$$
Y_m=
\sum_{\substack{0\le\ell\le m\\m-\ell\text{ odd}}}
\binom m\ell a^\ell b^{m-\ell}(-5)^{(m-\ell-1)/2}.
$$
If $m$ is odd, the term with $\ell=0$ is odd, while every other admissible term has $\ell\ge2$ and is even. Hence
$$
v_2(Y_m)=0\qquad(m\text{ odd}).
$$

Now let $m$ be even, put $s=v_2(m)$ and $t=v_2(a)$. The admissible indices $\ell$ are odd. The term with $\ell=1$ has valuation $s+t$. For every odd $\ell$,
$$
\binom m\ell=\frac m\ell\binom{m-1}{\ell-1},
$$
so $v_2\!\left(\binom m\ell\right)\ge s$. Every term with $\ell\ge3$ therefore has valuation at least $s+3t>s+t$. The $\ell=1$ term is the unique term of least valuation, and consequently
$$
v_2(Y_m)=v_2(m)+v_2(a)\qquad(m\text{ even}).
$$

Step 3: Apply the lemma to the two primitive families
For $A=48+25r$, the real coefficient has valuation $v_2(48)=4$ and the $r$-coefficient is odd. For $B=72+7r$, these values are $v_2(72)=3$ and odd, respectively. Since $k\ge5$, only even exponents can contribute, and Step 2 gives
$$
v_2([r]A^m)=v_2(m)+4,
\qquad
v_2([r]B^m)=v_2(m)+3,
$$
where $[r]z$ denotes the coefficient of $r$ in $z$.

Step 4: Determine the contributing exponent classes
Recall that $q=2^{k-4}$. The $A$-family has coefficient valuation $k$ exactly when
$$
v_2(m)=k-4,
$$
that is,
$$
m=q(2h+1)\qquad(h\ge0).
$$
Each such exponent contributes four primitive pairs. The $B$-family has coefficient valuation $k$ exactly when
$$
v_2(m)=k-3,
$$
that is,
$$
m=2q(2h+1)\qquad(h\ge0),
$$
and each such exponent also contributes four primitive pairs. These two exponent classes are disjoint and exhaustive.

Step 5: Sum the two geometric series
Therefore
$$
C_k(T)
=4\sum_{h\ge0}T^{q(2h+1)}
+4\sum_{h\ge0}T^{2q(2h+1)}
=\frac{4T^q}{1-T^{2q}}+\frac{4T^{2q}}{1-T^{4q}}.
$$
Putting the two terms over the denominator $1-T^{4q}$ yields
$$
C_k(T)=\frac{4T^q(1+T^q+T^{2q})}{1-T^{4q}}.
$$
For the boundary value $k=5$, one has $q=2$: the first coefficients occur at exponents $2,4,6$ modulo $8$, matching the valuations of the $A$- and $B$-families from Step 3.

Final Answer: $\boxed{\frac{4T^q(1+T^q+T^{2q})}{1-T^{4q}}}$
