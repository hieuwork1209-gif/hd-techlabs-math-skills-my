## Steps

Step 1: Identify the nonprincipal split prime ideals
Let $r=\sqrt{-14}$ and $\mathcal O=\mathbb Z[r]$. Its norm is
$$
N(a+br)=a^2+14b^2.
$$
Put
$$
P=(3,r-1),\qquad Q=(5,r+1).
$$
Then
$$
(3)=P\overline P,\qquad (5)=Q\overline Q.
$$
The element $1-r$ has norm $15$ and lies in both $P$ and $\overline Q$, so norm comparison gives
$$
P\overline Q=(1-r).
$$
Hence $[P]=[Q]$ in the ideal class group; denote this common class by $g$.

The ideal $PQ$ is not principal. Indeed, a generator would have norm $15$, but the only solutions of $x^2+14y^2=15$ are $x=\pm1$, $y=\pm1$. Membership in $P$ requires $x+y\equiv0\pmod3$, while membership in $Q$ requires $x-y\equiv0\pmod5$, and none of these four elements satisfies both conditions.

On the other hand, $1-4r$ has norm $225$. It lies in $P$ and $Q$, but not in their conjugates, so its ideal factorization must be
$$
(1-4r)=P^2Q^2.
$$
Thus $g^2\ne1$ while $g^4=1$. In particular, $g$ has order $4$.

Step 2: Classify the primitive representations
Let $\alpha=x+yr$ satisfy $N(\alpha)=15^m$. Unique factorization of ideals gives
$$
(\alpha)=P^a\overline P^{m-a}Q^b\overline Q^{m-b}
$$
for some $0\le a,b\le m$.

If both $P$ and $\overline P$ occur, then $(3)$ divides $(\alpha)$, so $3$ divides both $x$ and $y$. Thus primitivity forces $a\in\{0,m\}$; similarly $b\in\{0,m\}$.

The cross choices are always principal because
$$
P^m\overline Q^m=(1-r)^m,
$$
and its conjugate gives the other cross choice. The same-side choice $P^mQ^m$ has class $g^{2m}$, so it is principal exactly when $m$ is even. If $m=2n$, then
$$
P^mQ^m=(1-4r)^n.
$$
Therefore every $m$ has one cross family, generated up to sign and conjugation by $(1-r)^m$, contributing four primitive pairs. When $m$ is even there is one additional same-side family, generated by $(1-4r)^{m/2}$, also contributing four primitive pairs.

Step 3: Prove the $2$-adic Lucas lemma
Let $z$ be either $1-r$ or $1-4r$, and put $M=N(z)$, so $M$ is odd and $z+\overline z=2$. Define
$$
U_n=\frac{z^n-\overline z^n}{z-\overline z},\qquad
V_n=z^n+\overline z^n.
$$
Then
$$
U_0=0,\quad U_1=1,\quad U_{n+2}=2U_{n+1}-MU_n,
$$
and
$$
V_0=V_1=2,\quad V_{n+2}=2V_{n+1}-MV_n.
$$
Modulo $2$, the recurrence for $U_n$ gives $U_{n+2}\equiv U_n$, hence $U_n$ is odd exactly when $n$ is odd. Modulo $4$, induction gives
$$
V_n\equiv2\pmod4
$$
for every $n$, because $M$ is odd. Thus $v_2(V_n)=1$.

Using
$$
U_{2n}=U_nV_n,
$$
write $n=2^st$ with $t$ odd. Repeated doubling gives
$$
v_2(U_n)=s=v_2(n).
$$

Step 4: Apply the lemma to the two primitive families
Write
$$
(1-r)^m=X_m+Y_mr.
$$
Since $1-r-(1+r)=-2r$, the definition of $U_m$ gives $Y_m=-U_m$. Hence
$$
v_2(Y_m)=v_2(m).
$$

For the same-side family write
$$
(1-4r)^n=R_n+S_nr.
$$
Now $(1-4r)-(1+4r)=-8r$, so $S_n=-4U_n$. Therefore
$$
v_2(S_n)=2+v_2(n).
$$
If $m=2n$, this becomes
$$
v_2(S_{m/2})=1+v_2(m).
$$

Step 5: Determine the exponent classes and sum
Fix $k\ge2$ and recall $q=2^{k-1}$. The cross family has $v_2(y)=k$ exactly when
$$
v_2(m)=k,
$$
so
$$
m=2q(2h+1)\qquad(h\ge0).
$$
The same-side family exists only for even $m$ and has $v_2(y)=k$ exactly when
$$
1+v_2(m)=k,
$$
so
$$
m=q(2h+1)\qquad(h\ge0).
$$
Each contributing family supplies four primitive pairs. Consequently
$$
C_k(T)=4\sum_{h\ge0}T^{q(2h+1)}+4\sum_{h\ge0}T^{2q(2h+1)}
=\frac{4T^q}{1-T^{2q}}+\frac{4T^{2q}}{1-T^{4q}}.
$$
Combining the fractions gives
$$
C_k(T)=\frac{4T^q(1+T^q+T^{2q})}{1-T^{4q}}.
$$
For $k=2$, $q=2$: the same-side family contributes at $m\equiv2\pmod4$, while the cross family contributes at $m\equiv4\pmod8$, agreeing with the first primitive representations of $15^m$.

Final Answer: $\boxed{\frac{4T^q(1+T^q+T^{2q})}{1-T^{4q}}}$
