## Steps

Step 1: Normalize the quadratic fields and record the transpose signs

Fix a nonsquare $d\in\mathbb F_p^\times$. If $E\subset M_8(\mathbb F_p)$ is a unital field of order $p^2$, then $W=\mathbb F_p^8$ has dimension $4$ over $E$. Hence for $x\in E$, the matrix trace is
$$
4\operatorname{Tr}_{E/\mathbb F_p}(x).
$$
Since $p$ is odd, the matrix-trace-zero part of $E$ is exactly its one-dimensional field-trace-zero line.

If $0\ne X$ lies on that line, then $X^2=aI_8$ for a nonsquare $a$. Since $d/a$ is a square, the line contains exactly two matrices $\pm X_E$ satisfying
$$
X_E^2=dI_8.
$$
Conversely, every $X$ with $X^2=dI_8$ has minimal polynomial $t^2-d$, hence generates a quadratic field and has trace zero.

If $E$ is stable under transpose, then transpose restricts to an $\mathbb F_p$-automorphism of the commutative field $E$. Therefore on its trace-zero line it acts by a sign. Thus the desired field sextuples correspond, with multiplicity $2^6$, to normalized sextuples $(X_1,\dots,X_6)$ satisfying
$$
X_i^2=dI_8,\qquad X_iX_j=-X_jX_i\quad(i\ne j),
$$
and
$$
X_i^T=\varepsilon_iX_i,\qquad \varepsilon_i\in\{\pm1\}.
$$
We must determine which sign patterns $\varepsilon=(\varepsilon_1,\dots,\varepsilon_6)$ can occur for the ordinary transpose involution.

Step 2: Identify the six-generator Clifford algebra with the full matrix algebra

Let $C$ be the $\mathbb F_p$-algebra generated by $e_1,\dots,e_6$ with
$$
e_i^2=d,\qquad e_ie_j=-e_je_i\quad(i\ne j).
$$
By repeatedly moving generators into increasing order and replacing $e_i^2$ by $d$, every word is a scalar multiple of an ordered squarefree monomial
$$
e_S=e_{i_1}\cdots e_{i_k}\qquad(i_1<\cdots<i_k).
$$
Thus $C$ is spanned by the $2^6=64$ elements $e_S$, so $\dim_{\mathbb F_p}C\le64$.

We now construct a representation whose image is all of $M_8(\mathbb F_p)$. For every $q\in\mathbb F_p^\times$ there are $A,B\in M_2(\mathbb F_p)$ such that
$$
A^2=B^2=qI_2,\qquad AB=-BA,
$$
and $A,B$ generate $M_2(\mathbb F_p)$. If $q=r^2$, take
$$
A=r\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
B=r\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.
$$
If $q$ is nonsquare, write $\mathbb F_{p^2}=\mathbb F_p(\sqrt q)$. Its multiplicative group is cyclic of order $p^2-1$, and the norm is $x\mapsto x^{p+1}$, whose image therefore has size $(p^2-1)/(p+1)=p-1$; hence the norm map onto $\mathbb F_p^\times$ is surjective. Choose $a,c\in\mathbb F_p$ with
$$
a^2-qc^2=q
$$
and take
$$
A=\begin{pmatrix}0&q\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
B=\begin{pmatrix}a&-qc\\c&-a\end{pmatrix}.
$$
A direct multiplication gives $A^2=B^2=qI_2$ and $AB=-BA$. Also $A$ and $B$ are noncommuting, so the unital subalgebra they generate cannot be a proper commutative subalgebra of $M_2$; equivalently, $I,A,B,AB$ are linearly independent and form a basis of $M_2$.

Choose anticommuting $U,V\in M_2(\mathbb F_p)$ with square $dI_2$. For the symplectic involution adjoint to
$$
J=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix},
$$
every trace-zero $2\times2$ matrix is skew-adjoint, so $U$ and $V$ are skew-adjoint. Choose anticommuting $A,B\in M_2(\mathbb F_p)$ with square $-d^{-1}I_2$. The anti-automorphism that fixes $A$ and $B$ is an orthogonal involution, hence is adjoint to a nondegenerate symmetric matrix $H$.

Put $P=UV$ and define in $M_4=M_2\otimes M_2$
$$
Y_1=U\otimes I_2,\quad Y_2=V\otimes I_2,
$$
$$
Y_3=P\otimes A,\quad Y_4=P\otimes B.
$$
These four matrices square to $dI_4$ and pairwise anticommute. They also generate all of $M_4$: $Y_1,Y_2$ generate $M_2\otimes I_2$; since $P=UV$ is invertible and belongs to that subalgebra, multiplying $Y_3$ and $Y_4$ by $P^{-1}\otimes I_2$ gives $I_2\otimes A$ and $I_2\otimes B$. Because $A,B$ generate the second $M_2$ factor, the generated algebra contains both $M_2\otimes I_2$ and $I_2\otimes M_2$, hence is $M_4$.

The $Y_i$ are skew-adjoint for the alternating form with matrix
$$
H_4=J\otimes H.
$$
Let
$$
S=Y_1Y_2Y_3Y_4.
$$
Reversing four pairwise anticommuting factors contributes $(-1)^6=1$, while each $Y_i$ is skew-adjoint, so $S$ is self-adjoint. Also $S$ anticommutes with every $Y_i$ and satisfies $S^2=d^4I_4$.

Choose anticommuting $C_0,D_0\in M_2(\mathbb F_p)$ with square $d^{-3}I_2$, skew-adjoint for $J$, and define in $M_8=M_4\otimes M_2$
$$
Z_i=Y_i\otimes I_2\quad(1\le i\le4),
$$
$$
Z_5=S\otimes C_0,\qquad Z_6=S\otimes D_0.
$$
Again the six $Z_i$ square to $dI_8$ and pairwise anticommute. More importantly, they generate the full matrix algebra directly: $Z_1,\dots,Z_4$ generate $M_4\otimes I_2$; $S$ is invertible and lies in this first factor, so multiplying $Z_5,Z_6$ by $S^{-1}\otimes I_2$ produces $I_4\otimes C_0$ and $I_4\otimes D_0$. Since $C_0,D_0$ generate $M_2$, the six $Z_i$ generate
$$
(M_4\otimes I_2)(I_4\otimes M_2)=M_4\otimes M_2=M_8(\mathbb F_p).
$$

Therefore the homomorphism
$$
\rho:C\longrightarrow M_8(\mathbb F_p),\qquad e_i\longmapsto Z_i,
$$
is surjective. Since $\dim M_8=64$ while $C$ is spanned by $64$ squarefree monomials, surjectivity forces
$$
64\le\dim C\le64.
$$
Thus $\rho$ is an isomorphism, not merely a representation:
$$
C\cong M_8(\mathbb F_p).
$$

Let $\tau_-$ be the involution of $C$ with $\tau_-(e_i)=-e_i$. Under the above realization all six $Z_i$ are skew-adjoint for the symmetric form
$$
H_8=H_4\otimes J,
$$
so $\tau_-$ is adjoint to $H_8$. Its determinant is a square because
$$
\det(H_8)=\det(H_4)^2\det(J)^4.
$$

Step 3: Classify the transpose sign patterns using involutions of $M_8$

We use the standard classification in the precise form needed here. Let $F$ be a field of characteristic not $2$. Every involution of the first kind on the split central simple algebra $M_n(F)$ is
$$
\sigma_H(X)=H^{-1}X^TH
$$
for a nonsingular matrix $H$ satisfying $H^T=\delta H$ with $\delta\in\{1,-1\}$. The case $\delta=1$ is an orthogonal involution, adjoint to a nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form; the case $\delta=-1$ is symplectic, adjoint to a nondegenerate alternating form. Two orthogonal involutions $\sigma_H$ and $\sigma_K$ are conjugate by an $F$-algebra automorphism of $M_n(F)$ exactly when the symmetric forms are similar, i.e. when
$$
K=\lambda G^THG
$$
for some $G\in\mathrm{GL}_n(F)$ and $\lambda\in F^\times$.

For $F=\mathbb F_p$ with $p$ odd, nondegenerate symmetric forms of a fixed dimension are classified by the square class of their determinant. In dimension $2m$, the split (hyperbolic, or $+$) class has determinant square class $(-1)^m$. Here $n=8=2\cdot4$, so the split $+$ class is exactly the square-determinant class. In particular $I_8$ is split, and its adjoint involution is ordinary transpose. These hypotheses apply here because $p$ is odd, Step 2 identifies $C$ with the split algebra $M_8(\mathbb F_p)$, and every $\tau_\varepsilon$ below fixes the center $\mathbb F_p$.

For a sign pattern $\varepsilon$, define the involution $\tau_\varepsilon$ of $C$ by
$$
\tau_\varepsilon(e_i)=\varepsilon_i e_i.
$$
Let $r$ be the number of indices with $\varepsilon_i=-1$. For an ordered monomial $e_S$ with $|S|=k$, reversal gives
$$
\tau_\varepsilon(e_S)
=(-1)^{k(k-1)/2}\left(\prod_{i\in S}\varepsilon_i\right)e_S.
$$
If
$$
c_k(r)=[t^k](1-t)^r(1+t)^{6-r},
$$
then, because the reversal signs for $k=0,1,\dots,6$ are
$$
+,+,-,-,+,+,-,
$$
we have the reproducible trace formula
$$
\operatorname{Tr}(\tau_\varepsilon)
=\sum_{k=0}^6(-1)^{k(k-1)/2}c_k(r).
$$
Evaluating it gives
$$
\begin{array}{c|ccccccc}
r&0&1&2&3&4&5&6\\ \hline
\operatorname{Tr}(\tau_\varepsilon)&-8&-8&8&8&-8&-8&8.
\end{array}
$$

For $M_8$, an orthogonal involution has fixed-space dimension $8\cdot9/2=36$, whereas a symplectic involution has fixed-space dimension $8\cdot7/2=28$. Since an involution has only eigenvalues $\pm1$ in odd characteristic,
$$
\dim\operatorname{Fix}(\tau_\varepsilon)
=\frac{64+\operatorname{Tr}(\tau_\varepsilon)}2.
$$
Therefore $\tau_\varepsilon$ is orthogonal exactly for
$$
r=2,3,6.
$$

It remains to determine the orthogonal type. The case $r=6$ is $\tau_-$. By Step 2 its Gram matrix $H_8$ has square determinant, so the finite-field classification just stated shows that it is split $+$ and hence conjugate to transpose.

For $r=2$, let $T$ be the set of the four positive-sign indices and put $u=\prod_{i\in T}e_i$. For $r=3$, let $T$ be the set of the three negative-sign indices and use the same definition. The anticommutation relations give, in both cases,
$$
\tau_\varepsilon=\operatorname{Int}(u)\circ\tau_-,
\qquad
\tau_-(u)=u.
$$
If $\rho$ is the split realization from Step 2 and $U=\rho(u)$, then
$$
\tau_-(X)=H_8^{-1}X^TH_8
$$
implies
$$
\tau_\varepsilon(X)=(H_8U^{-1})^{-1}X^T(H_8U^{-1}).
$$
The relation $\tau_-(u)=u$ makes $H_8U^{-1}$ symmetric, so this is indeed the Gram matrix of the twisted orthogonal involution.

Each generator has characteristic polynomial $(t^2-d)^4$, hence
$$
\det\rho(e_i)=d^4,
$$
which is a square in $\mathbb F_p^\times$. Thus $\det U$ is a square and
$$
\det(H_8U^{-1})\equiv\det(H_8)\pmod{(\mathbb F_p^\times)^2}.
$$
So the twists for $r=2$ and $r=3$ also have square determinant. By the classification theorem above, every orthogonal $\tau_\varepsilon$ with $r=2,3,6$ is split $+$ and is conjugate to ordinary transpose. No other sign pattern can be conjugate to transpose because the remaining involutions are symplectic.

Consequently the admissible sign patterns are exactly
$$
\binom62+\binom63+\binom66=15+20+1=36.
$$

Step 4: Count normalized sextuples for one admissible sign pattern

Fix one admissible $\varepsilon$ and one intertwining isomorphism
$$
\rho_0:(C,\tau_\varepsilon)\longrightarrow(M_8(\mathbb F_p),T).
$$
If $\rho$ is any other $\mathbb F_p$-algebra isomorphism $C\to M_8$, then $\rho\rho_0^{-1}$ is an $\mathbb F_p$-algebra automorphism of $M_8$. By Skolem-Noether in the full-matrix case, every such automorphism is inner, so
$$
\rho=\operatorname{Ad}_g\circ\rho_0
$$
for some $g\in\mathrm{GL}_8(\mathbb F_p)$. The matrix $g$ is unique up to multiplication by a nonzero scalar because the centralizer of all of $M_8$ is its scalar center.

Because $\rho_0$ already intertwines $\tau_\varepsilon$ with transpose, $\rho$ does so exactly when
$$
T\circ\operatorname{Ad}_g=\operatorname{Ad}_g\circ T.
$$
Now
$$
T\circ\operatorname{Ad}_g=\operatorname{Ad}_{(g^{-1})^T}\circ T,
$$
so equality holds exactly when the two inner automorphisms agree, equivalently when $(g^{-1})^T$ is a scalar multiple of $g$. Thus
$$
g^Tg=\lambda I_8
$$
for some $\lambda\in\mathbb F_p^\times$. Therefore the distinct normalized sextuples with this sign pattern are parametrized by projective orthogonal similitudes
$$
\mathrm{PGO}_8^+(\mathbb F_p)
=\mathrm{GO}_8^+(\mathbb F_p)/(\mathbb F_p^\times I_8).
$$

We now justify the order of this projective group, including the multiplier factor. For a split $8$-dimensional form, choose a hyperbolic basis with Gram matrix
$$
J_8=\begin{pmatrix}0&I_4\\I_4&0\end{pmatrix}.
$$
For every $\lambda\in\mathbb F_p^\times$,
$$
g_\lambda=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda I_4&0\\0&I_4\end{pmatrix}
$$
satisfies
$$
g_\lambda^TJ_8g_\lambda=\lambda J_8.
$$
Hence the multiplier map
$$
\mu:\mathrm{GO}_8^+(\mathbb F_p)\to\mathbb F_p^\times
$$
is surjective, with kernel $\mathrm O_8^+(\mathbb F_p)$. Therefore
$$
|\mathrm{GO}_8^+(\mathbb F_p)|=(p-1)|\mathrm O_8^+(\mathbb F_p)|.
$$
The scalar subgroup $\mathbb F_p^\times I_8$ also has order $p-1$, so
$$
|\mathrm{PGO}_8^+(\mathbb F_p)|
=\frac{|\mathrm{GO}_8^+(\mathbb F_p)|}{p-1}
=|\mathrm O_8^+(\mathbb F_p)|.
$$
This is the full projective similitude group, not merely $\mathrm O_8^+/\{\pm I\}$; the nonsquare-multiplier projective coset supplies the factor that would otherwise be missed.

Hence the total number of normalized sextuples over all $36$ admissible sign patterns is
$$
36|\mathrm O_8^+(\mathbb F_p)|.
$$

Step 5: Evaluate the orthogonal-group order and divide by generator signs

For a split $2n$-dimensional quadratic space over $\mathbb F_p$ with $p$ odd, counting ordered hyperbolic bases gives the exact order
$$
|\mathrm O_{2n}^+(\mathbb F_p)|
=2p^{n(n-1)}(p^n-1)\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}(p^{2i}-1).
$$
With $n=4$ this becomes
$$
|\mathrm O_8^+(\mathbb F_p)|
=2p^{12}(p^2-1)(p^4-1)^2(p^6-1).
$$

Finally, each ordered field sextuple gives exactly $2^6=64$ normalized generator sextuples, because each trace-zero line contains exactly the two generators $\pm X_i$ with square $dI_8$. Therefore the required number of ordered field sextuples is
$$
\frac{36}{64}|\mathrm O_8^+(\mathbb F_p)|
=\frac{9p^{12}(p^2-1)(p^4-1)^2(p^6-1)}8.
$$

Final Answer: $\boxed{\frac{9p^{12}(p^2-1)(p^4-1)^2(p^6-1)}8}$
