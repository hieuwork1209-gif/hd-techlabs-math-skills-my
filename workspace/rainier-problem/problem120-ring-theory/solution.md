## Steps

Step 1: Normalize the trace-zero generator of each quadratic field

Fix a nonsquare $d\in\mathbb F_p^\times$. Let $E\subset M_4(\mathbb F_p)$ be a unital field of order $p^2$. The natural $E$-action on $W=\mathbb F_p^4$ makes $W$ a two-dimensional vector space over $E$. Hence for $x\in E$, the matrix trace of multiplication by $x$ on $W$ is twice the field trace $\operatorname{Tr}_{E/\mathbb F_p}(x)$.

Therefore the trace-zero part of $E$ is a one-dimensional $\mathbb F_p$-subspace. If $0\ne X$ lies on that line, then its minimal polynomial is $t^2-a$ with $a$ a nonsquare, so $X^2=aI_4$. Since $d/a$ is a square, the line contains exactly two matrices $\pm X_E$ satisfying
$$
X_E^2=dI_4.
$$
Conversely, if $X^2=dI_4$, then $t^2-d$ is irreducible and $\mathbb F_p[I_4,X]$ is a field of order $p^2$; its characteristic polynomial is $(t^2-d)^2$, so $\operatorname{tr}(X)=0$.

Thus the desired field quadruples are obtained from ordered quadruples $(X_1,X_2,X_3,X_4)$ satisfying
$$
X_i^2=dI_4,\qquad X_iX_j=-X_jX_i\quad(i\ne j),
$$
by dividing the number of normalized generator quadruples by $2^4=16$.

Step 2: Build the universal four-generator algebra

Let $C$ be the $\mathbb F_p$-algebra generated by $e_1,e_2,e_3,e_4$ with relations
$$
e_i^2=d,\qquad e_ie_j=-e_je_i\quad(i\ne j).
$$
Reordering words and replacing $e_i^2$ by $d$ shows that $C$ is spanned by the $16$ monomials
$$
e_{i_1}\cdots e_{i_r}\qquad(0\le r\le4,\ i_1<\cdots<i_r),
$$
so $\dim_{\mathbb F_p}C\le16$.

We now construct a surjection $C\to M_4(\mathbb F_p)$. We use the following elementary fact: for every $q\in\mathbb F_p^\times$ there exist $A,B\in M_2(\mathbb F_p)$ such that
$$
A^2=B^2=qI_2,\qquad AB=-BA,
$$
and $A,B$ generate $M_2(\mathbb F_p)$.

If $q=r^2$ is a square, take
$$
A=r\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
B=r\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.
$$
If $q$ is a nonsquare, let $L=\mathbb F_p(\sqrt q)$. The norm map $L^\times\to\mathbb F_p^\times$ is onto because $L^\times$ is cyclic of order $p^2-1$ and the norm is $x\mapsto x^{p+1}$. Hence choose $a,c\in\mathbb F_p$ with $a^2-qc^2=q$, and put
$$
A=\begin{pmatrix}0&q\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
B=\begin{pmatrix}a&-qc\\c&-a\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Then $A^2=B^2=qI_2$ and $AB=-BA$. In either case the four matrices $I_2,A,B,AB$ are linearly independent, so $A,B$ generate $M_2(\mathbb F_p)$.

Step 3: Identify $C$ explicitly with $M_4(\mathbb F_p)$

Apply Step 2 first with $q=d$, obtaining $U,V\in M_2(\mathbb F_p)$, and then with
$$
q=-\frac1d,
$$
obtaining $A,B\in M_2(\mathbb F_p)$. In
$$
M_4(\mathbb F_p)\cong M_2(\mathbb F_p)\otimes M_2(\mathbb F_p)
$$
set
$$
Y_1=U\otimes I_2,\qquad
Y_2=V\otimes I_2,\qquad
Y_3=UV\otimes A,\qquad
Y_4=UV\otimes B.
$$
Because $(UV)^2=-d^2I_2$, one has
$$
Y_i^2=dI_4
$$
for all $i$, and the four matrices pairwise anticommute. Therefore $e_i\mapsto Y_i$ defines a homomorphism
$$
C\longrightarrow M_4(\mathbb F_p).
$$
The matrices $Y_1,Y_2$ generate $M_2(\mathbb F_p)\otimes I_2$. Since $UV$ is invertible, multiplying $Y_3,Y_4$ by $(UV)^{-1}\otimes I_2$ gives $I_2\otimes A$ and $I_2\otimes B$, which generate $I_2\otimes M_2(\mathbb F_p)$. Hence the image is all of $M_4(\mathbb F_p)$.

The target has dimension $16$ over $\mathbb F_p$ and $\dim C\le16$, so
$$
C\cong M_4(\mathbb F_p).
$$

Step 4: Classify all normalized quadruples by conjugation

A normalized quadruple $(X_1,X_2,X_3,X_4)$ gives a unital homomorphism
$$
\rho:C\to M_4(\mathbb F_p),\qquad e_i\mapsto X_i.
$$
Since $C\cong M_4(\mathbb F_p)$ is simple, $\rho$ is injective. Both source and target have dimension $16$, so $\rho$ is an isomorphism.

It remains to see directly that all such representations are conjugate. Let $f_{ij}=\rho(E_{ij})$, where $E_{ij}$ are the standard matrix units. Choose $0\ne v\in f_{11}W$ and set
$$
v_i=f_{i1}v\qquad(1\le i\le4).
$$
Then $f_{ij}v_k=\delta_{jk}v_i$, so $v_1,v_2,v_3,v_4$ form a basis of $W$. In this basis the $f_{ij}$ are the standard matrix units. Thus every normalized quadruple is conjugate to the explicit one from Step 3.

The stabilizer of one normalized quadruple consists of invertible matrices commuting with all four generators. Since those generators generate all of $M_4(\mathbb F_p)$, the stabilizer is exactly the scalar group $\mathbb F_p^\times$, of size $p-1$.

Step 5: Apply orbit-stabilizer and divide by the sign choices

Therefore the number of normalized ordered generator quadruples is
$$
\frac{|\mathrm{GL}_4(\mathbb F_p)|}{p-1}.
$$
Each ordered field quadruple has exactly $2^4=16$ normalized generator quadruples, so the required number is
$$
\frac{|\mathrm{GL}_4(\mathbb F_p)|}{16(p-1)}.
$$
Using
$$
|\mathrm{GL}_4(\mathbb F_p)|
=p^6(p-1)^4(p+1)^2(p^2+1)(p^2+p+1),
$$
we obtain
$$
\frac{p^6(p-1)^3(p+1)^2(p^2+1)(p^2+p+1)}{16}.
$$

Final Answer: $\boxed{\frac{p^6(p-1)^3(p+1)^2(p^2+1)(p^2+p+1)}{16}}$
