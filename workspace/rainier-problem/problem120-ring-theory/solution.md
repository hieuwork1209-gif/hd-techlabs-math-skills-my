## Steps

Step 1: Normalize the quadratic fields and record the transpose signs

Fix a nonsquare $d\in\mathbb F_p^\times$. If $E\subset M_8(\mathbb F_p)$ is a unital field of order $p^2$, then $W=\mathbb F_p^8$ has dimension $4$ over $E$. Hence for $x\in E$, the matrix trace is
$$
4\operatorname{Tr}_{E/\mathbb F_p}(x).
$$
Since $p$ is odd, the matrix-trace-zero part of $E$ is exactly its one-dimensional field-trace-zero line.

If $0\ne X$ lies on that line, then $X^2=aI_8$ for a nonsquare $a$. Since $d/a$ is a square, the line contains exactly two matrices $\pm X_E$ satisfying
$$
X_E^2=dI_8.
$$
Conversely, every $X$ with $X^2=dI_8$ generates a quadratic field and has trace zero.

If $E$ is stable under transpose, then transpose restricts to an $\mathbb F_p$-automorphism of $E$. Therefore on its trace-zero line it acts by a sign. Thus the desired field sextuples correspond, with multiplicity $2^6$, to normalized sextuples $(X_1,\dots,X_6)$ satisfying
$$
X_i^2=dI_8,\qquad X_iX_j=-X_jX_i\quad(i\ne j),
$$
and
$$
X_i^T=\varepsilon_iX_i,\qquad \varepsilon_i\in\{\pm1\}.
$$
We must determine which sign patterns $\varepsilon=(\varepsilon_1,\dots,\varepsilon_6)$ can occur for the ordinary transpose involution.

Step 2: Identify the six-generator Clifford algebra and one split orthogonal involution

Let $C$ be generated by $e_1,\dots,e_6$ with
$$
e_i^2=d,\qquad e_ie_j=-e_je_i\quad(i\ne j).
$$
It is spanned by the $64$ ordered squarefree monomials. We first realize it as $M_8(\mathbb F_p)$ together with the involution
$$
\tau_-(e_i)=-e_i.
$$

For every $q\in\mathbb F_p^\times$ there exist $A,B\in M_2(\mathbb F_p)$ such that
$$
A^2=B^2=qI_2,\qquad AB=-BA,
$$
and $A,B$ generate $M_2(\mathbb F_p)$. If $q=r^2$, use
$$
A=r\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
B=r\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.
$$
If $q$ is nonsquare, choose $a,c\in\mathbb F_p$ with $a^2-qc^2=q$, possible because the norm map from $\mathbb F_{p^2}^\times$ is onto, and take
$$
A=\begin{pmatrix}0&q\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
B=\begin{pmatrix}a&-qc\\c&-a\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Choose $U,V\in M_2(\mathbb F_p)$ with square $dI_2$. For the symplectic involution with matrix $J$, every trace-zero matrix is skew-adjoint, so $U,V$ are skew-adjoint. Choose $A,B\in M_2(\mathbb F_p)$ with square $-d^{-1}I_2$. The anti-automorphism fixing $A$ and $B$ is an orthogonal involution, adjoint to some nondegenerate symmetric matrix $H$.

Put $P=UV$ and define in $M_4=M_2\otimes M_2$
$$
Y_1=U\otimes I_2,\quad Y_2=V\otimes I_2,
$$
$$
Y_3=P\otimes A,\quad Y_4=P\otimes B.
$$
These four matrices square to $dI_4$, pairwise anticommute, generate $M_4$, and are skew-adjoint for the alternating form with matrix
$$
H_4=J\otimes H.
$$
Let
$$
S=Y_1Y_2Y_3Y_4.
$$
Then $S$ is self-adjoint, anticommutes with every $Y_i$, and satisfies $S^2=d^4I_4$.

Choose $C_0,D_0\in M_2(\mathbb F_p)$ with square $d^{-3}I_2$, skew-adjoint for $J$, and put
$$
Z_i=Y_i\otimes I_2\quad(1\le i\le4),
$$
$$
Z_5=S\otimes C_0,\qquad Z_6=S\otimes D_0.
$$
The six $Z_i$ square to $dI_8$, pairwise anticommute, and generate $M_8(\mathbb F_p)$. Hence
$$
C\cong M_8(\mathbb F_p).
$$
Moreover $\tau_-$ is adjoint to the symmetric form
$$
H_8=H_4\otimes J.
$$
Its determinant is a square because
$$
\det(H_8)=\det(H_4)^2\det(J)^4.
$$
In dimension $8$, square determinant is the split plus type. Therefore
$$
(C,\tau_-)\cong (M_8(\mathbb F_p),T).
$$

Step 3: Classify all $64$ transpose sign patterns

For a sign pattern $\varepsilon$, let $\tau_\varepsilon$ be the involution of $C$ defined by
$$
\tau_\varepsilon(e_i)=\varepsilon_i e_i.
$$
Let $r$ be the number of indices with $\varepsilon_i=-1$. For an ordered monomial $e_S$ with $|S|=k$, reversing the order gives
$$
\tau_\varepsilon(e_S)
=(-1)^{k(k-1)/2}\left(\prod_{i\in S}\varepsilon_i\right)e_S.
$$
Hence the trace of $\tau_\varepsilon$ on the $64$-dimensional algebra is obtained by weighting the coefficients of
$$
(1-t)^r(1+t)^{6-r}
$$
by $(-1)^{k(k-1)/2}$. Directly,
$$
\begin{array}{c|ccccccc}
r&0&1&2&3&4&5&6\\ \hline
\operatorname{Tr}(\tau_\varepsilon)&-8&-8&8&8&-8&-8&8
\end{array}
$$

An involution of $M_8(\mathbb F_p)$ adjoint to a symmetric form has fixed-space dimension $8\cdot9/2=36$, while one adjoint to an alternating form has fixed-space dimension $8\cdot7/2=28$. Since the fixed-space dimension is
$$
\frac{64+\operatorname{Tr}(\tau_\varepsilon)}2,
$$
$\tau_\varepsilon$ is orthogonal exactly for
$$
r=2,3,6.
$$

It remains to check the orthogonal type. For $r=6$ we already know $\tau_\varepsilon=\tau_-$ is split plus. If $r=2$, let $T$ be the set of the four positive-sign indices and put $u=\prod_{i\in T}e_i$. If $r=3$, let $T$ be the set of the three negative-sign indices and use the same definition of $u$. In both cases
$$
\tau_\varepsilon=\operatorname{Int}(u)\circ\tau_-,
\qquad
\tau_-(u)=u.
$$
Under the split realization from Step 2, the new adjoint form is represented, up to scalar, by
$$
H_8\rho(u)^{-1}.
$$
Each generator has characteristic polynomial $(t^2-d)^4$, so
$$
\det\rho(e_i)=d^4,
$$
a square. Therefore $\det\rho(u)$ is a square, so twisting by $u$ does not change the discriminant. Thus every orthogonal $\tau_\varepsilon$ above is also split plus.

Consequently the admissible sign patterns are exactly
$$
\binom62+\binom63+\binom66=15+20+1=36.
$$

Step 4: Count normalized sextuples for one admissible sign pattern

Fix one admissible $\varepsilon$. Since
$$
(C,\tau_\varepsilon)\cong(M_8(\mathbb F_p),T),
$$
fix one intertwining isomorphism $\rho_0$. Any other algebra isomorphism $\rho:C\to M_8(\mathbb F_p)$ is
$$
\rho=\operatorname{Ad}_g\circ\rho_0
$$
for some $g\in\mathrm{GL}_8(\mathbb F_p)$, unique up to scalar.

The condition that $\rho$ also intertwine $\tau_\varepsilon$ with transpose is
$$
T\circ\operatorname{Ad}_g=\operatorname{Ad}_g\circ T,
$$
which is equivalent to
$$
g^Tg=\lambda I_8
$$
for some $\lambda\in\mathbb F_p^\times$. Thus the normalized sextuples with this sign pattern are parametrized by
$$
\mathrm{PGO}_8^+(\mathbb F_p).
$$
The multiplier map $\mathrm{GO}_8^+\to\mathbb F_p^\times$ is onto, with kernel $\mathrm O_8^+$. Therefore
$$
|\mathrm{PGO}_8^+(\mathbb F_p)|=|\mathrm O_8^+(\mathbb F_p)|.
$$
Hence the total number of normalized sextuples, over all $36$ admissible sign patterns, is
$$
36|\mathrm O_8^+(\mathbb F_p)|.
$$

Step 5: Evaluate the orthogonal-group order and divide by signs

For a split $2n$-dimensional quadratic space, counting ordered hyperbolic bases gives
$$
|\mathrm O_{2n}^+(\mathbb F_p)|
=2p^{n(n-1)}(p^n-1)\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}(p^{2i}-1).
$$
For $n=4$,
$$
|\mathrm O_8^+(\mathbb F_p)|
=2p^{12}(p^2-1)(p^4-1)^2(p^6-1).
$$
Each ordered field sextuple has exactly $2^6=64$ normalized generator sextuples. Therefore the required number is
$$
\frac{36}{64}|\mathrm O_8^+(\mathbb F_p)|
=\frac{9p^{12}(p^2-1)(p^4-1)^2(p^6-1)}8.
$$

Final Answer: $\boxed{\frac{9p^{12}(p^2-1)(p^4-1)^2(p^6-1)}8}$
