## Steps

Step 1: Normalize each quadratic field and translate the transpose condition

Fix a nonsquare $d\in\mathbb F_p^\times$. If $E\subset M_8(\mathbb F_p)$ is a unital field of order $p^2$, then $W=\mathbb F_p^8$ has dimension $4$ over $E$. Hence for $x\in E$, the matrix trace is
$$
4\operatorname{Tr}_{E/\mathbb F_p}(x).
$$
Since $p$ is odd, the matrix-trace-zero part of $E$ is exactly its one-dimensional field-trace-zero line.

If $0\ne X$ lies on that line, then $X^2=aI_8$ for a nonsquare $a$. Since $d/a$ is a square, the line contains exactly two matrices $\pm X_E$ satisfying
$$
X_E^2=dI_8.
$$
Conversely, every $X$ with $X^2=dI_8$ generates a quadratic field and has trace zero.

Therefore the desired field sextuples correspond, with multiplicity $2^6$, to ordered sextuples $(X_1,\dots,X_6)$ satisfying
$$
X_i^2=dI_8,\qquad X_iX_j=-X_jX_i\quad(i\ne j),
$$
and
$$
X_i^T=-X_i\quad(1\le i\le6).
$$
Thus it remains to count these normalized skew-symmetric generator sextuples and divide by $64$.

Step 2: Identify the six-generator Clifford algebra and its canonical involution

We use the elementary fact that for every $q\in\mathbb F_p^\times$ there exist $A,B\in M_2(\mathbb F_p)$ such that
$$
A^2=B^2=qI_2,\qquad AB=-BA,
$$
and $A,B$ generate $M_2(\mathbb F_p)$. Indeed, if $q=r^2$ is a square, take
$$
A=r\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
B=r\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.
$$
If $q$ is a nonsquare, choose $a,c\in\mathbb F_p$ with $a^2-qc^2=q$, possible by surjectivity of the norm from $\mathbb F_{p^2}^\times$, and take
$$
A=\begin{pmatrix}0&q\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
B=\begin{pmatrix}a&-qc\\c&-a\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Let $C$ be the algebra generated by $e_1,\dots,e_6$ with
$$
e_i^2=d,\qquad e_ie_j=-e_je_i\quad(i\ne j),
$$
and let $\tau$ be the involution determined by
$$
\tau(e_i)=-e_i.
$$
We construct $(C,\tau)$ explicitly.

Choose $U,V\in M_2(\mathbb F_p)$ with square $dI_2$. On $M_2(\mathbb F_p)$ the symplectic involution sends every trace-zero matrix to its negative, so $U,V$ are skew-adjoint for a nondegenerate alternating form with matrix $J$.

Next choose $A,B\in M_2(\mathbb F_p)$ with square $-d^{-1}I_2$. The involution fixing $A$ and $B$ has a three-dimensional fixed subspace, hence is orthogonal; write it as adjoint with respect to a nondegenerate symmetric matrix $H$.

Put $P=UV$ and in $M_4(\mathbb F_p)$ define
$$
Y_1=U\otimes I_2,\qquad Y_2=V\otimes I_2,
$$
$$
Y_3=P\otimes A,\qquad Y_4=P\otimes B.
$$
Since $P^2=-d^2I_2$, all four $Y_i$ square to $dI_4$, pairwise anticommute, and generate $M_4(\mathbb F_p)$. They are skew-adjoint for the alternating form with matrix
$$
H_4=J\otimes H.
$$

Set
$$
S=Y_1Y_2Y_3Y_4.
$$
Then $S$ anticommutes with every $Y_i$, satisfies
$$
S^2=d^4I_4,
$$
and is self-adjoint for the involution defined by $H_4$.

Choose $C_0,D_0\in M_2(\mathbb F_p)$ with square $d^{-3}I_2$, skew-adjoint for the symplectic form $J$. In $M_8(\mathbb F_p)=M_4\otimes M_2$ put
$$
Z_i=Y_i\otimes I_2\quad(1\le i\le4),
$$
$$
Z_5=S\otimes C_0,\qquad Z_6=S\otimes D_0.
$$
These six matrices square to $dI_8$, pairwise anticommute, and generate all of $M_8(\mathbb F_p)$. Hence the natural map
$$
C\longrightarrow M_8(\mathbb F_p)
$$
is onto. Since $C$ is spanned by its $2^6=64$ ordered squarefree monomials and $\dim M_8=64$, it is an isomorphism.

The involution $\tau$ is adjoint to the symmetric form
$$
H_8=H_4\otimes J.
$$
Moreover
$$
\det(H_4)=\det(J)^2\det(H)^2
$$
is a square, and therefore
$$
\det(H_8)=\det(H_4)^2\det(J)^4
$$
is also a square. In dimension $8$, a nondegenerate symmetric form with square determinant is of split plus type. Thus, after a change of basis, we may take $H_8=I_8$. In that basis
$$
Z_i^T=-Z_i
$$
for all $i$.

Step 3: Classify all normalized sextuples by orthogonal similitudes

Fix one normalized sextuple $(Z_1,\dots,Z_6)$ as above, giving an isomorphism
$$
\rho_0:(C,\tau)\longrightarrow (M_8(\mathbb F_p),T),
$$
where $T$ denotes transpose.

Any other normalized sextuple gives another algebra isomorphism $\rho:C\to M_8(\mathbb F_p)$. A direct matrix-unit argument shows that every automorphism of $M_8(\mathbb F_p)$ is inner, so
$$
\rho=\operatorname{Ad}_g\circ\rho_0
$$
for some $g\in\mathrm{GL}_8(\mathbb F_p)$, unique up to multiplication by a nonzero scalar.

The condition that $\rho$ also intertwine $\tau$ with transpose is
$$
T\circ\operatorname{Ad}_g=\operatorname{Ad}_g\circ T.
$$
For all $X\in M_8(\mathbb F_p)$ this says
$$
g^{-T}Xg^T=gXg^{-1}.
$$
Hence the two conjugating matrices differ by a scalar, equivalently
$$
g^Tg=\lambda I_8
$$
for some $\lambda\in\mathbb F_p^\times$. Thus $g$ lies in the orthogonal similitude group $\mathrm{GO}_8^+(\mathbb F_p)$.

Conversely every such similitude produces a normalized sextuple. Since scalar matrices induce the same inner automorphism, the number of normalized sextuples is
$$
\frac{|\mathrm{GO}_8^+(\mathbb F_p)|}{p-1}.
$$

Step 4: Reduce the similitude count to the split orthogonal group

For a split $8$-dimensional form, the multiplier map
$$
\mathrm{GO}_8^+(\mathbb F_p)\to\mathbb F_p^\times
$$
is onto. In a hyperbolic basis, for any $\mu\in\mathbb F_p^\times$, scaling all four first isotropic basis vectors by $\mu$ and fixing their partners has multiplier $\mu$. Its kernel is $\mathrm O_8^+(\mathbb F_p)$. Therefore
$$
|\mathrm{GO}_8^+(\mathbb F_p)|=(p-1)|\mathrm O_8^+(\mathbb F_p)|,
$$
so the number of normalized sextuples is simply
$$
|\mathrm O_8^+(\mathbb F_p)|.
$$

Step 5: Count the split orthogonal group from hyperbolic bases

More generally, in a split $2n$-dimensional quadratic space over $\mathbb F_p$, the number of nonzero isotropic vectors is
$$
(p^{n-1}+1)(p^n-1).
$$
After choosing such a vector $e_1$, there are exactly
$$
p^{2n-2}
$$
isotropic vectors $f_1$ with $\langle e_1,f_1\rangle=1$: in hyperbolic coordinates, the pairing condition fixes one coordinate and isotropy determines one more, leaving $2n-2$ free coordinates. The orthogonal complement of the hyperbolic plane $\langle e_1,f_1\rangle$ is again split of dimension $2n-2$.

Hence the orthogonal group, which acts simply transitively on ordered hyperbolic bases, has order
$$
|\mathrm O_{2n}^+(\mathbb F_p)|
=p^{n(n-1)}\prod_{i=1}^n (p^{i-1}+1)(p^i-1).
$$
For $n=4$ this simplifies to
$$
|\mathrm O_8^+(\mathbb F_p)|
=2p^{12}(p^2-1)(p^4-1)^2(p^6-1).
$$

Step 6: Divide by the sign choices

Each ordered field sextuple has exactly $2^6=64$ normalized generator sextuples. Therefore the required number is
$$
\frac{|\mathrm O_8^+(\mathbb F_p)|}{64}
=\frac{p^{12}(p^2-1)(p^4-1)^2(p^6-1)}{32}.
$$

Final Answer: $\boxed{\frac{p^{12}(p^2-1)(p^4-1)^2(p^6-1)}{32}}$
