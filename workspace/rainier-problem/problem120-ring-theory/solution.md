## Steps

Step 1: Normalize the six quadratic fields

Fix a nonsquare $d\in\mathbb F_p^\times$. If $E\subset M_8(\mathbb F_p)$ is a unital field of order $p^2$, then $\mathbb F_p^8$ is $4$-dimensional over $E$, so the matrix trace of $x\in E$ is $4\operatorname{Tr}_{E/\mathbb F_p}(x)$. Thus the matrix-trace-zero part of $E$ is its one-dimensional field-trace-zero line.

For $0\ne X$ on that line, $X^2=aI_8$ with $a$ nonsquare. Since $d/a$ is a square, the line has exactly two elements $\pm X_E$ satisfying $X_E^2=dI_8$. Conversely, $X^2=dI_8$ has irreducible minimal polynomial $t^2-d$, so $\mathbb F_p[X]$ is a field of order $p^2$ and $X$ has trace zero.

If $E$ is transpose-stable, transpose acts by a sign on this line. Hence field sextuples correspond, with multiplicity $2^6$, to normalized sextuples $(X_1,\ldots,X_6)$ with
$$
X_i^2=dI_8,\qquad X_iX_j=-X_jX_i\ (i\ne j),\qquad X_i^T=\varepsilon_iX_i,
$$
where $\varepsilon_i\in\{\pm1\}$.

Step 2: Realize the Clifford algebra and one symmetric adjoint involution

Let $C$ be generated by $e_1,\ldots,e_6$ with $e_i^2=d$ and $e_ie_j=-e_je_i$ for $i\ne j$. Ordered squarefree monomials span $C$, so $\dim C\le2^6=64$.

We use a direct $2\times2$ block. For every $q\in\mathbb F_p^\times$, write $q=x^2+y^2$: indeed the sets $S=\{z^2:z\in\mathbb F_p\}$ and $q-S$ both have $(p+1)/2$ elements, so they intersect. Set
$$
A_q=\begin{pmatrix}x&y\\y&-x\end{pmatrix},\qquad
B_q=\begin{pmatrix}-y&x\\x&y\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Then $A_q,B_q$ are symmetric and trace zero, $A_q^2=B_q^2=qI_2$, and $A_qB_q=-B_qA_q$. They generate $M_2$: if $\alpha I+\beta A_q+\gamma B_q+\delta A_qB_q=0$, conjugating by $A_q$ and adding gives $\alpha I+\beta A_q=0$, hence $\alpha=\beta=0$; multiplying the remainder by $B_q^{-1}$ gives $\gamma I+\delta A_q=0$, hence $\gamma=\delta=0$.

Take $U=A_d,V=B_d$, $A=A_{-d^{-1}},B=B_{-d^{-1}}$, and $P=UV$. Define
$$
Y_1=U\otimes I_2,\quad Y_2=V\otimes I_2,\quad Y_3=P\otimes A,\quad Y_4=P\otimes B.
$$
They square to $dI_4$, pairwise anticommute, and generate $M_4$: $Y_1,Y_2$ generate $M_2\otimes I_2$, while $(P^{-1}\otimes I_2)Y_3=I_2\otimes A$ and $(P^{-1}\otimes I_2)Y_4=I_2\otimes B$. Put $S=Y_1Y_2Y_3Y_4$; then $S^2=d^4I_4$ and $S$ anticommutes with every $Y_i$. With $C_0=A_{d^{-3}},D_0=B_{d^{-3}}$, set
$$
Z_i=Y_i\otimes I_2\ (1\le i\le4),\qquad Z_5=S\otimes C_0,\qquad Z_6=S\otimes D_0.
$$
These six matrices square to $dI_8$, pairwise anticommute, and generate $M_8=M_4\otimes M_2$. Thus $e_i\mapsto Z_i$ is a surjection $C\to M_8$ between spaces of dimension at most and exactly $64$, hence
$$
C\cong M_8(\mathbb F_p).
$$

Let $J=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix}$. Every trace-zero $2\times2$ matrix $X$ satisfies $J^{-1}X^TJ=-X$. Since $U,V,C_0,D_0$ are trace zero and $A,B$ are symmetric, while the adjoint sends $P=UV$ to $VU=-P$, the $Y_i$ are skew-adjoint for $H_4=J\otimes I_2$. Reversing four pairwise-anticommuting skew-adjoint factors uses six swaps, so $S$ is self-adjoint. Therefore every $Z_i$ is skew-adjoint for the symmetric matrix
$$
H_8=H_4\otimes J.
$$
Thus $\tau_-(e_i)=-e_i$ is represented by $\sigma_{H_8}(X)=H_8^{-1}X^TH_8$, and
$$
\det(H_8)=\det(H_4)^2\det(J)^4
$$
is a square.

Step 3: Determine the transpose-compatible sign patterns without classification theorems

For $\varepsilon=(\varepsilon_1,\ldots,\varepsilon_6)$ let $\tau_\varepsilon(e_i)=\varepsilon_i e_i$, extended as an anti-automorphism, and let $r$ be the number of negative signs. On a degree-$k$ squarefree monomial,
$$
\tau_\varepsilon(e_S)=(-1)^{k(k-1)/2}\left(\prod_{i\in S}\varepsilon_i\right)e_S.
$$
If $c_k(r)=[t^k](1-t)^r(1+t)^{6-r}$, then
$$
\operatorname{Tr}(\tau_\varepsilon)=c_0+c_1-c_2-c_3+c_4+c_5-c_6.
$$
Expanding the seven polynomials gives the coefficient vectors and signed sums
$$
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
r&(c_0,c_1,c_2,c_3,c_4,c_5,c_6)&\operatorname{Tr}(\tau_\varepsilon)\\ \hline
0&(1,6,15,20,15,6,1)&-8\\
1&(1,4,5,0,-5,-4,-1)&-8\\
2&(1,2,-1,-4,-1,2,1)&8\\
3&(1,0,-3,0,3,0,-1)&8\\
4&(1,-2,-1,4,-1,-2,1)&-8\\
5&(1,-4,5,0,-5,4,-1)&-8\\
6&(1,-6,15,-20,15,-6,1)&8.
\end{array}
$$
Because $p$ is odd, a linear involution is diagonalizable with eigenvalues $\pm1$, so
$$
\dim\operatorname{Fix}(\tau_\varepsilon)=\frac{64+\operatorname{Tr}(\tau_\varepsilon)}2.
$$
Transpose on $M_8$ fixes the $36$-dimensional space of symmetric matrices, and conjugating an anti-involution by an algebra isomorphism preserves the dimension of its fixed space. Hence transpose compatibility is possible only for
$$
r=2,3,6.
$$

We now prove directly that all these cases occur. First, any nonsingular symmetric $8\times8$ matrix $H$ over $\mathbb F_p$ with square determinant has the form $H=G^TG$. To see this, diagonalize the associated symmetric bilinear form by repeatedly splitting off a vector $v$ with $B(v,v)\ne0$; such a vector exists because otherwise polarization would give $B=0$. After rescaling diagonal basis vectors, the diagonal entries are $1$ or a fixed nonsquare $\nu$. A square determinant means an even number of $\nu$ entries. The same counting argument used in Step 2 gives $\nu=x^2+y^2$, and
$$
Q=\begin{pmatrix}x&-y\\y&x\end{pmatrix}
\quad\text{satisfies}\quad Q^TQ=\nu I_2.
$$
Pairing the $\nu$ entries therefore turns the diagonal form into $R^TR$, and undoing the diagonalizing change of basis gives $H=G^TG$. For such an $H$, direct multiplication shows
$$
T\circ\operatorname{Ad}_G=\operatorname{Ad}_G\circ\sigma_H,
$$
so $\sigma_H$ is transpose-compatible.

For $r=6$, this applies to $H_8$ from Step 2. For $r=2$, let $T_0$ be the four positive-sign indices; for $r=3$, let $T_0$ be the three negative-sign indices, and put $u=\prod_{i\in T_0}e_i$. If $m=|T_0|$, anticommutation gives $\operatorname{Int}(u)(e_i)=(-1)^{m-1}e_i$ for $i\in T_0$ and $(-1)^m e_i$ otherwise. Thus for $m=4$ or $3$, respectively,
$$
\tau_\varepsilon=\operatorname{Int}(u)\circ\tau_-,\qquad \tau_-(u)=(-1)^{m(m+1)/2}u=u.
$$
If $U$ is the image of $u$ in the realization of Step 2, then $\tau_\varepsilon$ is $\sigma_K$ with
$$
K=H_8U^{-1}.
$$
From $\tau_-(U)=U$ we get $U^TH_8=H_8U$, hence $K^T=K$. Each generator has characteristic polynomial $(t^2-d)^4$, so its determinant is $d^4$, a square; therefore $\det U$ and $\det K$ are squares. The square-determinant construction therefore makes every $r=2,3$ case transpose-compatible. Thus the admissible sign patterns are exactly
$$
\binom62+\binom63+\binom66=36.
$$

Step 4: Count normalized sextuples for one admissible sign pattern

Fix an admissible $\varepsilon$ and an intertwining isomorphism $\rho_0:(C,\tau_\varepsilon)\to(M_8(\mathbb F_p),T)$. Every automorphism of $M_8$ is inner: if $F_{ij}=\alpha(E_{ij})$, choose $0\ne v\in\operatorname{im}F_{11}$ and put $v_i=F_{i1}v$; then $F_{jk}v_i=\delta_{ki}v_j$. Also $F_{1i}v_i=v$, and applying $F_{1j}$ to a linear relation among the $v_i$ proves independence. Thus $(v_i)$ is a basis and $\alpha=\operatorname{Ad}_g$ for the change-of-basis matrix $g$. The conjugator is unique up to scalar.

Thus every other algebra isomorphism is $\operatorname{Ad}_g\circ\rho_0$. It still intertwines transpose exactly when
$$
T\circ\operatorname{Ad}_g=\operatorname{Ad}_g\circ T.
$$
Since $T\circ\operatorname{Ad}_g=\operatorname{Ad}_{(g^{-1})^T}\circ T$, equality holds exactly when $(g^{-1})^T$ and $g$ differ by a scalar, equivalently
$$
g^Tg=\lambda I_8.
$$
Hence the normalized sextuples for this sign pattern are parametrized by
$$
\mathrm{PGO}_8^+(\mathbb F_p)=\mathrm{GO}_8^+(\mathbb F_p)/(\mathbb F_p^\times I_8).
$$
The form $I_8$ is split: the symmetric matrix $J_8=\begin{pmatrix}0&I_4\\I_4&0\end{pmatrix}$ has determinant $1$, so the square-determinant argument in Step 3 shows it is congruent to $I_8$.

In this hyperbolic basis, for every $\lambda\in\mathbb F_p^\times$,
$$
g_\lambda=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda I_4&0\\0&I_4\end{pmatrix}
$$
satisfies $g_\lambda^TJ_8g_\lambda=\lambda J_8$. Hence the multiplier map $\mathrm{GO}_8^+\to\mathbb F_p^\times$ is onto with kernel $\mathrm O_8^+$, so
$$
|\mathrm{GO}_8^+|=(p-1)|\mathrm O_8^+|,
\qquad
|\mathrm{PGO}_8^+|=|\mathrm O_8^+|.
$$
Therefore the number of normalized sextuples over all admissible sign patterns is
$$
36|\mathrm O_8^+(\mathbb F_p)|.
$$

Step 5: Divide by generator signs and evaluate the orthogonal-group order

For completeness, count ordered hyperbolic bases in a split $2n$-space with quadratic form $q(x,y)=x\cdot y$. The number of nonzero isotropic vectors is
$$
N_n=p^n+(p^n-1)p^{n-1}-1=(p^{n-1}+1)(p^n-1).
$$
After choosing the first isotropic vector $e$, there are $p^{2n-2}$ isotropic $f$ with $B(e,f)=1$: in hyperbolic coordinates the equation $B(e,f)=1$ fixes one coordinate and $q(f)=0$ fixes one more. Their span is a hyperbolic plane and its orthogonal complement is split of dimension $2n-2$. Hence, if $H_n$ is the number of ordered hyperbolic bases,
$$
H_n=N_np^{2n-2}H_{n-1},\qquad H_0=1.
$$
An isometry is uniquely determined by the image of one fixed hyperbolic basis, and every ordered hyperbolic basis occurs, so $|\mathrm O_{2n}^+|=H_n$. Therefore
$$
|\mathrm O_{2n}^+(\mathbb F_p)|=2p^{n(n-1)}(p^n-1)\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}(p^{2i}-1).
$$
At $n=4$,
$$
|\mathrm O_8^+(\mathbb F_p)|=2p^{12}(p^2-1)(p^4-1)^2(p^6-1).
$$
Each ordered field sextuple gives exactly $2^6$ normalized generator sextuples. Hence the required number is
$$
\frac{36}{64}|\mathrm O_8^+(\mathbb F_p)|
=\frac{9p^{12}(p^2-1)(p^4-1)^2(p^6-1)}8.
$$

Final Answer: $\boxed{\frac{9p^{12}(p^2-1)(p^4-1)^2(p^6-1)}8}$
