## Steps

Step 1: Normalize the six quadratic fields

Fix a nonsquare $d\in\mathbb F_p^\times$. If $E\subset M_8(\mathbb F_p)$ is a unital field of order $p^2$, then $\mathbb F_p^8$ is $4$-dimensional over $E$. Hence the matrix trace of $x\in E$ is
$$
4\operatorname{Tr}_{E/\mathbb F_p}(x).
$$
Because $p$ is odd, the matrix-trace-zero part of $E$ is therefore the one-dimensional field-trace-zero line.

For $0\ne X$ on that line, $X^2=aI_8$ with $a$ nonsquare. Since $d/a$ is a square, that line contains exactly two elements $\pm X_E$ satisfying
$$
X_E^2=dI_8.
$$
Conversely, $X^2=dI_8$ implies that the minimal polynomial of $X$ is the irreducible polynomial $t^2-d$, so $\mathbb F_p[X]$ is a field of order $p^2$ and $X$ has trace zero.

If $E$ is transpose-stable, transpose restricts to an $\mathbb F_p$-automorphism of $E$ and hence acts by a sign on its trace-zero line. Thus field sextuples correspond, with multiplicity $2^6$, to normalized sextuples $(X_1,\ldots,X_6)$ satisfying
$$
X_i^2=dI_8,\qquad X_iX_j=-X_jX_i\quad(i\ne j),
$$
and
$$
X_i^T=\varepsilon_iX_i,\qquad \varepsilon_i\in\{\pm1\}.
$$

Step 2: Prove that the six Clifford generators give the full matrix algebra

Let $C$ be generated by $e_1,\ldots,e_6$ with
$$
e_i^2=d,\qquad e_ie_j=-e_je_i\quad(i\ne j).
$$
Every word reduces to a scalar multiple of an ordered squarefree monomial
$$
e_S=e_{i_1}\cdots e_{i_k}\qquad(i_1<\cdots<i_k),
$$
so $C$ is spanned by $2^6=64$ elements and therefore
$$
\dim_{\mathbb F_p}C\le64.
$$

We now construct a surjection $C\to M_8(\mathbb F_p)$. First note that for every $q\in\mathbb F_p^\times$ there are anticommuting $A,B\in M_2(\mathbb F_p)$ with
$$
A^2=B^2=qI_2
$$
which generate $M_2$. If $q=r^2$, take
$$
A=r\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
B=r\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.
$$
If $q$ is nonsquare, the norm map $N:\mathbb F_{p^2}^\times\to\mathbb F_p^\times$ is onto because, on the cyclic group of order $p^2-1$, it is $x\mapsto x^{p+1}$ and hence has image of size $p-1$. Choose $a,c\in\mathbb F_p$ with $a^2-qc^2=q$ and set
$$
A=\begin{pmatrix}0&q\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
B=\begin{pmatrix}a&-qc\\c&-a\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Then $A^2=B^2=qI_2$ and $AB=-BA$. To prove generation directly, suppose
$$
\alpha I_2+\beta A+\gamma B+\delta AB=0.
$$
Conjugation by $A$ fixes $I_2,A$ and negates $B,AB$. Adding the original relation to its conjugate and using $p\ne2$ gives $\alpha I_2+\beta A=0$. Since $A$ is not scalar, $\alpha=\beta=0$. The remaining equation is $(\gamma I_2+\delta A)B=0$; $B$ is invertible because $B^2=qI_2$, hence $\gamma I_2+\delta A=0$, so $\gamma=\delta=0$. Therefore $I_2,A,B,AB$ form a basis of $M_2$.

Choose such $U,V$ with square $dI_2$, and such $A,B$ with square $-d^{-1}I_2$. Put $P=UV$ and
$$
Y_1=U\otimes I_2,\quad Y_2=V\otimes I_2,\quad
Y_3=P\otimes A,\quad Y_4=P\otimes B.
$$
The $Y_i$ square to $dI_4$ and pairwise anticommute. Moreover $Y_1,Y_2$ generate $M_2\otimes I_2$; since $P$ is invertible and belongs to that factor,
$$
(P^{-1}\otimes I_2)Y_3=I_2\otimes A,\qquad
(P^{-1}\otimes I_2)Y_4=I_2\otimes B.
$$
Thus the $Y_i$ contain both tensor factors and generate $M_4=M_2\otimes M_2$.

Let
$$
S=Y_1Y_2Y_3Y_4.
$$
Then $S$ anticommutes with each $Y_i$ and $S^2=d^4I_4$. Choose anticommuting $C_0,D_0\in M_2$ with $C_0^2=D_0^2=d^{-3}I_2$ and define
$$
Z_i=Y_i\otimes I_2\quad(1\le i\le4),\qquad
Z_5=S\otimes C_0,\quad Z_6=S\otimes D_0.
$$
The six $Z_i$ square to $dI_8$ and pairwise anticommute. They generate all of $M_8$: $Z_1,\ldots,Z_4$ generate $M_4\otimes I_2$, and since $S$ is invertible in that factor,
$$
(S^{-1}\otimes I_2)Z_5=I_4\otimes C_0,\qquad
(S^{-1}\otimes I_2)Z_6=I_4\otimes D_0.
$$
Because $C_0,D_0$ generate $M_2$, the generated algebra is
$$
M_4\otimes M_2=M_8(\mathbb F_p).
$$
Hence $e_i\mapsto Z_i$ gives a surjective homomorphism $C\to M_8$. Since $M_8$ has dimension $64$ and $C$ has dimension at most $64$, the map is an isomorphism:
$$
C\cong M_8(\mathbb F_p).
$$
This proves the generation claim rather than merely exhibiting a representation.

For the involution $\tau_-(e_i)=-e_i$, choose the above $2\times2$ pairs so that $U,V,C_0,D_0$ are skew-adjoint for the standard alternating form $J$, while the anti-automorphism fixing $A,B$ is adjoint to a symmetric form $H$. Then the $Y_i$ are skew-adjoint for the alternating form $H_4=J\otimes H$, $S$ is self-adjoint, and all six $Z_i$ are skew-adjoint for the symmetric form
$$
H_8=H_4\otimes J.
$$
Therefore $\tau_-$ is an orthogonal involution with Gram matrix $H_8$, and
$$
\det(H_8)=\det(H_4)^2\det(J)^4
$$
is a square.

Step 3: Classify exactly which sign involutions are transpose

We use the classification theorem in the following explicit form. Over a field $F$ of characteristic not $2$, every involution of the first kind on the split algebra $M_n(F)$ is
$$
\sigma_H(X)=H^{-1}X^TH
$$
with $H^T=H$ (orthogonal type) or $H^T=-H$ (symplectic type). Two orthogonal involutions are conjugate by an $F$-algebra automorphism of $M_n(F)$ exactly when their symmetric Gram forms are similar:
$$
K=\lambda G^THG
$$
for some $G\in\mathrm{GL}_n(F)$ and $\lambda\in F^\times$.

For $F=\mathbb F_p$, $p$ odd, nondegenerate symmetric forms of a fixed dimension are classified by the square class of their determinant. In dimension $2m$, the split hyperbolic $(+)$ class has determinant square class $(-1)^m$. Here $n=8=2\cdot4$, so an $8$-dimensional symmetric form is split $+$ exactly when its determinant is a square. In particular, ordinary transpose, which is adjoint to $I_8$, is the split $+$ orthogonal involution. The hypotheses apply because $p$ is odd, Step 2 identifies $C$ with the split algebra $M_8(\mathbb F_p)$, and the involutions below fix the center $\mathbb F_p$.

For a sign pattern $\varepsilon=(\varepsilon_1,\ldots,\varepsilon_6)$ define
$$
\tau_\varepsilon(e_i)=\varepsilon_i e_i.
$$
Let $r$ be the number of negative signs. For a squarefree monomial $e_S$ of degree $k$,
$$
\tau_\varepsilon(e_S)=(-1)^{k(k-1)/2}
\left(\prod_{i\in S}\varepsilon_i\right)e_S.
$$
Writing
$$
c_k(r)=[t^k](1-t)^r(1+t)^{6-r},
$$
we obtain
$$
\operatorname{Tr}(\tau_\varepsilon)
=\sum_{k=0}^6(-1)^{k(k-1)/2}c_k(r),
$$
and direct evaluation gives
$$
\begin{array}{c|ccccccc}
r&0&1&2&3&4&5&6\\ \hline
\operatorname{Tr}(\tau_\varepsilon)&-8&-8&8&8&-8&-8&8.
\end{array}
$$
An orthogonal involution of $M_8$ has fixed-space dimension $8\cdot9/2=36$, while a symplectic involution has fixed-space dimension $8\cdot7/2=28$. Since
$$
\dim\operatorname{Fix}(\tau_\varepsilon)=
\frac{64+\operatorname{Tr}(\tau_\varepsilon)}2,
$$
$\tau_\varepsilon$ is orthogonal exactly for
$$
r=2,3,6.
$$

For $r=6$, this is $\tau_-$, whose Gram determinant is square by Step 2, so it is split $+$ and conjugate to transpose.

For $r=2$, let $T$ be the four positive-sign indices; for $r=3$, let $T$ be the three negative-sign indices. Put
$$
u=\prod_{i\in T}e_i.
$$
The anticommutation relations give in both cases
$$
\tau_\varepsilon=\operatorname{Int}(u)\circ\tau_-,\qquad
\tau_-(u)=u.
$$
Under the realization of Step 2, if $U=\rho(u)$ and $\tau_-$ is adjoint to $H_8$, then $\tau_\varepsilon$ is adjoint to the symmetric form with Gram matrix $H_8U^{-1}$. Each generator has characteristic polynomial $(t^2-d)^4$, hence determinant $d^4$, a square. Therefore $\det U$ is a square, so
$$
\det(H_8U^{-1})\equiv\det(H_8)
\pmod{(\mathbb F_p^\times)^2}.
$$
Thus every orthogonal case $r=2,3,6$ is split $+$ and therefore conjugate to transpose. The admissible sign patterns are exactly
$$
\binom62+\binom63+\binom66=15+20+1=36.
$$

Step 4: Count normalized sextuples for one admissible sign pattern

Fix one admissible $\varepsilon$ and one intertwining isomorphism
$$
\rho_0:(C,\tau_\varepsilon)\to(M_8(\mathbb F_p),T).
$$
Every other algebra isomorphism is $\operatorname{Ad}_g\circ\rho_0$ for some $g\in\mathrm{GL}_8$, unique up to nonzero scalar, because every automorphism of a full matrix algebra is inner and its center consists of the scalars.

The new isomorphism still intertwines the involutions exactly when
$$
T\circ\operatorname{Ad}_g=\operatorname{Ad}_g\circ T.
$$
Since
$$
T\circ\operatorname{Ad}_g=\operatorname{Ad}_{(g^{-1})^T}\circ T,
$$
this is equivalent to $(g^{-1})^T$ being a scalar multiple of $g$, or
$$
g^Tg=\lambda I_8.
$$
Hence the normalized sextuples for this sign pattern are parametrized by
$$
\mathrm{PGO}_8^+(\mathbb F_p)=
\mathrm{GO}_8^+(\mathbb F_p)/(\mathbb F_p^\times I_8).
$$

The multiplier factor must be retained. In a hyperbolic basis with Gram matrix
$$
J_8=\begin{pmatrix}0&I_4\\I_4&0\end{pmatrix},
$$
for every $\lambda\in\mathbb F_p^\times$ the matrix
$$
g_\lambda=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda I_4&0\\0&I_4\end{pmatrix}
$$
satisfies $g_\lambda^TJ_8g_\lambda=\lambda J_8$. Thus the multiplier map
$$
\mu:\mathrm{GO}_8^+\to\mathbb F_p^\times
$$
is surjective with kernel $\mathrm O_8^+$. Hence
$$
|\mathrm{GO}_8^+|=(p-1)|\mathrm O_8^+|.
$$
After quotienting by the scalar subgroup of order $p-1$,
$$
|\mathrm{PGO}_8^+|=|\mathrm O_8^+|.
$$
In particular one must not replace $\mathrm{PGO}_8^+$ by $\mathrm O_8^+/\{\pm I\}$; that would miss a factor $2$.

Therefore the number of normalized sextuples over all admissible sign patterns is
$$
36|\mathrm O_8^+(\mathbb F_p)|.
$$

Step 5: Divide by the generator signs

For a split $2n$-dimensional quadratic space over $\mathbb F_p$,
$$
|\mathrm O_{2n}^+(\mathbb F_p)|
=2p^{n(n-1)}(p^n-1)\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}(p^{2i}-1).
$$
At $n=4$,
$$
|\mathrm O_8^+(\mathbb F_p)|
=2p^{12}(p^2-1)(p^4-1)^2(p^6-1).
$$
Each ordered field sextuple gives exactly $2^6$ normalized generator sextuples, one independent sign choice for each trace-zero generator. Hence the required number is
$$
\frac{36}{64}|\mathrm O_8^+(\mathbb F_p)|
=\frac{9p^{12}(p^2-1)(p^4-1)^2(p^6-1)}8.
$$

Final Answer: $\boxed{\frac{9p^{12}(p^2-1)(p^4-1)^2(p^6-1)}8}$

The Clifford generation claim is proved by recovering both tensor factors and using the $64$-dimensional spanning bound. The classification theorem for first-kind involutions on split matrix algebras and the finite-field classification of symmetric forms are stated with the needed hypotheses. The projective similitude count explicitly proves multiplier surjectivity, preventing the factor-of-two error.
