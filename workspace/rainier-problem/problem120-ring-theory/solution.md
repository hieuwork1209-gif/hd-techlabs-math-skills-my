## Steps

Step 1: Normalize the trace-zero direction of each quadratic field

Put
$$
G_{32}=|\mathrm{GL}_{32}(\mathbb F_p)|
$$
and fix a nonsquare $d\in\mathbb F_p^\times$. If $E\subset M_{32}(\mathbb F_p)$ is a unital field of order $p^2$, then $W=\mathbb F_p^{32}$ has dimension $16$ over $E$. Hence for $x\in E$, the matrix trace of multiplication by $x$ on $W$ is
$$
16\operatorname{Tr}_{E/\mathbb F_p}(x).
$$
Since $p$ is odd, the matrix-trace-zero part of $E$ is exactly its one-dimensional field-trace-zero line.

If $0\ne X$ lies on that line, then $X^2=aI_{32}$ for a nonsquare $a$. Since $d/a$ is a square, that line contains exactly two matrices $\pm X_E$ satisfying
$$
X_E^2=dI_{32}.
$$
Conversely, every $X$ with $X^2=dI_{32}$ generates a quadratic field and has trace zero.

Thus the desired families of fields are obtained from normalized generators indexed by the $14$ vertices of the Heawood graph, each squaring to $dI_{32}$, with generators anticommuting across an incidence edge and commuting otherwise, by dividing by $2^{14}$.

Step 2: Compute the radical of the Fano-plane commutation form

Let
$$
\mathcal P=\mathcal L=\mathbb F_2^3\setminus\{0\},
$$
with a point $x\in\mathcal P$ incident to a line $y\in\mathcal L$ when $x\cdot y=0$. Let $B$ be the $7\times7$ incidence matrix over $\mathbb F_2$.

For $t\in\mathbb F_2^3$, define the length-$7$ vector
$$
c_t(x)=t\cdot x\qquad(x\in\mathcal P).
$$
The three nonzero vectors on any Fano line sum to $0$, so $Bc_t=0$. Conversely, over $\mathbb F_2$ the row indexed by $y$ is the function
$$
1+c_y,
$$
because $1+c_y(x)=1$ exactly when $x\cdot y=0$. Hence the row space of $B$ is
$$
\langle 1,c_{(1,0,0)},c_{(0,1,0)},c_{(0,0,1)}\rangle,
$$
which has dimension $4$. Therefore
$$
K:=\ker B=\{c_t:t\in\mathbb F_2^3\}
$$
has dimension $3$. Every nonzero vector of $K$ has weight $4$.

The commutation form for the bipartite Heawood graph has matrix
$$
H=\begin{pmatrix}0&B\\B^T&0\end{pmatrix},
$$
so
$$
\operatorname{rad}H=K\oplus K
$$
has dimension $6$.

Step 3: Determine the center of the universal graph algebra

Let $A$ be the $\mathbb F_p$-algebra generated by $e_v$ for the $14$ vertices, with
$$
e_v^2=d,
$$
with $e_ve_w=-e_we_v$ across an incidence edge and $e_ve_w=e_we_v$ otherwise. Ordering the vertices gives a basis of $2^{14}$ squarefree monomials.

A monomial with support vector $z\in\mathbb F_2^{14}$ commutes with every generator exactly when
$$
Hz=0.
$$
Hence the center is spanned by the $2^6=64$ monomials indexed by $K\oplus K$.

Take $(r,s)\in K\oplus K$. The square of its central monomial is
$$
(-1)^{r^TBs}d^{|r|+|s|}.
$$
Since $Bs=0$ and every nonzero vector of $K$ has weight $4$, this is always a square in $\mathbb F_p^\times$. Choosing bases of the two copies of $K$ and rescaling the six corresponding central monomials therefore gives six commuting central involutions. Their $64$ products are linearly independent, so
$$
Z(A)\cong \mathbb F_p[(C_2)^6]\cong \mathbb F_p^{64}.
$$
Moreover, choosing a complement to $K\oplus K$ in $\mathbb F_2^{14}$ shows that $A$ is free of rank $2^8=256$ over its center. Thus each primitive central idempotent cuts out a $256$-dimensional block.

Step 4: Show that every central block is $M_{16}(\mathbb F_p)$

Fix one primitive central idempotent. The commutation form on the quotient
$$
\mathbb F_2^{14}/\operatorname{rad}H
$$
is a nondegenerate alternating form of dimension $8$. Choose a symplectic basis
$$
a_1,b_1,\dots,a_4,b_4.
$$
Lifting these basis vectors to monomials in the block gives generators $A_i,B_i$ such that distinct pairs commute, while
$$
A_iB_i=-B_iA_i,
\qquad
A_i^2=q_iI,
\qquad
B_i^2=r_iI
$$
for some $q_i,r_i\in\mathbb F_p^\times$.

For any nonzero $q,r\in\mathbb F_p$, the algebra on two generators with squares $q,r$ and anticommutation is $M_2(\mathbb F_p)$. If $q=s^2$ is a square, use
$$
s\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\begin{pmatrix}0&r\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.
$$
If $q$ is a nonsquare, use
$$
\begin{pmatrix}0&q\\1&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\begin{pmatrix}a&-qc\\c&-a\end{pmatrix},
$$
where $a^2-qc^2=r$; such $a,c$ exist because the norm map $\mathbb F_{p^2}^\times\to\mathbb F_p^\times$ is onto.

Therefore each central block is the tensor product of four copies of $M_2(\mathbb F_p)$, hence
$$
M_{16}(\mathbb F_p).
$$
Consequently
$$
A\cong \bigoplus_{64}M_{16}(\mathbb F_p).
$$

Step 5: Count the $32$-dimensional representations

A simple module for each factor $M_{16}(\mathbb F_p)$ has dimension $16$. Since $W=\mathbb F_p^{32}$, a representation of $A$ on $W$ has total multiplicity $2$ across the $64$ simple factors.

There are two kinds of module type:
$$
64
$$
choices where one simple factor occurs with multiplicity $2$, and
$$
\binom{64}{2}=2016
$$
choices where two distinct simple factors occur once each.

For the first kind the invertible commutant is $\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb F_p)$; for the second it is $(\mathbb F_p^\times)^2$. Each module type is one $\mathrm{GL}_{32}(\mathbb F_p)$-conjugacy orbit. Hence the number of normalized generator families is
$$
G_{32}\left(
\frac{64}{|\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb F_p)|}
+\frac{2016}{(p-1)^2}
\right).
$$

Step 6: Divide by signs and simplify

Using
$$
|\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb F_p)|=p(p-1)^2(p+1),
$$
and dividing by the $2^{14}$ independent sign choices, the required number is
$$
\frac{G_{32}}{2^{14}(p-1)^2}
\left(\frac{64}{p(p+1)}+2016\right).
$$
Since $64+2016p(p+1)=32\bigl(63p(p+1)+2\bigr)$, this becomes
$$
\frac{G_{32}(63p^2+63p+2)}{512p(p+1)(p-1)^2}.
$$

Final Answer: $\boxed{\frac{G_{32}(63p^2+63p+2)}{512p(p+1)(p-1)^2}}$
