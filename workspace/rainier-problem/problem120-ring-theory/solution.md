## Steps

Step 1: Normalize the trace-zero direction of each quadratic field

Put
$$
G=|\mathrm{GL}_{1024}(\mathbb F_p)|
$$
and fix a nonsquare $d\in\mathbb F_p^\times$. If $E\subset M_{1024}(\mathbb F_p)$ is a unital field of order $p^2$, then $W=\mathbb F_p^{1024}$ has dimension $512$ over $E$. Hence for $x\in E$, the matrix trace of multiplication by $x$ on $W$ is
$$
512\operatorname{Tr}_{E/\mathbb F_p}(x).
$$
Since $p$ is odd, the matrix-trace-zero part of $E$ is exactly its one-dimensional field-trace-zero line.

If $0\ne X$ lies on that line, then $X^2=aI_{1024}$ for a nonsquare $a$. Since $d/a$ is a square, the line contains exactly two matrices $\pm X_E$ satisfying
$$
X_E^2=dI_{1024}.
$$
Conversely, every $X$ with $X^2=dI_{1024}$ generates a quadratic field and has trace zero.

The statement imposes anticommutation on incidence edges and commutation on nonedges; these are relation requirements, not an assertion that special elements such as $0$ cannot satisfy both identities. Thus the desired field families are obtained from normalized generators indexed by the $30$ vertices of the incidence graph of $W(3,2)$, each squaring to $dI_{1024}$, with generators anticommuting across an incidence edge and commuting otherwise, by dividing by $2^{30}$.

Step 2: Compute the binary incidence kernel of $W(3,2)$

Let $V=\mathbb F_2^4$ with symplectic form
$$
\langle x,y\rangle=x_1y_2+x_2y_1+x_3y_4+x_4y_3.
$$
The points are the $15$ nonzero vectors of $V$, and the lines are the two-dimensional totally isotropic subspaces, each contributing its three nonzero vectors. Each point lies on three such lines, so there are also $15$ lines.

Let $B$ be the $15\times15$ line-point incidence matrix over $\mathbb F_2$. We determine
$$
K=\ker B.
$$
Set
$$
q(x)=x_1x_2+x_3x_4,
$$
so
$$
q(x+y)+q(x)+q(y)=\langle x,y\rangle.
$$
For every linear form $\ell:V\to\mathbb F_2$ and every $\lambda\in\mathbb F_2$, the function
$$
f(x)=\lambda q(x)+\ell(x)\qquad(x\ne0)
$$
lies in $K$, because on an isotropic line $\{x,y,x+y\}$ one has $\langle x,y\rangle=0$.

These functions form a $5$-dimensional space. To see that there are no others, choose a symplectic basis $e_1,f_1,e_2,f_2$. For $f\in K$, define
$$
\lambda=f(e_1)+f(f_1)+f(e_1+f_1)
$$
and choose a linear form $\ell$ agreeing with $f$ on the four basis vectors. Then
$$
g=f+\lambda q+\ell
$$
vanishes on $e_1,f_1,e_2,f_2,e_1+f_1$. The isotropic-line relations first give
$$
g(e_1+e_2)=g(e_1+f_2)=g(f_1+e_2)=g(f_1+f_2)=0.
$$
The vectors $e_1+e_2$ and $f_1+f_2$ are orthogonal, so
$$
g(e_1+f_1+e_2+f_2)=0.
$$
Since $e_1+f_1$ is orthogonal to $e_2+f_2$, this forces $g(e_2+f_2)=0$. Every vector of $V$ is the sum of a vector in $\langle e_1,f_1\rangle$ and one in $\langle e_2,f_2\rangle$, and those two summands are orthogonal, so the same line relation gives $g=0$ everywhere. Hence
$$
\dim K=5,
\qquad
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb F_2}B=10.
$$

Also every vector in $K$ has even weight: summing the line equations over all $15$ lines counts every point three times. The same argument applies to $\ker B^T$.

Step 3: Determine the center of the graph-commutation algebra

Let $A$ be the $\mathbb F_p$-algebra generated by one element $e_v$ for each of the $30$ point and line vertices, with
$$
e_v^2=d,
$$
with $e_ve_w=-e_we_v$ across an incidence edge and $e_ve_w=e_we_v$ otherwise. Ordering the generators shows that the $2^{30}$ squarefree monomials form a spanning set.

The commutation form over $\mathbb F_2$ has matrix
$$
H=\begin{pmatrix}0&B^T\\B&0\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Therefore
$$
\operatorname{rad}H=\ker B\oplus\ker B^T
$$
has dimension $10$. A monomial is central exactly when its support vector lies in this radical.

For $(r,s)\in\ker B\oplus\ker B^T$, the square of the corresponding central monomial is
$$
(-1)^{s^TBr}d^{|r|+|s|}.
$$
Here $Br=0$, and both $|r|$ and $|s|$ are even, so this square is a square in $\mathbb F_p^\times$. After rescaling a basis of the radical, we obtain ten commuting central involutions. Hence
$$
Z(A)\cong \mathbb F_p[(C_2)^{10}]\cong \mathbb F_p^{1024}.
$$
Thus $A$ has $1024$ primitive central blocks. Since the quotient of the commutation space by its radical has dimension
$$
30-10=20,
$$
each block has dimension $2^{20}$ over $\mathbb F_p$.

Step 4: Identify every central block with $M_{1024}(\mathbb F_p)$

The induced alternating form on
$$
\mathbb F_2^{30}/\operatorname{rad}H
$$
is nondegenerate of dimension $20$. Choose a symplectic basis
$$
a_1,b_1,\dots,a_{10},b_{10}.
$$
Lifting these vectors to monomials inside a fixed central block gives elements $A_i,B_i$ such that distinct pairs commute, while
$$
A_iB_i=-B_iA_i,
\qquad
A_i^2=q_iI,
\qquad
B_i^2=r_iI
$$
for some $q_i,r_i\in\mathbb F_p^\times$.

For arbitrary nonzero $q,r\in\mathbb F_p$, the algebra on two generators with squares $q,r$ and anticommutation is $M_2(\mathbb F_p)$. If $q=s^2$ is a square, use
$$
s\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\begin{pmatrix}0&r\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.
$$
If $q$ is a nonsquare, use
$$
\begin{pmatrix}0&q\\1&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\begin{pmatrix}a&-qc\\c&-a\end{pmatrix},
$$
where $a^2-qc^2=r$; such $a,c$ exist because the norm map $\mathbb F_{p^2}^\times\to\mathbb F_p^\times$ is onto.

Therefore each central block is a tensor product of ten copies of $M_2(\mathbb F_p)$, hence
$$
M_{1024}(\mathbb F_p).
$$
Consequently
$$
A\cong \bigoplus_{1024}M_{1024}(\mathbb F_p).
$$

Step 5: Count the $1024$-dimensional representations

A normalized generator family gives a unital representation of $A$ on $W=\mathbb F_p^{1024}$. A nonzero module for any factor $M_{1024}(\mathbb F_p)$ has dimension at least $1024$. Hence exactly one of the $1024$ simple factors acts nontrivially, and it acts by its natural module.

Thus there are exactly $1024$ module types, each forming one $\mathrm{GL}_{1024}(\mathbb F_p)$-conjugacy orbit. The stabilizer of one normalized family is the scalar group $\mathbb F_p^\times$, of size $p-1$. Therefore the number of normalized generator families is
$$
1024\frac{G}{p-1}.
$$

Step 6: Divide by the independent sign choices

Each of the $30$ quadratic fields has exactly two normalized trace-zero generators, so each field family corresponds to $2^{30}$ normalized generator families. Therefore the required number is
$$
\frac{1024G}{2^{30}(p-1)}
=\frac{G}{2^{20}(p-1)}.
$$

Final Answer: $\boxed{\frac{G}{2^{20}(p-1)}}$

## Black-Box Audit

The binary incidence kernel is derived directly from the quadratic refinements of the symplectic form. The center and all $1024$ matrix blocks are then obtained explicitly from the radical and a symplectic basis, without using a classification theorem for central simple algebras.
