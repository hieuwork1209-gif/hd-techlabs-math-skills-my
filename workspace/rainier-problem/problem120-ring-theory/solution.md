## Steps

Step 1: Normalize the trace-zero generator of each quadratic field

Fix a nonsquare $d\in\mathbb F_p^\times$. Let $E\subset M_4(\mathbb F_p)$ be a unital field of order $p^2$. The natural $E$-action on $W=\mathbb F_p^4$ makes $W$ a two-dimensional vector space over $E$. Hence for $x\in E$, the matrix trace of multiplication by $x$ on $W$ is twice the field trace $\operatorname{Tr}_{E/\mathbb F_p}(x)$.

Therefore the trace-zero part of $E$ is a one-dimensional $\mathbb F_p$-subspace. If $0\ne X$ lies on that line, then its minimal polynomial over $\mathbb F_p$ is $t^2-a$ with $a$ a nonsquare, so $X^2=aI_4$. Since $d/a$ is a square, the line contains exactly two matrices $\pm X_E$ satisfying
$$
X_E^2=dI_4.
$$
Conversely, if $X^2=dI_4$, then $t^2-d$ is irreducible and $\mathbb F_p[I_4,X]$ is a field of order $p^2$; its characteristic polynomial is $(t^2-d)^2$, so $\operatorname{tr}(X)=0$.

Thus the desired field triples are obtained from ordered triples $(X_1,X_2,X_3)$ satisfying
$$
X_i^2=dI_4,\qquad X_iX_j=-X_jX_i\quad(i\ne j),
$$
by dividing the number of such normalized generator triples by $2^3=8$.

Step 2: Introduce the universal anticommuting algebra

Let $C$ be the $\mathbb F_p$-algebra generated by $e_1,e_2,e_3$ with relations
$$
e_i^2=d,\qquad e_ie_j=-e_je_i\quad(i\ne j).
$$
Every word reduces to a linear combination of
$$
1,e_1,e_2,e_3,e_1e_2,e_2e_3,e_3e_1,\omega,
\qquad \omega=e_1e_2e_3,
$$
so $\dim_{\mathbb F_p}C\le8$. The element $\omega$ is central and
$$
\omega^2=-d^3.
$$
The arithmetic of $-d^3$ is the only place where the two congruence classes of $p$ diverge.

Step 3: Count normalized triples when $p\equiv1\pmod4$

Here $-1$ is a square, so $-d^3$ is a nonsquare. Hence
$$
K=\mathbb F_p(\omega)\cong\mathbb F_{p^2}.
$$
Every element of $\mathbb F_p^\times$ is a square in $K^\times$, so choose $s,c\in K^\times$ with
$$
s^2=d,\qquad c^2=-\frac1d.
$$
In $M_2(K)$ put
$$
U=s\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
V=s\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
T=cUV.
$$
Then $U^2=V^2=T^2=dI_2$, the three matrices pairwise anticommute, and $U,V$ generate $M_2(K)$ as a $K$-algebra. Moreover
$$
UVT=-cd^2I_2
$$
has square $-d^3$, so it generates $K$ over $\mathbb F_p$. Therefore the induced map $C\to M_2(K)$ is onto. Since both sides have dimension at most and at least $8$ over $\mathbb F_p$, respectively,
$$
C\cong M_2(K).
$$

A normalized triple in $M_4(\mathbb F_p)$ is therefore a unital representation of $M_2(K)$ on $W=\mathbb F_p^4$. The center makes $W$ two-dimensional over $K$, and the standard matrix-unit argument shows that every such module is the natural $K^2$ module. Hence all normalized triples form one $\mathrm{GL}_4(\mathbb F_p)$-orbit. Its stabilizer is the scalar group $K^\times$, of size $p^2-1$. Thus
$$
N_{\mathrm{norm}}=\frac{|\mathrm{GL}_4(\mathbb F_p)|}{p^2-1},
$$
and after dividing by $8$,
$$
N_{p\equiv1(4)}
=\frac{p^6(p-1)^3(p+1)(p^2+1)(p^2+p+1)}8.
$$

Step 4: Split the algebra when $p\equiv3\pmod4$

Now both $-1$ and $d$ are nonsquares, so $-d^3$ is a square. Choose $\lambda\in\mathbb F_p^\times$ with
$$
\lambda^2=-d^3.
$$
We show directly that
$$
C\cong M_2(\mathbb F_p)\oplus M_2(\mathbb F_p).
$$

Let $L=\mathbb F_p(\sqrt d)$. The norm map $L^\times\to\mathbb F_p^\times$ is surjective, so choose $a,c\in\mathbb F_p$ with
$$
a^2-dc^2=d.
$$
Set
$$
U=\begin{pmatrix}0&d\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
V=\begin{pmatrix}a&-dc\\c&-a\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Then
$$
U^2=V^2=dI_2,\qquad UV=-VU,
$$
and $U,V$ generate $M_2(\mathbb F_p)$.

For $\sigma\in\{+1,-1\}$ define
$$
T_\sigma=-\frac{\sigma\lambda}{d^2}UV.
$$
Since $(UV)^2=-d^2I_2$, one has $T_\sigma^2=dI_2$, and $T_\sigma$ anticommutes with both $U$ and $V$. Also
$$
UVT_\sigma=\sigma\lambda I_2.
$$
Thus there are onto homomorphisms
$$
\phi_\sigma:C\to M_2(\mathbb F_p),
\qquad
(e_1,e_2,e_3)\mapsto(U,V,T_\sigma).
$$
The central idempotents
$$
z_\pm=\frac12\left(1\pm\frac{\omega}{\lambda}\right)
$$
map under $(\phi_+,\phi_-)$ to $(I_2,0)$ and $(0,I_2)$. Hence the combined map
$$
C\to M_2(\mathbb F_p)\oplus M_2(\mathbb F_p)
$$
is onto. The target has dimension $8$, while $\dim C\le8$, so it is an isomorphism.

Step 5: Count the three module types when $p\equiv3\pmod4$

For a normalized triple, let $W_\pm=z_\pm W$. Then
$$
W=W_+\oplus W_-.
$$
A nonzero module for $M_2(\mathbb F_p)$ has even dimension: using the standard matrix units, the two diagonal idempotent images have equal dimension. Since $\dim_{\mathbb F_p}W=4$, the only possibilities are
$$
(\dim W_+,\dim W_-)=(4,0),(2,2),(0,4).
$$
For each dimension pair there is exactly one module isomorphism type, hence exactly one $\mathrm{GL}_4(\mathbb F_p)$-orbit of normalized triples.

For types $(4,0)$ and $(0,4)$, one copy of $M_2(\mathbb F_p)$ acts on two copies of its natural module, so the invertible commutant is $\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb F_p)$. For type $(2,2)$, each simple factor acts once, so the invertible commutant is $(\mathbb F_p^\times)^2$. Therefore
$$
N_{\mathrm{norm}}
=2\frac{|\mathrm{GL}_4(\mathbb F_p)|}{|\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb F_p)|}
+\frac{|\mathrm{GL}_4(\mathbb F_p)|}{(p-1)^2}.
$$
Using
$$
|\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb F_p)|=p(p-1)^2(p+1),
$$
and dividing by $8$, we obtain
$$
N_{p\equiv3(4)}
=\frac{p^5(p-1)^2(p+1)(p^2+1)(p^2+p+1)(p^2+p+2)}8.
$$

Step 6: Combine the two congruence classes

Let
$$
\chi=(-1)^{(p-1)/2}.
$$
The factor distinguishing the two cases is
$$
\begin{cases}
p(p-1),&\chi=1,\\
p^2+p+2,&\chi=-1,
\end{cases}
$$
which equals
$$
p^2+1-(p+1)\chi.
$$
Hence the number of ordered triples is
$$
\frac{p^5(p-1)^2(p+1)(p^2+1)(p^2+p+1)\bigl(p^2+1-(p+1)(-1)^{(p-1)/2}\bigr)}8.
$$

Final Answer: $\boxed{\frac{p^5(p-1)^2(p+1)(p^2+1)(p^2+p+1)(p^2+1-(p+1)(-1)^{(p-1)/2})}{8}}$
