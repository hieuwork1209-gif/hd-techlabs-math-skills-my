## Steps

Step 1: Normalize the trace-zero direction of each quadratic field

Put
$$
G_{16}=|\mathrm{GL}_{16}(\mathbb F_p)|
$$
and fix a nonsquare $d\in\mathbb F_p^\times$. If $E\subset M_{16}(\mathbb F_p)$ is a unital field of order $p^2$, then $W=\mathbb F_p^{16}$ has dimension $8$ over $E$. Hence for $x\in E$, the matrix trace of multiplication by $x$ on $W$ is
$$
8\operatorname{Tr}_{E/\mathbb F_p}(x).
$$
Since $p$ is odd, the matrix-trace-zero part of $E$ is exactly its one-dimensional field-trace-zero line.

If $0\ne X$ lies on that line, then $X^2=aI_{16}$ for a nonsquare $a$. Since $d/a$ is a square, the line contains exactly two matrices $\pm X_E$ satisfying
$$
X_E^2=dI_{16}.
$$
Conversely, every $X$ with $X^2=dI_{16}$ generates a quadratic field and has trace zero.

Thus the desired field sextuples are obtained from normalized generator sextuples $(X_1,\dots,X_6)$ with $X_i^2=dI_{16}$, with adjacent indices on the cycle $C_6$ anticommuting and nonadjacent distinct indices commuting, by dividing by $2^6=64$.

Step 2: Form the universal cycle algebra and find its central directions

Let $A$ be generated by $e_1,\dots,e_6$ with
$$
e_i^2=d,
$$
with $e_i e_j=-e_j e_i$ when $i,j$ are adjacent on the cycle $C_6$, and $e_i e_j=e_j e_i$ for all other distinct pairs. Reordering words shows that $A$ is spanned by the $64$ ordered squarefree monomials.

Set
$$
u=e_1e_3e_5,\qquad v=e_2e_4e_6.
$$
Each generator has either zero or two neighbors in the support of $u$, and likewise for $v$, so both $u$ and $v$ are central. The factors within each product commute, hence
$$
u^2=v^2=d^3.
$$
Because $d^3$ is a nonsquare, each of $u$ and $v$ generates a copy of $\mathbb F_{p^2}$.

Step 3: Identify the cycle algebra explicitly

Let
$$
K=\mathbb F_p(s),\qquad s^2=d,
$$
so $K\cong\mathbb F_{p^2}$. In $M_2(K)$ put
$$
Z=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
X=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Thus $Z^2=X^2=I_2$ and $ZX=-XZ$. In $M_4(K)=M_2(K)\otimes M_2(K)$ define
$$
Y_1=s(Z\otimes I),\quad
Y_2=s(X\otimes I),\quad
Y_3=s(Z\otimes Z),
$$
$$
Y_4=s(I\otimes X),\quad
Y_5=s(I\otimes Z),\quad
Y_6^{\pm}=\pm s(X\otimes X).
$$
For either sign, these six matrices square to $dI_4$ and have exactly the commutation pattern of $C_6$. Moreover
$$
Y_1Y_3Y_5=s^3I_4,
\qquad
Y_2Y_4Y_6^{\pm}=\pm s^3I_4.
$$
The first, second, fourth, and fifth matrices generate the two tensor factors once the scalar $s$ is available, while $s^3I_4$ generates $K$ over $\mathbb F_p$. Hence each sign gives a surjection
$$
\phi_\pm:A\to M_4(K).
$$

The central element $vu^{-1}$ maps to $+I_4$ under $\phi_+$ and to $-I_4$ under $\phi_-$. Since $p$ is odd, the idempotents
$$
\frac12(1\pm vu^{-1})
$$
separate the two images. Therefore the combined map
$$
(\phi_+,\phi_-):A\to M_4(K)\oplus M_4(K)
$$
is onto. The target has dimension $64$ over $\mathbb F_p$, equal to the spanning bound for $A$, so
$$
A\cong M_4(K)\oplus M_4(K).
$$

Step 4: Classify the representations on $\mathbb F_p^{16}$

A normalized sextuple gives a unital representation of $A$ on $W=\mathbb F_p^{16}$. A simple module for either factor $M_4(K)$ is $K^4$, which has dimension $8$ over $\mathbb F_p$. Since $W$ has dimension $16$, the two simple-factor multiplicities can only be
$$
(2,0),\qquad(1,1),\qquad(0,2).
$$
For each multiplicity pair there is exactly one module isomorphism type, hence one $\mathrm{GL}_{16}(\mathbb F_p)$-conjugacy orbit of normalized sextuples.

For types $(2,0)$ and $(0,2)$, the invertible commutant is $\mathrm{GL}_2(K)$. For type $(1,1)$, the invertible commutant is $K^\times\times K^\times$. Thus
$$
N_{\mathrm{norm}}
=2\frac{G_{16}}{|\mathrm{GL}_2(K)|}
+\frac{G_{16}}{(p^2-1)^2}.
$$

Step 5: Simplify and divide by the sign choices

Since $|K|=p^2$,
$$
|\mathrm{GL}_2(K)|
=p^2(p^2-1)^2(p^2+1).
$$
Therefore
$$
N_{\mathrm{norm}}
=G_{16}\left(
\frac{2}{p^2(p^2-1)^2(p^2+1)}
+\frac1{(p^2-1)^2}
\right)
$$
$$
=G_{16}\frac{p^4+p^2+2}{p^2(p^2+1)(p^2-1)^2}.
$$
Each ordered field sextuple has exactly $2^6=64$ normalized generator sextuples, so the required number is
$$
\frac{G_{16}(p^4+p^2+2)}{64p^2(p^2+1)(p^2-1)^2}.
$$

Final Answer: $\boxed{\frac{G_{16}(p^4+p^2+2)}{64p^2(p^2+1)(p^2-1)^2}}$
