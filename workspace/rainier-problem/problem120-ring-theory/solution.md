## Steps

Step 1: Normalize the trace-zero generator of each quadratic field

Fix a nonsquare $d\in\mathbb F_p^\times$. Let $E\subset M_4(\mathbb F_p)$ be a unital field of order $p^2$. The natural $E$-action on $W=\mathbb F_p^4$ makes $W$ a two-dimensional vector space over $E$. Hence for $x\in E$, the matrix trace of multiplication by $x$ on $W$ is twice the field trace $\operatorname{Tr}_{E/\mathbb F_p}(x)$.

Therefore the trace-zero part of $E$ is a one-dimensional $\mathbb F_p$-subspace. If $0\ne X$ lies on that line, then its minimal polynomial over $\mathbb F_p$ is
$$
t^2-a
$$
with $a$ a nonsquare, so $X^2=aI_4$. Since $d/a$ is a square, the line contains exactly two matrices $\pm X_E$ satisfying
$$
X_E^2=dI_4.
$$
Thus every field $E$ has exactly two normalized trace-zero generators.

Conversely, if $X\in M_4(\mathbb F_p)$ satisfies $X^2=dI_4$, then $t^2-d$ is irreducible, so $\mathbb F_p[I_4,X]$ is a field of order $p^2$. Its characteristic polynomial is $(t^2-d)^2$, hence $\operatorname{tr}(X)=0$.

Thus the desired field triples are obtained from ordered triples $(X_1,X_2,X_3)$ satisfying
$$
X_i^2=dI_4,\qquad X_iX_j=-X_jX_i\quad(i\ne j),
$$
by dividing the number of such normalized generator triples by $2^3$.

Step 2: Identify the universal algebra generated by a normalized triple

Let $C$ be the $\mathbb F_p$-algebra generated by $e_1,e_2,e_3$ with relations
$$
e_i^2=d,\qquad e_ie_j=-e_je_i\quad(i\ne j).
$$
Every word reduces to an $\mathbb F_p$-linear combination of
$$
1,e_1,e_2,e_3,e_1e_2,e_2e_3,e_3e_1,\omega,
\qquad \omega=e_1e_2e_3,
$$
so $\dim_{\mathbb F_p}C\le8$. The element $\omega$ is central and
$$
\omega^2=-d^3.
$$
Because $p\equiv1\pmod4$, $-1$ is a square in $\mathbb F_p$. Since $d$ is a nonsquare, $-d^3$ is a nonsquare. Hence
$$
K=\mathbb F_p(\omega)\cong\mathbb F_{p^2}.
$$

Step 3: Split $C$ explicitly over its center

Every element of $\mathbb F_p^\times$ is a square in $K^\times$, so choose $s,c\in K^\times$ with
$$
s^2=d,\qquad c^2=-\frac1d.
$$
In $M_2(K)$ put
$$
U=s\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
V=s\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
T=cUV.
$$
Then
$$
U^2=V^2=T^2=dI_2
$$
and the three matrices pairwise anticommute. Moreover $U,V$ generate $M_2(K)$ as a $K$-algebra, while
$$
UVT=-cd^2I_2
$$
has square $-d^3$ and therefore generates $K$ over $\mathbb F_p$. Thus the induced homomorphism
$$
C\longrightarrow M_2(K)
$$
is onto. Since the target has dimension $8$ over $\mathbb F_p$ and $C$ has dimension at most $8$, it is an isomorphism:
$$
C\cong M_2(K).
$$
In particular, $C$ is simple.

Step 4: Classify all normalized triples in $M_4(\mathbb F_p)$

A normalized triple $(X_1,X_2,X_3)$ gives a unital homomorphism
$$
\rho:C\to M_4(\mathbb F_p),\qquad e_i\mapsto X_i.
$$
Since $C$ is simple and $\rho(1)=I_4$, the map is injective. Hence $W=\mathbb F_p^4$ is a left $C\cong M_2(K)$-module.

The center $K$ acts on $W$, so $W$ is a two-dimensional vector space over $K$. The image of $C$ consists of $K$-linear endomorphisms of $W$; both $C$ and $\operatorname{End}_K(W)$ have dimension $4$ over $K$. Therefore
$$
\rho(C)=\operatorname{End}_K(W)\cong M_2(K).
$$
Let $f_{ij}$ be the standard matrix units of $M_2(K)$. Then $f_{11}W$ and $f_{22}W$ are nonzero complementary $K$-subspaces, and $f_{12},f_{21}$ give inverse isomorphisms between them. Since $\dim_KW=2$, both are one-dimensional. Choosing $0\ne w\in f_{11}W$, the basis $(w,f_{21}w)$ identifies the action with the standard action of $M_2(K)$ on $K^2$. Hence every such $C$-module is isomorphic to the natural two-dimensional $K$-module, so any two normalized triples are conjugate by an element of $\mathrm{GL}_4(\mathbb F_p)$.

Step 5: Compute the stabilizer of one normalized triple

The stabilizer under conjugation is the group of invertible $\mathbb F_p$-linear maps commuting with $\rho(C)=\operatorname{End}_K(W)$. Any such map first commutes with the scalar action of $K$, so it is $K$-linear. The centralizer of the full matrix algebra $\operatorname{End}_K(W)$ inside itself consists only of the scalar maps. Hence the stabilizer is
$$
K^\times,
$$
which has size $p^2-1$.

By orbit-stabilizer, the number of normalized ordered generator triples is therefore
$$
\frac{|\mathrm{GL}_4(\mathbb F_p)|}{p^2-1}.
$$
Each ordered field triple has exactly $2^3=8$ normalized generator triples, so the required number is
$$
\frac{|\mathrm{GL}_4(\mathbb F_p)|}{8(p^2-1)}.
$$

Step 6: Simplify the exact count

Using
$$
|\mathrm{GL}_4(\mathbb F_p)|
=(p^4-1)(p^4-p)(p^4-p^2)(p^4-p^3),
$$
we obtain
$$
\frac{|\mathrm{GL}_4(\mathbb F_p)|}{8(p^2-1)}
=\frac{p^6(p-1)^3(p+1)(p^2+1)(p^2+p+1)}8.
$$

Final Answer: $\boxed{\frac{p^6(p-1)^3(p+1)(p^2+1)(p^2+p+1)}8}$
