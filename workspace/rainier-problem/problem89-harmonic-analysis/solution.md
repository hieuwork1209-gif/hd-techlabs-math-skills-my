## Steps

Step 1: Prove the rank-one and rank-two Walsh uncertainty bounds

Let
$$
V=\mathbb F_2^5,
\qquad
(\mathcal Fu)(\xi)=\frac1{\sqrt{32}}\sum_{x\in V}u(x)(-1)^{\xi(x)}.
$$
For $x,y\in V$,
$$
\sum_{\xi\in V^*}(-1)^{\xi(x)+\xi(y)}
$$
is $32$ when $x=y$ and $0$ otherwise, so $\mathcal F$ is orthogonal.

For a nonzero linear subspace $L\leq\mathcal U$, let $S=S(L)$ and $T=T(L)$, and let $P_S,P_T$ be the corresponding coordinate projections. Put
$$
A=P_S\mathcal F^{-1}P_T\mathcal F P_S.
$$
Every $u\in L$ is supported on $S$ and has Fourier support in $T$, hence $Au=u$. Therefore $A$ has eigenvalue $1$ with multiplicity at least $\dim L$. Since every normalized Walsh entry has squared modulus $1/32$,
$$
\operatorname{tr}A=\frac{|S||T|}{32}.
$$
Thus
$$
|S||T|\geq32\dim L.
$$
For a line this gives the rank-one bound
$$
|S||T|\geq32,
$$
and for a two-plane,
$$
|S||T|\geq64.
$$
Hence $U_2^*\geq64$.

Step 2: Classify every rank-one equality line

Let $\ell=\mathbb R f\leq\mathcal U$ satisfy
$$
|S(\ell)|\,|T(\ell)|=32.
$$
Then $\operatorname{tr}A=1$, and $A$ already has one eigenvalue $1$. Since $A$ is positive semidefinite, $\operatorname{rank}A=1$. Let $M$ be the $T\times S$ normalized Walsh submatrix, so $A=M^*M$ and $\operatorname{rank}M=1$.

Choose $x_0\in S$ and normalize every row by its $x_0$-entry:
$$
r_\xi=\left((-1)^{\xi(x-x_0)}\right)_{x\in S}.
$$
All normalized rows have first coordinate $1$, and rank one forces them all to be identical.

Let
$$
W=\operatorname{span}(S-S),
\qquad
j=\dim W.
$$
Two normalized rows agree exactly when their indices have the same restriction to $W$. Hence all $\xi\in T$ lie in a single restriction class
$$
T\subset \gamma+W^\perp
$$
for some $\gamma\in V^*$. Also
$$
S\subset x_0+W.
$$
Therefore
$$
|S|\leq2^j,
\qquad
|T|\leq2^{5-j}.
$$
The product of the upper bounds is $32$, and equality is assumed, so both bounds are equalities:
$$
S=x_0+W,
\qquad
T=\gamma+W^\perp.
$$

The one-dimensional intersection of these support conditions is generated by
$$
f(x)=(-1)^{\gamma(x)}\mathbf 1_{x_0+W}(x).
$$
Because $f\in\mathcal U$, we must have $0\notin S$ and $0\notin T$, equivalently
$$
x_0\notin W,
\qquad
\gamma|_W\neq0.
$$
Thus $1\leq j\leq4$. Conversely every such affine-character line lies in $\mathcal U$ and attains product $32$.

Step 3: Classify every minimizing two-plane

Now let $L\leq\mathcal U$ be two-dimensional with
$$
|S(L)|\,|T(L)|=64.
$$
Then $\operatorname{tr}A=2$, while $A$ already has two eigenvalues equal to $1$. Hence $\operatorname{rank}A=2$.

Let $M$ again be the normalized Walsh submatrix. Choose $x_0\in S$ and normalized sign rows
$$
r_\xi=\left((-1)^{\xi(x-x_0)}\right)_{x\in S}.
$$
Choose independent rows $r_1,r_2$. Any other normalized row is $ar_1+br_2$. Its first coordinate gives $a+b=1$. At a coordinate where $r_1,r_2$ have opposite signs, the sign condition gives $a-b=\pm1$. Thus
$$
(a,b)=(1,0)
\quad\text{or}\quad
(a,b)=(0,1).
$$
So exactly two normalized row types occur.

Put
$$
W=\operatorname{span}(S-S),
\qquad
k=\dim W.
$$
As in Step 2, normalized row types are exactly restriction classes on $W$. Hence there are distinct restrictions $\alpha,\beta\in W^*$ such that
$$
T\subset (\widetilde\alpha+W^\perp)\sqcup(\widetilde\beta+W^\perp),
$$
where $\widetilde\alpha,\widetilde\beta$ are any extensions to $V^*$. Also
$$
S\subset x_0+W.
$$
Therefore
$$
|S|\leq2^k,
\qquad
|T|\leq2^{6-k}.
$$
Their product upper bound is $64$, so equality of $|S||T|$ forces equality in both:
$$
S=x_0+W,
$$
$$
T=(\widetilde\alpha+W^\perp)\sqcup(\widetilde\beta+W^\perp).
$$

Define
$$
f_\alpha(x)=(-1)^{\widetilde\alpha(x)}\mathbf 1_{x_0+W}(x),
\qquad
f_\beta(x)=(-1)^{\widetilde\beta(x)}\mathbf 1_{x_0+W}(x).
$$
Their Fourier supports are precisely the two cosets above. For any $u$ supported on $S$,
$
\langle Au,u\rangle
=\|P_T\mathcal Fu\|^2
\leq\|u\|^2.
$
Thus $Au=u$ holds exactly when equality holds in this projection inequality, equivalently when $\mathcal Fu$ is supported on $T$. Hence the full space of functions supported on $S$ with Fourier support in $T$ is precisely the $1$-eigenspace of $A$. Since $A$ is positive semidefinite of rank $2$ and trace $2$, that eigenspace has dimension $2$. Therefore
$
L=\operatorname{span}\{f_\alpha,f_\beta\}.
$
The conditions $L\leq\mathcal U$ give
$$
x_0\notin W,
\qquad
\alpha\neq0,
\qquad
\beta\neq0.
$$
The two restrictions are distinct. Hence
$$
2\leq k\leq4.
$$
Conversely every such choice gives a minimizing two-plane. Thus
$$
U_2^*=64.
$$

Step 4: Count all minimizing two-planes

For fixed $k$, the number of $k$-dimensional subspaces $W\leq V$ is
$$
\binom{5}{k}_2
=\prod_{i=0}^{k-1}\frac{2^5-2^i}{2^k-2^i}.
$$
There are
$$
2^{5-k}-1
$$
affine cosets $x_0+W$ avoiding $0$, and
$$
\binom{2^k-1}{2}
$$
unordered pairs of distinct nonzero restrictions in $W^*$.

The physical support recovers the affine coset and $W$, while the Fourier support recovers the unordered pair of restriction classes, so every minimizing plane is counted once. Therefore
$$
N_2^*
=\sum_{k=2}^4
\binom{5}{k}_2
(2^{5-k}-1)
\binom{2^k-1}{2}.
$$
Since
$$
\binom52_2=\binom53_2=155,
\qquad
\binom54_2=31,
$$
we get
$$
N_2^*
=155\cdot7\cdot3
+155\cdot3\cdot21
+31\cdot105
=16275.
$$

Step 5: Find the rank-one extremal lines inside a minimizing two-plane

Fix a minimizing plane of type $k$:
$$
L=\operatorname{span}\{f_\alpha,f_\beta\},
$$
and put
$$
\delta=\alpha+\beta\neq0.
$$
For a nonzero vector
$$
g=af_\alpha+bf_\beta,
$$
factor on $x_0+W$ as
$$
g=f_\beta\bigl(a(-1)^{\delta(\,cdot-x_0\,)}+b\bigr).
$$

If $a=0$ or $b=0$, then $g$ is one of the two affine-character generators. Its physical support has size $2^k$, its Fourier support has size $2^{5-k}$, and the product is $32$.

If $a=\pm b\neq0$, then the factor in parentheses vanishes on exactly one of the two affine halves cut out by $\delta$. Thus
$$
|S(\mathbb Rg)|=2^{k-1}.
$$
Both Fourier cosets occur, so
$$
|T(\mathbb Rg)|=2^{6-k},
$$
again giving product $32$.

If $ab\neq0$ and $a\neq\pm b$, then the factor never vanishes, so the physical support is all of $x_0+W$, while both Fourier cosets occur. Hence the product is $64$, not $32$.

Therefore every minimizing two-plane contains exactly four rank-one equality lines, represented by
$$
[1:0],\ [0:1],\ [1:1],\ [1:-1].
$$
Two have physical support dimension $k$, and two have physical support dimension $k-1$.

Step 6: Count how many minimizing planes contain a fixed rank-one extremal line

Fix a rank-one equality line
$$
\ell=\mathbb R f_{\gamma,A},
\qquad
A=x_0+W,
\qquad
\dim W=j,
$$
where $x_0\notin W$ and $\gamma\in W^*\setminus\{0\}$.

A minimizing plane containing $\ell$ can have type only $k=j$ or $k=j+1$, by Step 5.

For type $k=j$, the affine support must be exactly $A$, and $\ell$ must be one of the two full-support character lines. The second nonzero restriction may be any
$$
\beta\in W^*\setminus\{0,\gamma\},
$$
so the number of such planes is
$$
2^j-2.
$$

For type $k=j+1$, the line $\ell$ must be one of the two half-support extremal lines. Choose a $(j+1)$-dimensional subspace $W'\supset W$. There are
$$
2^{5-j}-1
$$
such one-dimensional extensions of $W$ in $V/W$. Exactly one is
$$
W+\langle x_0\rangle,
$$
for which the affine coset containing $A$ also contains $0$ and is forbidden. Hence there are
$$
2^{5-j}-2
$$
admissible choices of $W'$.

For each admissible $W'$, let $\delta\in W'^*$ be the unique nonzero functional with kernel $W$. The character $\gamma$ has exactly two extensions to $W'^*$, and they differ by $\delta$. Their unordered pair is therefore uniquely determined and produces exactly one minimizing plane whose relevant half-support line is $\ell$.

Thus the number of minimizing two-planes containing a rank-one equality line of physical support dimension $j$ is independent of the chosen line and equals
$
(2^j-2)+(2^{5-j}-2)
=2^j+2^{5-j}-4.
$
Hence for $j=1,2,3,4$, respectively, both the minimum and maximum incidence counts are
$
14,\ 8,\ 8,\ 14.
$

Final Answer: $\boxed{(64,16275,14,14,8,8,8,8,14,14)}$
