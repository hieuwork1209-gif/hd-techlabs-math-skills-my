## Steps

Step 1: Derive the sharp support-sensitive polynomial certificate
Set $a=\frac13$. For any real polynomial $r$ of degree at most $3$ with $r(a)=1$, the support condition $|X|\leq1$ gives the pointwise inequality
$$
\mathbf 1_{\{a\}}(X)\leq \frac{1-X^2}{1-a^2}r(X)^2.
$$
Indeed, both sides equal $1$ at $X=a$, while away from $a$ the left side is $0$ and the right side is nonnegative. Hence
$$
\mathbb P(X=a)\leq \frac{1}{1-a^2}\,\mathbb E\left[(1-X^2)r(X)^2\right].
$$
Because $(1-t^2)r(t)^2$ has degree at most $8$, the prescribed moments turn the right side into
$$
\frac{1}{1-a^2}\cdot\frac12\int_{-1}^1(1-t^2)r(t)^2\,dt.
$$
Thus we minimize the weighted norm
$$
\langle f,g\rangle=\frac12\int_{-1}^1(1-t^2)f(t)g(t)\,dt
$$
over cubic polynomials satisfying $r(a)=1$.

At the minimizer $r_*$, every variation $h$ of degree at most $3$ with $h(a)=0$ satisfies $\langle r_*,h\rangle=0$. Such $h$ has the form $(t-a)s(t)$ with $\deg s\leq2$. Write
$$
r_*(t)=c_3t^3+c_2t^2+c_1t+c_0.
$$
Taking $s(t)=1,t,t^2$ and evaluating the three elementary integrals gives
$$
35c_0-21c_1+7c_2-9c_3=0,
$$
$$
21c_0-7c_1+9c_2-3c_3=0,
$$
$$
7c_0-9c_1+3c_2-5c_3=0.
$$
Their one-dimensional solution space is generated by
$$
C(t)=35t^3+7t^2-21t-5.
$$
Since $C(a)=-\frac{268}{27}$, the constraint $r_*(a)=1$ gives
$$
r_*(t)=-\frac{27}{268}C(t).
$$
Now $r_*-1$ vanishes at $a$, so the same first-order orthogonality gives
$$
\langle r_*,r_*\rangle=\langle r_*,1\rangle.
$$
The odd terms of $C$ integrate to zero and
$$
\frac12\int_{-1}^1(1-t^2)C(t)\,dt
=\frac12\int_{-1}^1(-7t^4+12t^2-5)\,dt
=-\frac{12}{5}.
$$
Therefore
$$
\langle r_*,r_*\rangle
=-\frac{27}{268}\left(-\frac{12}{5}\right)
=\frac{81}{335}.
$$
As $1-a^2=\frac89$, every admissible law satisfies
$$
\mathbb P\left(X=\frac13\right)\leq \frac{9}{8}\cdot\frac{81}{335}=\frac{729}{2680}.
$$

Step 2: Determine the support forced by equality
Equality in Step 1 is possible only when the pointwise gap vanishes almost surely. Therefore every maximizing law is supported on
$$
\left\{-1,1,\frac13\right\}\cup\{t:C(t)=0\}.
$$
The sign values
$$
C\left(-\frac34\right)<0<C\left(-\frac7{10}\right),
$$
$$
C\left(-\frac14\right)>0>C\left(-\frac15\right),
$$
$$
C\left(\frac34\right)<0<C\left(\frac45\right)
$$
show that $C$ has three distinct real roots $r_1,r_2,r_3$ satisfying
$$
-\frac34<r_1<-\frac7{10},\qquad
-\frac14<r_2<-\frac15,\qquad
\frac34<r_3<\frac45.
$$
Thus the equality support consists of six distinct points
$$
Z=\left\{-1,r_1,r_2,\frac13,r_3,1\right\}.
$$

Step 3: Construct the quadrature measure on the forced support
Define the node polynomial
$$
N(t)=(t^2-1)\left(t-\frac13\right)C(t).
$$
The three orthogonality equations used to derive $C$ are exactly
$$
\frac12\int_{-1}^1N(t)s(t)\,dt=0
\qquad(\deg s\leq2).
$$
For each $z\in Z$, let
$$
\ell_z(t)=\frac{N(t)}{(t-z)N'(z)},
\qquad
w_z=\frac12\int_{-1}^1\ell_z(t)\,dt.
$$
The polynomials $\ell_z$ form the degree-$5$ Lagrange basis on $Z$. Hence every polynomial $p$ of degree at most $5$ satisfies
$$
\frac12\int_{-1}^1p(t)\,dt=\sum_{z\in Z}w_zp(z).
$$
If $\deg p\leq8$, divide $p=qN+s$ with $\deg q\leq2$ and $\deg s\leq5$. The displayed orthogonality gives
$$
\frac12\int_{-1}^1q(t)N(t)\,dt=0,
$$
and $N(z)=0$ on $Z$, so
$$
\frac12\int_{-1}^1p(t)\,dt=\sum_{z\in Z}w_zp(z).
$$
Thus any positive weights $w_z$ define a discrete probability law with exactly the required moments through degree $8$.

Step 4: Prove that all quadrature weights are positive and identify the target weight
For $z=\frac13$, the Lagrange polynomial simplifies to
$$
\ell_{1/3}(t)=\frac{(1-t^2)C(t)}{(1-a^2)C(a)}.
$$
Using the integral from Step 1,
$$
w_{1/3}
=\frac{-12/5}{(8/9)(-268/27)}
=\frac{729}{2680}.
$$
For the endpoints, direct integration of the degree-$5$ Lagrange polynomials gives
$$
w_{-1}=\frac{13}{360}>0,
\qquad
w_1=\frac7{240}>0.
$$

Now let $z$ be any of the three roots of $C$. The polynomial
$$
P_z(t)=
\frac{(1-t^2)(t-a)^2}{(1-z^2)(z-a)^2}
\left(\frac{C(t)}{(t-z)C'(z)}\right)^2
$$
has degree $8$, is nonnegative on $[-1,1]$, equals $1$ at $t=z$, and vanishes at every other node of $Z$. Applying the degree-$8$ quadrature identity from Step 3 gives
$$
w_z=\frac12\int_{-1}^1P_z(t)\,dt>0.
$$
Thus every $w_z$ is positive. Taking $p=1$ in the quadrature identity gives $\sum_{z\in Z}w_z=1$, so
$$
\mu_* = \sum_{z\in Z}w_z\delta_z
$$
is a probability distribution. Step 3 shows that its moments through degree $8$ are the required uniform moments, and the displayed value of $w_{1/3}$ shows that it attains the upper bound.

Step 5: Prove uniqueness of the maximizing distribution
Every equality case is supported on the same six-point set $Z$ by Step 2. The first six moment equations, for powers $0,1,\ldots,5$, form a Vandermonde system for the six masses on the distinct nodes in $Z$, so they have at most one solution. Step 3 constructs one such solution, namely the weights $w_z$. Therefore $\mu_*$ is the unique maximizing distribution.

Consequently the exact maximum is
$$
\frac{729}{2680}.
$$

Final Answer: $\boxed{\frac{729}{2680}}$
