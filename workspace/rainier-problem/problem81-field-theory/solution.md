## Steps

Step 1: Parameterize the lifts.
For $1\le j\le r$, write $[x]$ for the class of $x\in M^\times$ in $M^\times/(M^\times)^3$. The relation hypothesis says that the kernel of the map from the exponent space of the classes $[g_i^{(j)}]$ and $[p_j]$ is generated by the $r$ vectors that are constant $1$ in one $g^{(j)}$-family and $0$ elsewhere. Hence Kummer theory gives
$$
\operatorname{Gal}(L/M)\cong\left\{(x_i^{(j)},u_j):\sum_{i=1}^n x_i^{(j)}=0\text{ for every }j\right\},
$$
with all coordinates in $\mathbb F_3$, acting by
$$
\alpha_i^{(j)}\mapsto\omega^{x_i^{(j)}}\alpha_i^{(j)},\qquad
\rho_j\mapsto\omega^{u_j}\rho_j.
$$

Let $\sigma$ be a lift of the fixed $\pi$. Then uniquely
$$
\sigma(\alpha_i^{(j)})=\omega^{a_i^{(j)}}\alpha_{\pi(i)}^{(j)},\qquad
\sigma(\rho_j)=\omega^{s_j}\rho_j.
$$
We justify the constraints $\sum_i a_i^{(j)}=0$. Put $A_j=\prod_i\alpha_i^{(j)}\in M$. Since $A_j^3=\prod_i g_i^{(j)}$ is fixed by $S_n=\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb Q)$, it lies in $\mathbb Q^\times$. For $\tau\in\operatorname{Gal}(M/\mathbb Q(\omega))\cong S_n$, the quotient $\tau(A_j)/A_j$ lies in $\mu_3$ and defines a homomorphism $S_n\to\mu_3$. Because $S_n^{\mathrm{ab}}\cong C_2$, this homomorphism is trivial. Thus every $A_j$ is fixed, so
$$
A_j=\sigma(A_j)=\omega^{\sum_i a_i^{(j)}}A_j,
$$
and therefore $\sum_i a_i^{(j)}=0$. Conversely, the cube-class span is $\pi$-stable, so the lifts form a torsor under $\operatorname{Gal}(L/M)$; hence these are the only constraints. The requirement $\sigma(\rho_j)\ne\rho_j$ is exactly $s_j\ne0$ for every $j$.

Let
$$
G=\mathbb F_3^r,\qquad m=2q^2.
$$
For each transposition $C$ of $\pi$, define
$$
Z_C=\left(\sum_{i\in C}a_i^{(1)},\ldots,\sum_{i\in C}a_i^{(r)}\right)\in G.
$$
If $m_z$ is the number of transpositions with label $z$, then
$$
\sum_{z\in G}m_z=m,\qquad \sum_{z\in G}m_zz=0.
$$

Step 2: Convert the cycle condition to flat Fourier magnitude.
For $k=(k_1,\ldots,k_r)\in G$, the action on the fiber coordinates $u\in G$ over an ordered pair $(i,\ell)$ is
$$
(i,\ell,u)\longmapsto\left(\pi(i),\pi(\ell),u+(k_js_j+a_i^{(j)}-a_\ell^{(j)})_{j=1}^r\right).
$$
After two steps, if $i$ lies in transposition $C$ and $\ell$ in transposition $D$, the fiber is translated by
$$
2(k_1s_1,\ldots,k_rs_r)+Z_C-Z_D.
$$
A zero translation gives $3^r$ cycles of length $2$ over that base orbit; a nonzero translation has order $3$ and gives $3^{r-1}$ cycles of length $6$. Since every $s_j\ne0$, the vector $2(k_js_j)_j$ runs through all of $G$ as $k$ varies.

Define
$$
N_w=\sum_{z\in G}m_zm_{z+w}.
$$
For twist $0$, the number of base pair-orbits with zero accumulated phase is $2N_0-m$: each pair of distinct transposition blocks contributes two base orbits, while each block contributes one internal base orbit. For $w\ne0$, the number is $2N_w$. Thus all $3^r$ induced permutations have the same cycle type exactly when
$$
N_0-N_w=\frac m2=q^2\qquad(w\ne0).
$$
For a character $\chi$ of $G$, set
$$
\widehat m(\chi)=\sum_{z\in G}m_z\chi(z).
$$
The Fourier transform of $N$ is $|\widehat m(\chi)|^2$. Since $N_w$ is constant for $w\ne0$, the preceding condition is equivalent to
$$
|\widehat m(\chi)|^2=q^2
$$
for every nontrivial $\chi$.

Step 3: Classify all integral profiles.
Put $Q=3^r$ and $D=(Q-1)/2$, the number of one-dimensional directions in the dual space of $G$. The congruence $2q\equiv1\pmod Q$ implies $q\equiv2\pmod3$. Hence $x^2+x+1$ has no root modulo $q$: a root would be a nontrivial cube root of unity in $\mathbb F_q^\times$, which exists only when $3\mid q-1$. Therefore $q$ is inert, hence prime, in $\mathbb Z[\omega]$.

For nontrivial $\chi$, the Eisenstein integer $\widehat m(\chi)$ has norm $q^2$. Primality of $q$ gives
$$
\widehat m(\chi)=q\varepsilon
$$
for a unit $\varepsilon\in\{\pm1,\pm\omega,\pm\omega^2\}$. Modulo $1-\omega$, every character value is $1$, so
$$
\widehat m(\chi)\equiv\sum_zm_z=2q^2\pmod{1-\omega}.
$$
Since $q\equiv2\pmod3$, division by $q$ gives $\varepsilon\equiv1\pmod{1-\omega}$. Thus
$$
\varepsilon\in\{1,\omega,\omega^2\}.
$$

Pair each nonzero character with its inverse. Each of the $D$ character directions is represented by a nonzero linear form $\lambda:G\to\mathbb F_3$. If
$$
S_t=\sum_{\lambda(z)=t}m_z\qquad(t\in\mathbb F_3),
$$
then $S_0+S_1+S_2=2q^2$, while the two nontrivial Fourier coefficients are $q\omega^a$ and $q\omega^{-a}$ for some $a\in\mathbb F_3$. The three-term inverse Fourier transform therefore shows that exactly one of the three affine hyperplanes $\lambda(z)=t$ has total
$$
\frac{2q^2+2q}{3},
$$
and the other two each have total $(2q^2-q)/3$. Call the first hyperplane distinguished.

Choose the distinguished hyperplane in every character direction, and let $d(z)$ be the number of distinguished hyperplanes containing $z$. Grouping the Fourier inversion by the $D$ directions gives
$$
Qm_z=2q^2+q(3d(z)-D).
$$
Because $m_z$ is integral and $q$ is coprime to $Q$,
$$
2q+3d(z)-D\equiv0\pmod Q.
$$
Using $2q\equiv1\pmod Q$ and $D=(Q-1)/2$ gives
$$
d(z)\equiv\frac{3^{r-1}-1}{2}\pmod{3^{r-1}}.
$$
Since $0\le d(z)\le D$, there are only two possibilities:
$$
d(z)=d_0:=\frac{3^{r-1}-1}{2}
\quad\text{or}\quad
d(z)=D.
$$
The $D$ distinguished hyperplanes contain $D3^{r-1}$ point-hyperplane incidences. If $x$ points had $d(z)=D$, then
$$
xD+(Q-x)d_0=D3^{r-1}.
$$
Since $D-d_0=3^{r-1}$, this equation gives $x=1$. Hence there is a unique point $z_0$ with $d(z_0)=D$.

Set
$$
H=\frac{2q^2+(Q-1)q}{Q},\qquad
L_0=\frac{2q^2-q}{Q}.
$$
The inversion formula yields
$$
m_{z_0}=H,\qquad m_z=L_0\quad(z\ne z_0).
$$
These are integers because $Q\mid2q-1$, and $H-L_0=q$. Finally, $\sum_{z\in G}z=0$, so the global phase constraint gives
$$
0=\sum_zm_zz=(H-L_0)z_0=qz_0.
$$
As $q\not\equiv0\pmod3$, we get $z_0=0$. Conversely, this heavy-at-zero profile has every nontrivial Fourier coefficient equal to $H-L_0=q$, so it satisfies the required autocorrelation condition.

Step 4: Count the lifts.
The $m=2q^2$ transposition labels can be assigned in
$$
\frac{(2q^2)!}{H!(L_0!)^{Q-1}}
$$
ways. For a fixed label on one transposition, each of the $r$ coordinate pairs $(a_i^{(j)},a_{\pi(i)}^{(j)})$ has $3$ choices with prescribed sum, so there are $3^r$ internal phase choices per transposition. Thus all transpositions contribute
$$
3^{rm}=3^{2rq^2}.
$$
Finally every $s_j$ has two nonzero choices, contributing $2^r$. Therefore
$$
\boxed{2^r3^{2rq^2}\frac{(2q^2)!}{H!(L_0!)^{Q-1}}}.
$$
Final Answer: $\boxed{2^r3^{2rq^2}\frac{(2q^2)!}{H!(L_0!)^{Q-1}}}$, where $Q=3^r$, $H=(2q^2+(Q-1)q)/Q$, and $L_0=(2q^2-q)/Q$.

---

## Answer

$2^r3^{2rq^2}(2q^2)!/[H!(L_0!)^{Q-1}]$, where $Q=3^r$, $H=(2q^2+(Q-1)q)/Q$, and $L_0=(2q^2-q)/Q$.
