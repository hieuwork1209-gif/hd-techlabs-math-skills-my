# Normalized Math Problem

## LaTeX (Normalized)

Let $\ell\ge5$ be prime with $2$ generating $\mathbb F_\ell^\times$, let $r\ge3$, put $Q=\ell^r$, and suppose $q,q+2Q,q+4Q$ are prime with $2q\equiv1\pmod Q$. Put $n=4q^2$. Let $K/\mathbb Q$ be Galois with group $S_n$ on $[n]$, let $\zeta$ be a primitive $\ell$th root of unity, assume $K\cap\mathbb Q(\zeta)=\mathbb Q$, and set $M=K(\zeta)$.

For $1\le j\le r$, choose $g_i^{(j)}\in K^\times$ with $\tau(g_i^{(j)})=g_{\tau(i)}^{(j)}$ and $p_j\in\mathbb Q^\times$. Assume $\prod_i g_i^{(r)}=\prod_jp_j$, and in $M^\times/(M^\times)^\ell$ the only relations among their classes are generated by
$$
\sum_i[g_i^{(j)}]=0\ (j<r),\qquad \sum_i[g_i^{(r)}]=\sum_j[p_j].
$$
Choose $(\alpha_i^{(j)})^\ell=g_i^{(j)}$, $\rho_j^\ell=p_j$ with $\prod_i\alpha_i^{(j)}\in M$ for $j<r$ and $\prod_i\alpha_i^{(r)}=\prod_j\rho_j$. Set $L=M(\alpha_i^{(j)},\rho_j)$, $\Gamma=\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb Q(\zeta))$, and $\pi_\sigma=\sigma|_M$.

Fix $\pi\in S_n$ consisting of $2q^2$ transpositions and put
$$
\widetilde C_\pi=\{\tau\in\Gamma:\pi_\tau\in C_{S_n}(\pi)\}.
$$
For $k\in\mathbb F_\ell^r$, let
$$
\Omega_k=\left\{\left(\zeta^{u_j}\rho_j^{k_j}\frac{\alpha_i^{(j)}}{\alpha_h^{(j)}}\right)_{j=1}^r:i\ne h,\ u\in\mathbb F_\ell^r\right\}.
$$

Determine the number of $\widetilde C_\pi$-conjugacy orbits of $\sigma\in\Gamma$ with $\pi_\sigma=\pi$ and $\sigma(\rho_j)\ne\rho_j$ for all $j$ such that for some flag $W\subset U\subset\mathbb F_\ell^r$, $\operatorname{codim}U=1$, $\operatorname{codim}W=2$, the cycle type on $\Omega_k$ is constant on $W$, $U\setminus W$, and $\mathbb F_\ell^r\setminus U$. Let their $2$-cycle counts be $C_W,C_U,C_O$ and require
$$
C_W-C_U=8\ell^2Q(q+Q),\qquad C_U-C_O=8\ell Q(q+3Q).
$$
Writing $\sigma(\rho_j)=\zeta^{s_j}\rho_j$ and letting $e_r$ be the last standard basis vector, also require
$$
\left(\sum_j s_j\right)e_r\in U\setminus W.
$$
Give a closed formula.

## Domain Explanation

Kummer relations couple the total radical phase to the nonzero $\rho_j$-phases, while cycle data yield a three-level autocorrelation spectrum. Inert primes, affine phase recovery, centralizer conjugation, and finite-field incidence are load-bearing, so Field theory remains the appropriate sub-domain.
