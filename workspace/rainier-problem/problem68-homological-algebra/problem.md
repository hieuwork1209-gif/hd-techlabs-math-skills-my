# Normalized Math Problem

## LaTeX (Normalized)

Let $\Bbbk$ be a field of characteristic $2$. Fix integers
$$
a\geq21,\qquad 21\mid a,\qquad b,c\geq2.
$$
Let $R$ be the quotient of
$$
\Bbbk[x_1,\ldots,x_a,u_1,\ldots,u_b,v_1,\ldots,v_c]
$$
by the ideal generated by
$$
x_i^2,\qquad u_j^2,\qquad v_k^2
$$
for all admissible indices, by every cross-product
$$
x_i u_j,\qquad u_j v_k,\qquad v_k x_i,
$$
and by
$$
\prod_{i=1}^{a}x_i-\prod_{j=1}^{b}u_j,\qquad
\prod_{j=1}^{b}u_j-\prod_{k=1}^{c}v_k.
$$

For $1\leq i\leq a$, set
$$
X_i=\prod_{\substack{1\leq r\leq a\\r\neq i}}x_r,
$$
and extend the notation cyclically by $X_{i+a}=X_i$. In $R^a$, let
$$
g=
\begin{pmatrix}
g_1\\
\vdots\\
g_a
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
g_i=X_i+X_{i+2}+X_{i+4}+X_{i+8}+X_{i+14},
$$
where the indices on the $X_i$ are read modulo $a$.

Via the quotient sending every variable to $0$, regard $\Bbbk$ as an $R$-module. Determine, as a rational function of $t$,
$$
P_{R^a/Rg}^{R}(t)
=\sum_{n\geq0}\dim_{\Bbbk}\operatorname{Tor}_n^R(R^a/Rg,\Bbbk)t^n.
$$

## Domain Explanation

This problem is fundamentally about the structure of a commutative quotient ring $R$ and the homological invariants of the $R$-module $R^a/Rg$. The main structural work is commutative-algebraic: one analyzes the fiber-product and socle-quotient structure of $R$, its Artin local/Gorenstein behavior, and the cyclic annihilator data controlling the module. Abstract Algebra / Homological Algebra is the natural next-best comparison because the computation also uses minimal free resolutions, $\operatorname{Tor}$, $\operatorname{Ext}$, Yoneda products, and long exact $\operatorname{Ext}$ sequences. However, those homological tools are applied to and driven by the specific commutative ring and module structure, while the rational Poincare series merely packages the resulting Betti numbers $\beta_n^R(R^a/Rg)=\dim_{\Bbbk}\operatorname{Tor}_n^R(R^a/Rg,\Bbbk)$. Therefore Abstract Algebra / Commutative algebra remains the best primary classification, with Abstract Algebra / Homological Algebra as the closest alternative.
