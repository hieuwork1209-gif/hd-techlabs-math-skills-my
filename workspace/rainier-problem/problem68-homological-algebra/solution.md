## Steps

Step 1: Compute the residue-field Poincare series

For
$$
A_s=\Bbbk[z_1,\ldots,z_s]/(z_1^2,\ldots,z_s^2),
$$
tensoring the one-variable periodic resolutions gives
$$
P_{\Bbbk}^{A_s}(t)=\frac{1}{(1-t)^s}.
$$
Before the three top monomials are identified, the ring is the fiber product
$$
T=A_a\times_{\Bbbk}A_b\times_{\Bbbk}A_c.
$$
A minimal resolution over $T$ has a reduced-word basis whose adjacent nonempty blocks come from different branches. Counting those words gives
$$
\frac{1}{P_{\Bbbk}^{T}(t)}=(1-t)^a+(1-t)^b+(1-t)^c-2.
$$
Write
$$
X=\prod_{i=1}^{a}x_i,\qquad U=\prod_{j=1}^{b}u_j,\qquad V=\prod_{k=1}^{c}v_k.
$$
The kernel of $T\to R$ is the two-dimensional socle space spanned by $X-U$ and $U-V$. For a quotient by a socle class in the square of the maximal ideal, a minimal resolution is obtained by inserting a degree-$2$ separator before another copy of the old resolution. The socle annihilates the maximal ideal, so the separator construction needs no higher correction and remains minimal. With two possible separator labels, the series satisfies
$$
P_{\Bbbk}^{R}(t)=P_{\Bbbk}^{T}(t)+2t^2P_{\Bbbk}^{T}(t)P_{\Bbbk}^{R}(t).
$$
Solving this equation gives
$$
P_{\Bbbk}^{R}(t)=\frac{1}{D(t)},
$$
where
$$
D(t)=(1-t)^a+(1-t)^b+(1-t)^c-2-2t^2.
$$

Step 2: Determine the radical of the cyclic relation module

Let
$$
\omega=\prod_{i=1}^{a}x_i=\prod_{j=1}^{b}u_j=\prod_{k=1}^{c}v_k
$$
in $R$. Let $\mathfrak m$ be the maximal ideal generated by all variables, and set $N=Rg$. Every element of $\mathfrak m^2$ annihilates $g$, as do all $u_j$ and $v_k$. For $\lambda=(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_a)\in\Bbbk^a$,
$$
\left(\sum_{j=1}^{a}\lambda_jx_j\right)g
=\omega C\lambda,
$$
where
$$
(C\lambda)_i=\lambda_i+\lambda_{i+2}+\lambda_{i+4}+\lambda_{i+8}+\lambda_{i+14}
$$
with cyclic indices.

Identify cyclic vectors with $\Bbbk[s]/(s^a-1)$. Up to a cyclic reindexing, $C$ is multiplication by
$$
p(s)=1+s^2+s^4+s^8+s^{14}.
$$
In characteristic $2$,
$$
p(s)=(s^2+s+1)^4(s^3+s+1)^2.
$$
Write
$$
a=2^e m,\qquad e=v_2(a),\qquad m\text{ odd}.
$$
Because $21\mid a$, the odd integer $m$ is divisible by $21$. Hence both
$$
q_2(s)=s^2+s+1
$$
and
$$
q_3(s)=s^3+s+1
$$
divide $s^m-1$. Since in characteristic $2$
$$
s^a-1=(s^m-1)^{2^e},
$$
each irreducible factor of $s^m-1$ occurs in $s^a-1$ with multiplicity exactly $2^e$. Therefore
$$
\gcd\bigl(p(s),s^a-1\bigr)
=q_2(s)^{\min(4,2^e)}q_3(s)^{\min(2,2^e)}.
$$
For a monic polynomial $h$, the kernel of multiplication by $p$ on $\Bbbk[s]/(h)$ has dimension $\deg\gcd(p,h)$. Thus the nullity of $C$ is
$$
d=2\min(4,2^e)+3\min(2,2^e),
$$
so explicitly
$$
d=\begin{cases}
5,&e=0,\\
10,&e=1,\\
14,&e\ge2.
\end{cases}
$$
Hence
$$
r:=\operatorname{rank}C=a-d.
$$
It follows that $N/\mathfrak mN\cong\Bbbk$, while $\mathfrak mN\cong\Bbbk^r$ and is annihilated by $\mathfrak m$. Therefore
$$
0\longrightarrow\Bbbk^r\longrightarrow N\longrightarrow\Bbbk\longrightarrow0
$$
is exact.

Step 3: Compute the Yoneda quotient defined by the extension

Let
$$
E=\operatorname{Ext}_R^*(\Bbbk,\Bbbk),
\qquad H_E(t)=\frac{1}{D(t)}.
$$
The extension in Step 2 determines an $r$-dimensional subspace $W\subseteq E_1$ contained in the $x$-branch. Because the field has characteristic $2$, the $x$-branch Yoneda algebra is
$$
E_x=\Bbbk[\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_a],
$$
with every generator in degree $1$. Quotienting by the $r$ independent linear forms in $W$ leaves a polynomial algebra on $d=a-r$ degree-$1$ generators, so
$$
H_{E_x/WE_x}(t)=(1-t)^rH_{E_x}(t).
$$

Let
$$
E_T=\operatorname{Ext}_T^*(\Bbbk,\Bbbk),
\qquad H_T(t)=P_{\Bbbk}^{T}(t).
$$
In the fiber-product Yoneda algebra, the suffix after an initial $x$-block has series $H_T/H_{E_x}$. Replacing that initial block by its quotient therefore changes the full series by
$$
\left(H_{E_x/WE_x}-H_{E_x}\right)\frac{H_T}{H_{E_x}},
$$
which gives
$$
H_{E_T/WE_T}(t)=(1-t)^rH_T(t).
$$
After the two socle quotients, the separator-word description changes only the initial block, hence for
$$
Q=E/WE
$$
we get
$$
H_Q(t)=\frac{H_{E_T/WE_T}(t)}{1-2t^2H_T(t)}=(1-t)^rH_E(t)=\frac{(1-t)^r}{D(t)}.
$$

Step 4: Recover the Poincare series of the cyclic module

Write
$$
e_n=\dim_{\Bbbk}E_n,
\qquad q_n=\dim_{\Bbbk}Q_n.
$$
Applying $\operatorname{Ext}_R^*(-,\Bbbk)$ to the exact sequence in Step 2 gives connecting maps
$$
\delta_n:E_n^r\longrightarrow E_{n+1}.
$$
Yoneda multiplication identifies their images with $(WE)_{n+1}$, so
$$
\operatorname{rank}\delta_n=e_{n+1}-q_{n+1}.
$$
For $n\geq1$, exactness gives
$$
\beta_n^R(N)=q_n+re_n-e_{n+1}+q_{n+1}.
$$
Since $\beta_0^R(N)=1$, summing these dimensions yields
$$
P_N^R(t)=rH_E(t)+H_Q(t)+\frac{H_Q(t)-H_E(t)}{t}.
$$
Substituting the series from Steps 1 and 3 gives
$$
P_N^R(t)=
\frac{r+(1-t)^r+\frac{(1-t)^r-1}{t}}{D(t)}.
$$

Step 5: Pass to the presented module

Set $M=R^a/Rg$. The defining sequence
$$
0\longrightarrow N\longrightarrow R^a\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow0
$$
is minimal because every coordinate of $g$ lies in the maximal ideal. Therefore
$$
P_M^R(t)=a+tP_N^R(t).
$$
Using $r=a-d$ gives
$$
P_M^R(t)
=a+\frac{(1+t)(1-t)^r+rt-1}{D(t)},
$$
where
$$
D(t)=(1-t)^a+(1-t)^b+(1-t)^c-2-2t^2,
$$
and
$$
d=2\min(4,2^{v_2(a)})+3\min(2,2^{v_2(a)}),\qquad r=a-d.
$$
Final Answer: $\boxed{P_{R^a/Rg}^{R}(t)=a+\frac{(1+t)(1-t)^r+rt-1}{D(t)}}$

## Answer

$a+\frac{(1+t)(1-t)^r+rt-1}{D(t)}$
