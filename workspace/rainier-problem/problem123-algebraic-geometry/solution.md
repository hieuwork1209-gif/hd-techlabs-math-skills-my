## Steps

Step 1: Compute the genus of the curve and the structure of the acting group
Let
$$
h=\gcd(r,2),
\qquad
c=\gcd(r,a-1),
\qquad
d=\gcd\left(c,\frac r h\right),
\qquad
e=\gcd(r,a+1,4).
$$
Set
$$
F=\mathbb C(x),
\qquad
K=F(y,z),
$$
with
$$
y^r=x(x-1),
\qquad
z^r=x(x+1).
$$
The first degree-$r$ Kummer extension is totally ramified at $x=1$, while the second is unramified there. Hence the two cyclic extensions have trivial intersection over $F$, so
$$
[K:F]=r^2.
$$
Thus $C\to\mathbb P^1_x$ is Galois with deck group $(\mathbb Z/r\mathbb Z)^2$.

The valuation pairs of the two radicands at $0,1,-1,\infty$ are
$$
(1,1),\qquad(1,0),\qquad(0,1),\qquad(-2,-2).
$$
Therefore the inertia orders are
$$
r,\qquad r,\qquad r,\qquad \frac r h.
$$
A branch point of inertia order $m$ in a Galois cover of degree $D$ contributes $D-D/m$ to the ramification term. Hence Riemann-Hurwitz gives
$$
2g(C)-2
=-2r^2+3(r^2-r)+(r^2-rh)
=r(2r-3-h),
$$
so
$$
g(C)=1+r^2-\frac{r(3+h)}2.
$$

Let $\zeta$ be the primitive $r$-th root from the statement and define
$$
\rho(x,y,z)=(x,\zeta y,\zeta^a z),
\qquad
\tau(x,y,z)=(-x,z,y).
$$
Since $a^2\equiv1\pmod r$,
$$
\tau\rho\tau=\rho^a.
$$
Also $\rho$ has order $r$ and $\tau\notin\langle\rho\rangle$ because $\tau$ sends $x$ to $-x$. Therefore
$$
H=\langle\rho,\tau\rangle
$$
has order $2r$.

Step 2: Sum the fixed points of the nontrivial rotations
Write the deck group additively as $(\mathbb Z/r\mathbb Z)^2$. The inertia subgroups are
$$
I_0=\langle(1,1)\rangle,
\qquad
I_1=\langle(1,0)\rangle,
\qquad
I_{-1}=\langle(0,1)\rangle,
\qquad
I_\infty=\langle(-2,-2)\rangle.
$$
The numbers of points above $0,1,-1,\infty$ are respectively
$$
r,\qquad r,\qquad r,\qquad rh.
$$
For $1\leq k<r$, the deck transformation $\rho^k$ corresponds to
$$
(k,ak).
$$
Because $a^2\equiv1\pmod r$, the residue class of $a$ is a unit modulo $r$. Hence no nontrivial $\rho^k$ lies in $I_1$ or $I_{-1}$.

At $0$, the condition $\rho^k\in I_0$ is
$$
(a-1)k\equiv0\pmod r.
$$
There are exactly
$$
c=\gcd(r,a-1)
$$
solutions $k$ modulo $r$, including $k=0$. Thus $c-1$ nontrivial rotations fix all $r$ points above $0$.

At infinity, one also needs the common diagonal coordinate $k$ to lie in the subgroup generated by $2$. The solution set of $(a-1)k\equiv0\pmod r$ is a subgroup of $\mathbb Z/r\mathbb Z$ of size $c$, while the subgroup generated by $2$ has size $r/h$. Their intersection therefore has size
$$
d=\gcd\left(c,\frac r h\right).
$$
Thus $d-1$ nontrivial rotations fix all $rh$ points above infinity. The total fixed-point contribution from the nonidentity powers of $\rho$ is
$$
S_{\rm rot}=r(c-1)+rh(d-1).
$$

Step 3: Sum the fixed points of the elements outside the rotation subgroup
For $0\leq k<r$, put
$$
s_k=\tau\rho^k,
\qquad
\mu_k=\zeta^{(a-1)k},
\qquad
q=\frac yz.
$$
Since $s_k$ sends $x$ to $-x$, a fixed point can lie only above $x=0$ or $x=\infty$. Moreover
$$
s_k(q)=\mu_k q^{-1}.
$$

Above $x=0$,
$$
q^r=\frac{x-1}{x+1}\longrightarrow-1.
$$
Choose a primitive $2r$-th root $\xi$ with $\xi^2=\zeta$ and $\xi^r=-1$. The $r$ branches above $0$ have
$$
q=\xi\zeta^j,
\qquad 0\leq j<r.
$$
The fixed-point condition $q=s_k(q)$ becomes
$$
2j+1\equiv(a-1)k\pmod r.
$$
If $r$ is odd, this has exactly one solution. If $r$ is even, then $a$ is odd, so the right side is even while the left side is odd, and there is no solution. Hence every $s_k$ fixes one point above $0$ for odd $r$ and no point above $0$ for even $r$.

At infinity, first suppose $r$ is odd. Then the $r$ points above infinity are distinguished by $q=\zeta^j$. The fixed condition is
$$
2j\equiv(a-1)k\pmod r,
$$
which has exactly one solution because $2$ is invertible modulo $r$. Thus for odd $r$, every $s_k$ fixes one point above $0$ and one point above infinity, so the total contribution is $2r$.

Now suppose $r=2m$ is even and set $u=1/x$. Define regular local functions
$$
A=u y^m,
\qquad
B=u z^m.
$$
They satisfy
$$
A^2=1-u,
\qquad
B^2=1+u.
$$
At infinity, $A,B\in\{\pm1\}$ and
$$
q^m=\frac AB.
$$
For each of the four sign pairs there are exactly $m$ possible values of $q$, accounting for all $2r$ points above infinity.

Since $a$ is odd,
$$
s_k(A)=(-1)^{k+1}B,
\qquad
s_k(B)=(-1)^{k+1}A,
\qquad
s_k(q)=\mu_kq^{-1}.
$$
Writing $q=\zeta^j$, a fixed point must satisfy
$$
2j\equiv(a-1)k\pmod{2m},
\qquad
(-1)^j=(-1)^{k+1}.
$$
The first congruence has two solutions modulo $2m$, differing by $m$.

If $m$ is odd, those two solutions have opposite parity, so exactly one satisfies the parity condition. For that value of $q$, there are two choices for $A=\pm1$. Hence every $s_k$ fixes exactly two points above infinity, giving total contribution $2r$.

If $m$ is even, write $b=(a-1)/2$. The two solutions have the same parity, namely
$$
j\equiv bk\pmod2.
$$
The parity condition becomes
$$
bk\equiv k+1\pmod2.
$$
If $a\equiv1\pmod4$, then $b$ is even, so this holds exactly for odd $k$. There are $r/2$ such values of $k$, and for each there are two admissible values of $q$ and two choices of $A$, hence four fixed points. The total is again $2r$. If $a\equiv3\pmod4$, the parity condition has no solution, so the total is $0$.

These cases are encoded by
$$
e=\gcd(r,a+1,4).
$$
Indeed,
$$
2+h-e=
\begin{cases}
2,&r\text{ odd},\\
2,&r\equiv2\pmod4,\\
2,&4\mid r\text{ and }a\equiv1\pmod4,\\
0,&4\mid r\text{ and }a\equiv3\pmod4.
\end{cases}
$$
Therefore the total fixed-point contribution from the coset $\tau\langle\rho\rangle$ is
$$
S_{\rm coset}=r(2+h-e).
$$

Step 4: Apply Riemann-Hurwitz to the full quotient
Let
$$
Q=C/H.
$$
For a finite group action on a smooth curve,
$$
\sum_{P\in C}(|H_P|-1)
=\sum_{\sigma\in H\setminus\{1\}}\#\operatorname{Fix}(\sigma),
$$
because a point $P$ is counted once for every nonidentity element of its stabilizer. Hence Riemann-Hurwitz for the degree-$2r$ map $C\to Q$ gives
$$
2g(C)-2
=2r\bigl(2g(Q)-2\bigr)+S_{\rm rot}+S_{\rm coset}.
$$
Using
$$
2g(C)-2=r(2r-3-h),
$$
$$
S_{\rm rot}=r(c-1)+rh(d-1),
$$
and
$$
S_{\rm coset}=r(2+h-e),
$$
we obtain
$$
r(2r-3-h)
=2r\bigl(2g(Q)-2\bigr)+r(c+hd+1-e).
$$
Dividing by $r$ and solving for $g(Q)$ yields
$$
4g(Q)=2r-c-hd-h+e.
$$
Therefore
$$
g(Q)=\frac{2r-c-hd-h+e}{4}.
$$

Final Answer: $\boxed{\frac{2r-c-hd-h+e}{4}}$

## Answer
