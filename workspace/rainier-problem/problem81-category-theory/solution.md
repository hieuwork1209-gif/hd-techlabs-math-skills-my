## Steps

Step 1: Translate the categorical conditions into Heisenberg coordinates
Let
$$
\Gamma_g=\left\langle a_1,b_1,\ldots,a_g,b_g:\prod_{i=1}^g[a_i,b_i]=1\right\rangle
$$
and let $H_p$ be the Heisenberg group from the problem. Since both categories have one object, a functor $F:\mathcal S_g\to\mathcal H_p$ is exactly a homomorphism $\varphi:\Gamma_g\to H_p$, and two functors are naturally isomorphic exactly when their homomorphisms are conjugate in $H_p$.

Write an element of $H_p$ as $(v,z)$ with $v=(x,y)\in\mathbb F_p^2$ and $z\in\mathbb F_p$, with multiplication
$$
(v,z)(v',z')=(v+v',z+z'+xy').
$$
Then
$$
[(v,z),(v',z')]=(0,\det(v,v')).
$$
Write
$$
\varphi(a_i)=(v_i,\alpha_i),\qquad \varphi(b_i)=(w_i,\beta_i).
$$
The surface relation is therefore equivalent to
$$
\sum_{i=1}^g\det(v_i,w_i)=0.
$$
The central coordinates $\alpha_i,\beta_i$ do not occur in this relation.

The homomorphism is surjective exactly when the $2g$ vectors
$$
v_1,w_1,\ldots,v_g,w_g
$$
span $\mathbb F_p^2$. Indeed, surjectivity clearly implies spanning. Conversely, if the projected vectors span, two elements of the image have independent projections, so their commutator is a nonzero central element. Hence the image contains the whole center and surjects onto $H_p/Z(H_p)\cong\mathbb F_p^2$, so it is all of $H_p$.

Let $\mathcal T\subset\mathcal S_g$ be generated by $a_1,b_1$. Its restriction is full exactly when $v_1,w_1$ are independent. Thus the required local non-fullness is
$$
\det(v_1,w_1)=0.
$$

Step 2: Count the allowed first handle and the remaining surface relation
Let
$$
A=\#\{(v,w)\in(\mathbb F_p^2)^2:\det(v,w)=0\}.
$$
If $v=0$, there are $p^2$ choices for $w$. If $v\neq0$, there are $p^2-1$ choices for $v$ and then exactly $p$ choices for $w$ in the line spanned by $v$. Hence
$$
A=p^2+p(p^2-1)=p^3+p^2-p.
$$

For $h\geq1$, let $X_h$ be the number of $h$ ordered pairs $(v_i,w_i)$ with
$$
\sum_{i=1}^h\det(v_i,w_i)=0,
$$
and let $Y_h$ be the number with the sum equal to any fixed nonzero element of $\mathbb F_p$. For one pair, the zero determinant count is $A$, while for any fixed nonzero determinant the count is
$$
B=|SL_2(\mathbb F_p)|=p(p^2-1)=p^3-p.
$$
Appending one more pair gives
$$
X_{h+1}=AX_h+(p-1)BY_h,
$$
$$
Y_{h+1}=BX_h+\bigl(A+(p-2)B\bigr)Y_h.
$$
Therefore
$$
X_{h+1}-Y_{h+1}=(A-B)(X_h-Y_h)=p^2(X_h-Y_h).
$$
Since $X_1-Y_1=p^2$, we get
$$
X_h-Y_h=p^{2h}.
$$
Also
$$
X_h+(p-1)Y_h=p^{4h},
$$
so
$$
X_h=p^{4h-1}+(p-1)p^{2h-1}.
$$
Thus, after imposing $\det(v_1,w_1)=0$, the number of projected $2g$-tuples satisfying the surface relation is
$$
A\left(p^{4g-5}+(p-1)p^{2g-3}\right).
$$

Step 3: Remove the tuples that fail global fullness
A projected tuple fails to span $\mathbb F_p^2$ exactly when all $2g$ vectors lie in a common line, including the all-zero tuple. Every such tuple automatically satisfies both determinant conditions.

There are $p+1$ lines in $\mathbb F_p^2$. For a fixed line, there are $p^{2g}-1$ nonzero ordered $2g$-tuples lying in it, and every nonzero nonspanning tuple determines its line uniquely. Hence the number of nonspanning projected tuples is
$$
1+(p+1)(p^{2g}-1).
$$
Therefore the number of projected tuples satisfying the surface relation, the local non-fullness condition, and global fullness is
$$
P_{g,p}=(p^3+p^2-p)\left(p^{4g-5}+(p-1)p^{2g-3}\right)-1-(p+1)(p^{2g}-1).
$$

Step 4: Restore the central coordinates and quotient by natural isomorphism
For every projected tuple counted in Step 3, the $2g$ central coordinates
$$
\alpha_1,\beta_1,\ldots,\alpha_g,\beta_g
$$
are arbitrary, giving $p^{2g}$ functors.

Natural isomorphism is simultaneous conjugation by $H_p$. For a full functor, its image is all of $H_p$, so its stabilizer under conjugation is the center
$$
Z(H_p)=\{(0,z):z\in\mathbb F_p\},
$$
which has size $p$. Since $|H_p|=p^3$, every natural-isomorphism orbit of full functors has size
$$
\frac{p^3}{p}=p^2.
$$
Thus the number of natural-isomorphism classes is
$$
N_{g,p}=p^{2g-2}P_{g,p}.
$$

Step 5: State the exact count
Substituting the value of $P_{g,p}$ from Step 3 gives
$$
N_{g,p}=p^{2g-2}\left((p^3+p^2-p)(p^{4g-5}+(p-1)p^{2g-3})-1-(p+1)(p^{2g}-1)\right).
$$

Final Answer: $\boxed{p^{2g-2}\left((p^3+p^2-p)(p^{4g-5}+(p-1)p^{2g-3})-1-(p+1)(p^{2g}-1)\right)}$
