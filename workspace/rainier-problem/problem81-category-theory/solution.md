## Steps

Step 1: Classify one invertible Beck distributive law
Let
$$
D=k[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2),
$$
and let $T_i=D\otimes_k-$ be three labeled copies of the corresponding monad on $\operatorname{Vect}_k$. A natural transformation
$$
\lambda_{ji}:T_jT_i\Rightarrow T_iT_j
$$
is determined by its value on $k$, hence by a $k$-linear map
$$
D_j\otimes D_i\longrightarrow D_i\otimes D_j.
$$
Write $x=\varepsilon_i$ and $y=\varepsilon_j$. The two unit axioms force
$$
\lambda(1\otimes1)=1\otimes1,
\qquad
\lambda(1\otimes x)=x\otimes1,
\qquad
\lambda(y\otimes1)=1\otimes y.
$$
Thus write
$$
\lambda(y\otimes x)=a+b x+c y+dxy.
$$
Apply the two multiplication axioms to $y\otimes x\otimes x$ and to $y\otimes y\otimes x$. Since $x^2=y^2=0$, the first gives
$$
c=0,
\qquad
a(1+d)=0,
$$
while the second gives
$$
b=0,
\qquad
a(1+d)=0.
$$
Hence every Beck distributive law has the form
$$
\lambda(y\otimes x)=a+dxy,
\qquad
a(1+d)=0.
$$
Its matrix in the basis $1,x,y,xy$ has determinant $d$, so it is invertible exactly when
$$
d\ne0.
$$
The associated twisted tensor product algebra has generators $x,y$ and relations
$$
x^2=y^2=0,
\qquad
yx=a+dxy.
$$

Step 2: Detect exactly when a pairwise composite monad is simple
If $a=0$, then the span of $x$ and $xy$ is a nonzero proper two-sided ideal, so the twisted algebra is not simple.

If $a\ne0$, then the Beck equation $a(1+d)=0$ forces
$$
d=-1.
$$
The algebra then satisfies
$$
yx+xy=a.
$$
Sending
$$
x\longmapsto E_{12},
\qquad
y\longmapsto aE_{21}
$$
gives a surjective homomorphism onto $M_2(k)$. Both algebras have dimension $4$, hence this is an isomorphism. Therefore a pairwise composite monad is simple exactly when its parameter $a_{ji}$ is nonzero.

Step 3: Impose the Yang-Baxter coherence
For the three laws write their parameters as
$$
(a_{21},d_{21}),
\qquad
(a_{31},d_{31}),
\qquad
(a_{32},d_{32}).
$$
Evaluating the Yang-Baxter equation on
$$
\varepsilon_3\otimes\varepsilon_2\otimes\varepsilon_1
$$
and reducing to the ordered basis gives exactly
$$
a_{32}(1-d_{21}d_{31})=0,
$$
$$
a_{31}(d_{32}-d_{21})=0,
$$
$$
a_{21}(d_{32}d_{31}-1)=0.
$$
To make all three pairwise composite monads simple, Step 2 requires
$$
a_{21},a_{31},a_{32}\ne0.
$$
Then each Beck law has
$$
d_{21}=d_{31}=d_{32}=-1,
$$
and the three Yang-Baxter equations are automatic. Thus the first extremal value is
$$
S_p=3,
$$
and its maximizers are indexed freely by
$$
(a_{21},a_{31},a_{32})\in(k^\times)^3.
$$

Step 4: Identify the total composite algebra
For a first-stage maximizer, the total composite monad is tensoring by the $8$-dimensional algebra $A$ generated by $x_1,x_2,x_3$ with
$$
x_i^2=0,
$$
and
$$
x_jx_i+x_ix_j=a_{ji}
\qquad(i<j).
$$
Let $W$ be the span of $x_1,x_2,x_3$. Since $p$ is odd, these relations are the Clifford relations for the symmetric bilinear form whose Gram matrix is
$$
B=\frac12
\begin{pmatrix}
0&a_{21}&a_{31}\\
a_{21}&0&a_{32}\\
a_{31}&a_{32}&0
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Its determinant is
$$
\det B=\frac{a_{21}a_{31}a_{32}}4\ne0.
$$
Hence $B$ is nondegenerate. Diagonalize it over $k$, and rescale one orthogonal basis vector so that the change-of-basis matrix has determinant $1$. Thus there is an orthogonal basis $u,v,w$ of $W$ with
$$
u^2=\alpha,
\qquad
v^2=\beta,
\qquad
w^2=\gamma,
$$
where
$$
\alpha\beta\gamma=\det B,
$$
and
$$
uv=-vu,
\qquad
uw=-wu,
\qquad
vw=-wv.
$$
The PBW basis is
$$
1,u,v,w,uv,uw,vw,uvw.
$$
An element commuting with $u,v,w$ has zero coefficients on the six middle basis vectors, so
$$
Z(A)=k\oplus k\omega,
\qquad
\omega=uvw.
$$
Moreover
$$
\omega^2=-\alpha\beta\gamma
=-\det B
=-\frac{a_{21}a_{31}a_{32}}4.
$$

Step 5: Minimize the number of primitive central idempotents
Put
$$
c=-\frac{a_{21}a_{31}a_{32}}4\in k^\times.
$$
Then
$$
Z(A)\cong k[z]/(z^2-c).
$$
If $c$ is a nonsquare, this is the quadratic field extension of $k$, so $A$ has exactly one primitive central idempotent, namely $1$. If $c=s^2$ is a square, then
$$
k[z]/(z^2-c)\cong k\times k,
$$
and the two primitive central idempotents are
$$
\frac12\left(1\pm\frac{\omega}{s}\right).
$$
Therefore the smallest possible value is
$$
E_p=1.
$$

For each fixed pair $(a_{21},a_{31})\in(k^\times)^2$, multiplication by the fixed nonzero scalar
$$
-\frac{a_{21}a_{31}}4
$$
is a bijection of $k^\times$, and exactly $(p-1)/2$ choices of $a_{32}$ make $c$ a nonsquare. Hence
$$
K_p=(p-1)^2\frac{p-1}{2}
=\frac{(p-1)^3}{2}.
$$

Final Answer: $\boxed{\left(3,1,\frac{(p-1)^3}{2}\right)}$
