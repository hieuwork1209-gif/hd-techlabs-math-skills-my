## Steps

Step 1: Lift the iteration to the multiplicative coordinate

Fix an odd prime $\ell$ and an integer $n\ge1$, and put
$$
m=2^n.
$$
For $z\ne0$, define
$$
\pi(z)=z+z^{-1}.
$$
If $f(x)=x^2-2$, then
$$
f(\pi(z))
=(z+z^{-1})^2-2
=z^2+z^{-2}
=\pi(z^2).
$$
Induction therefore gives
$$
f^{\circ n}(\pi(z))=\pi(z^m).
\tag{1}
$$

Let
$$
\beta=\ell^{1/m}>0,
\qquad
\zeta=e^{2\pi i/m}.
$$
The equation in (1)
$$
f^{\circ n}(\pi(z))=\frac{\ell^2+1}{\ell}
=\ell+\ell^{-1}
$$
is equivalent, with $y=z^m$, to
$$
y+y^{-1}=\ell+\ell^{-1}.
$$
After multiplying by $y$,
$$
y^2-(\ell+\ell^{-1})y+1
=(y-\ell)(y-\ell^{-1})=0.
$$
Thus every root of the original degree-$m$ polynomial is
$$
x_k=\beta\zeta^k+\beta^{-1}\zeta^{-k},
\qquad
k\in\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z.
\tag{2}
$$
These $m$ numbers are distinct. Indeed, if $x_j=x_k$ and $j\ne k$, then from (2)
$$
\beta(\zeta^j-\zeta^k)
=-\beta^{-1}(\zeta^{-j}-\zeta^{-k})
=\beta^{-1}\frac{\zeta^j-\zeta^k}{\zeta^{j+k}},
$$
so
$$
\beta^2=\zeta^{-(j+k)},
$$
which is impossible because $\beta^2>1$ while the right side has complex absolute value $1$. Hence (2) is the complete root set.

Step 2: Show that the splitting field is the full radical-cyclotomic compositum

Let
$$
M=\mathbb Q(\beta,\zeta).
$$
Equation (2) shows that the splitting field $K_n$ of the given iterate satisfies
$$
K_n\subseteq M.
$$
Also, $M$ is the splitting field of $X^m-\ell$, hence $M/\mathbb Q$ is Galois.

Take $\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(M/\mathbb Q)$. Since $\beta$ is a root of $X^m-\ell$ and $\zeta$ is a primitive $m$th root of unity, there are
$$
a\in\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z,
\qquad
b\in(\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z)^\times
$$
such that
$$
\sigma(\beta)=\beta\zeta^a,
\qquad
\sigma(\zeta)=\zeta^b.
$$
Therefore
$$
\sigma(x_k)=x_{a+bk}.
\tag{3}
$$
If $\sigma$ fixes $K_n$ pointwise, then it fixes every $x_k$. By distinctness of the roots, (3) gives
$$
a+bk\equiv k\pmod m
$$
for every $k$. Taking $k=0$ gives $a=0$, and then $k=1$ gives $b=1$. Thus $\sigma$ is the identity.

Hence
$$
\operatorname{Gal}(M/K_n)=1,
$$
so
$$
K_n=M.
\tag{4}
$$

Step 3: Prove that the radical and cyclotomic fields intersect trivially

Set
$$
E=\mathbb Q(\beta),
\qquad
C=\mathbb Q(\zeta).
$$
The polynomial $X^m-\ell$ is Eisenstein at the prime $\ell$, so
$$
[E:\mathbb Q]=m.
\tag{5}
$$
Moreover $\beta$ is an algebraic integer and
$$
|N_{E/\mathbb Q}(\beta)|=\ell.
$$
Thus the principal ideal
$$
\mathfrak p=(\beta)\subset\mathcal O_E
$$
has norm $\ell$, hence is prime. Since $\ell=\beta^m$,
$$
(\ell)=\mathfrak p^m.
\tag{6}
$$
Therefore $\ell$ is totally ramified in $E/\mathbb Q$.

On the other hand, $\ell$ is unramified in $C$. For $n=1$, $C=\mathbb Q$, so this is immediate. For $n\ge2$, put $d=m/2$. Then
$$
\Phi_m(X)=X^d+1
$$
and $C=\mathbb Q(\zeta)$ is generated by a root of this polynomial. Its polynomial discriminant is
$$
\operatorname{disc}(X^d+1)=\pm d^d,
$$
which is a power of $2$. Since for an integral primitive element the field discriminant divides the polynomial discriminant up to the square of the index,
$$
\operatorname{disc}(\Phi_m)
=[\mathcal O_C:\mathbb Z[\zeta]]^2\operatorname{disc}(C),
$$
no odd prime can ramify in $C$. In particular, $\ell$ is unramified in $C$.

Let
$$
F=E\cap C.
$$
If $F\ne\mathbb Q$, then total ramification in (6) descends to the intermediate field $F$: the unique prime of $E$ above $\ell$ contracts to the unique prime of $F$ above $\ell$, with residue degree $1$, so the fundamental identity forces ramification index $[F:\mathbb Q]>1$. Thus $\ell$ would ramify in $F$.

But $F\subseteq C$, and every subextension of the unramified-at-$\ell$ extension $C/\mathbb Q$ is also unramified at $\ell$. This contradiction gives
$$
E\cap C=\mathbb Q.
\tag{7}
$$

Step 4: Determine the Galois group of the compositum

By (5), (7), and the fact that $C/\mathbb Q$ is Galois,
$$
[M:\mathbb Q]
=[E:\mathbb Q][C:\mathbb Q]
=m\varphi(m).
$$
Since $m=2^n$,
$$
\varphi(m)=2^{n-1}.
$$

The subgroup fixing $C$ consists of
$$
\tau_a:\quad
\beta\mapsto\beta\zeta^a,
\qquad
\zeta\mapsto\zeta,
\qquad
a\in\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z,
$$
so it is cyclic of order $m$.

Because $E\cap C=\mathbb Q$, each cyclotomic automorphism
$$
\sigma_b:\quad
\zeta\mapsto\zeta^b,
\qquad
b\in(\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z)^\times
$$
extends to $M$ while fixing $\beta$. These extensions form a subgroup isomorphic to $(\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z)^\times$. Their conjugation action on the cyclic radical subgroup is
$$
\sigma_b\tau_a\sigma_b^{-1}=\tau_{ba}.
$$
Therefore
$$
\operatorname{Gal}(M/\mathbb Q)
\cong
C_m\rtimes(\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z)^\times,
$$
where the unit group acts on $C_m\cong\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z$ by multiplication.

Finally, (4) and $m=2^n$ give
$$
\operatorname{Gal}(K_n/\mathbb Q)
\cong
C_{2^n}\rtimes(\mathbb Z/2^n\mathbb Z)^\times.
$$

## Solution Concepts

- Semiconjugacy of $x^2-2$ to the squaring map through $z+z^{-1}$.
- Ramification separation between a pure radical extension and a power-of-two cyclotomic extension.
- Faithful affine action on the iterate roots and the resulting semidirect-product Galois group.

Final Answer: $\displaystyle C_{2^n}\rtimes(\mathbb Z/2^n\mathbb Z)^\times$.
