## Steps

Step 1: Determine the Galois group of the quartic splitting field
Let
$$
f(x)=x^4-x-1
$$
and let $K_4$ be its splitting field over $\mathbb Q$. For a monic integral polynomial whose reduction modulo a prime $p$ is squarefree, if the irreducible factor degrees modulo $p$ are $d_1,\ldots,d_t$, then the Galois group acting on the roots contains an element whose disjoint cycle lengths are $d_1,\ldots,d_t$.

The discriminant identity for $x^4+qx+r$ is
$$
\operatorname{disc}(x^4+qx+r)=256r^3-27q^4.
$$
Thus
$$
\operatorname{disc}(f)=-256-27=-283,
$$
so neither $2$ nor $7$ divides the discriminant.

Modulo $2$,
$$
f(x)\equiv x^4+x+1.
$$
This polynomial has no root in $\mathbb F_2$, and the only irreducible quadratic over $\mathbb F_2$ is $x^2+x+1$. Moreover,
$$
x^4+x+1=(x^2+x+1)(x^2+x)+1,
$$
so the reduction is irreducible. Hence $f$ is irreducible over $\mathbb Q$, and $\operatorname{Gal}(K_4/\mathbb Q)$ contains a $4$-cycle.

Modulo $7$,
$$
f(x)=(x-3)(x^3+3x^2+2x+5).
$$
The cubic takes the nonzero values $5,4,1,2,6,5,5$ at $0,1,\ldots,6$, so it is irreducible over $\mathbb F_7$. Hence the Galois group also contains a $3$-cycle.

Therefore its order is divisible by both $4$ and $3$, hence by $12$. It is a subgroup of $S_4$. If it had order $12$, it would be an index-$2$ subgroup of $S_4$, hence $A_4$, but $A_4$ contains no $4$-cycle. Thus
$$
\operatorname{Gal}(K_4/\mathbb Q)\cong S_4.
$$

Step 2: Determine the compositum Galois group
Let
$$
g(x)=x^3-2
$$
and let $K_3$ be its splitting field. For Eisenstein's criterion at $2$, the nonleading coefficients are divisible by $2$, while the constant term $-2$ is not divisible by $4$, so $g$ is irreducible. For a cubic $x^3+ax+b$, the discriminant is $-4a^3-27b^2$, hence
$$
\operatorname{disc}(g)=-108.
$$
An irreducible cubic has Galois group $A_3$ exactly when its discriminant is a square in $\mathbb Q$; otherwise its Galois group is $S_3$. Therefore
$$
\operatorname{Gal}(K_3/\mathbb Q)\cong S_3.
$$

Set $M=K_4\cap K_3$. Because both splitting fields are Galois over $\mathbb Q$, the extension $M/\mathbb Q$ is Galois and $\operatorname{Gal}(M/\mathbb Q)$ is a quotient of both $S_4$ and $S_3$.

For completeness, the conjugacy classes of $S_4$ have sizes
$$
1,\ 6,\ 3,\ 8,\ 6
$$
for the identity, transpositions, double transpositions, $3$-cycles, and $4$-cycles. A normal subgroup is a union of conjugacy classes containing the identity, and its order must divide $24$. The only possibilities are therefore
$$
1,\quad V_4,\quad A_4,\quad S_4.
$$
Likewise, the conjugacy classes of $S_3$ have sizes $1,3,2$, so its normal subgroups are
$$
1,\quad A_3,\quad S_3.
$$
Thus the only possible nontrivial common quotient types are $C_2$ and $S_3$.

For a separable polynomial with roots $\alpha_i$, the square root of its discriminant
$$
\prod_{i<j}(\alpha_i-\alpha_j)
$$
changes by the sign of a permutation of the roots. Hence the quadratic subfield fixed by the alternating group is generated by the square root of the discriminant. Consequently the unique quadratic subfields are
$$
\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-283})\subset K_4
$$
and
$$
\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})\subset K_3,
$$
which are distinct. Therefore $M$ cannot have Galois group $C_2$. It also cannot have Galois group $S_3$, because then $M=K_3$ and the unique quadratic subfield of $K_3$ would lie in $K_4$. Hence
$$
K_4\cap K_3=\mathbb Q.
$$
If $L=K_4K_3$, restriction therefore gives
$$
\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb Q)\cong S_4\times S_3.
$$

Step 3: Reduce the field count to quotient data using Goursat's lemma
By the Galois correspondence, intermediate fields of $L/\mathbb Q$ are in bijection with subgroups of $S_4\times S_3$.

Use Goursat's lemma in the following exact counting form. Subgroups of a direct product $G\times H$ are in bijection with data
$$
A\leq G,\qquad B\leq H,\qquad N\trianglelefteq A,\qquad M\trianglelefteq B,
$$
together with an isomorphism
$$
A/N\cong B/M.
$$
For an isomorphism type $Q$, let $a_Q$ be the number of pairs $(A,N)$ for $S_4$ with $A/N\cong Q$, and let $b_Q$ be the analogous number for $S_3$. For each fixed pair of quotient copies isomorphic to $Q$, the number of isomorphisms between them is $|\operatorname{Aut}(Q)|$, so the contribution of $Q$ is
$$
a_Qb_Q|\operatorname{Aut}(Q)|.
$$

The six subgroups of $S_3$ are one trivial subgroup, three subgroups $C_2$, one subgroup $C_3$, and $S_3$ itself. Therefore
$$
b_1=6,
$$
$$
b_{C_2}=3+1=4,
$$
where the extra contribution is $S_3/A_3$,
$$
b_{C_3}=1,
$$
and
$$
b_{S_3}=1.
$$
These are all quotient types from subgroups of $S_3$ that can occur nontrivially in the Goursat count.

Step 4: Count the corresponding quotient data inside $S_4$
The subgroups of $S_4$ have the following isomorphism types and multiplicities:
$$
\begin{array}{c|ccccccccc}
\text{type}&1&C_2&C_3&C_4&V_4&S_3&D_8&A_4&S_4\\
\hline
\text{count}&1&9&4&3&4&4&3&1&1.
\end{array}
$$
The counts are obtained as follows. The $9$ subgroups $C_2$ come from the $6$ transpositions and $3$ double transpositions. The $4$ subgroups $C_3$ come from the $8$ three-cycles, two generators per subgroup. The $3$ subgroups $C_4$ come from the $6$ four-cycles, two generators per subgroup. There is one normal Klein four subgroup of double transpositions and three further Klein four subgroups generated by pairs of disjoint transpositions. Every subgroup of order $6$ is isomorphic to $S_3$ because $S_4$ has no element of order $6$; its faithful action on four points cannot have orbit pattern $2+2$, so it has orbit pattern $3+1$ and is a point stabilizer, giving $4$ such subgroups. The subgroups of order $8$ are Sylow $2$-subgroups, each isomorphic to $D_8$; their number divides $3$ and is odd, and it is not $1$ because $S_4$ has no normal subgroup of order $8$, so there are $3$. The unique subgroup of order $12$ is the index-$2$ subgroup $A_4$.

By Lagrange's theorem, subgroup orders divide $24$. Orders $1,2,3,4,6,8,12,24$ are exactly the orders covered above, and groups of order $4$ are either $C_4$ or $V_4$. Hence the table is exhaustive and gives $30$ subgroups in total. Therefore
$$
a_1=30.
$$

For quotient $C_2$, count index-$2$ normal subgroups inside each subgroup type. Each $C_2$ contributes $1$, each $C_4$ contributes $1$, each $V_4$ contributes $3$, each $S_3$ contributes $1$, each $D_8$ contributes $3$, and $S_4$ contributes its subgroup $A_4$; the remaining types contribute none. Thus
$$
a_{C_2}=9+3+4\cdot3+4+3\cdot3+1=38.
$$

For quotient $C_3$, the four subgroups $C_3$ contribute their trivial kernels, and $A_4/V_4\cong C_3$ contributes once. Hence
$$
a_{C_3}=5.
$$
For quotient $S_3$, the four point-stabilizer copies of $S_3$ contribute their trivial kernels, and $S_4/V_4\cong S_3$ contributes once. Hence
$$
a_{S_3}=5.
$$

Step 5: Sum all Goursat contributions
The relevant automorphism groups have orders
$$
|\operatorname{Aut}(1)|=1,\qquad
|\operatorname{Aut}(C_2)|=1,\qquad
|\operatorname{Aut}(C_3)|=2,\qquad
|\operatorname{Aut}(S_3)|=6.
$$
Therefore the total number of subgroups of $S_4\times S_3$ is
$$
30\cdot6+38\cdot4+5\cdot1\cdot2+5\cdot1\cdot6
=180+152+10+30
=372.
$$
The Galois correspondence then gives exactly $372$ intermediate fields between $\mathbb Q$ and $L$.

Final Answer: $\boxed{372}$
