Let us regard the interval [0,1] as a circle of circumference 1, because the condition f(0)=f(1) allows us to glue the endpoints. Then f is a 1-Lipschitz function on this circle. The condition integral_0^1 f(x) dx=0 says that its average value on the circle is zero.

We will prove the sharp inequality integral_0^1 f(x)^2 dx <= 1/48, and then determine when equality holds.

1. A sharp variance lemma on the circle

Let d(x,y)=min(|x-y|,1-|x-y|) denote geodesic distance. A 1-Lipschitz function satisfies |f(x)-f(y)| <= d(x,y). We use the variance identity, valid because integral f=0: integral f(x)^2 dx = (1/2) integral integral (f(x)-f(y))^2 dx dy.

The sharp form of the circle Lipschitz variance inequality is: if g is 1-Lipschitz on the unit circle and has mean zero, then integral g^2 <= 1/48. Equality occurs exactly for triangular waves g(x)=+/- (1/4-d(x,a)).

Let M=max g and m=min g. Since g is 1-Lipschitz on a circle of circumference 1, going from a maximum point to a minimum point requires distance at least M-m. Hence M-m <= 1/2. Because the mean is zero, M>=0>=m. Write A=M and B=-m. Then A+B=M-m<=1/2.

For any x, |g(x)-M| <= d(x,x_M), so g(x)>=M-d(x,x_M). Similarly g(x)<=m+d(x,x_m). The largest possible oscillation with given extrema is obtained by making both inequalities equalities, i.e. by a linear decrease of slope 1 from the maximum to the minimum. Thus the extremizer must have constant slopes +/-1, and the only possible zero-mean shape is the symmetric triangular wave. Its height is forced by M=-m=1/4.
